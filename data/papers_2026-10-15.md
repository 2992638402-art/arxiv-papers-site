## 1. E2DRL: End-to-End Differentiable Reinforcement Learning for 3D Obstacle Avoidance in Quadruped Robots

**arXiv ID:** [2610.19627v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19627v1)

**作者:** Cheng Fan, Bingyang Zhou, Gang Yang, Zixuan Zhuang, Aoqian Zhang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Differentiable reinforcement learning shows strong promise for robot policy learning, but its application to legged locomotion has remained limited to blind walking or simulated 2D tasks. Extending it to end-to-end visual obstacle avoidance is challenging because hard collision constraints and early termination upon contact corrupt first-order gradients, trapping optimizers in local minima. We present E2DRL, which addresses this with a soft differentiable obstacle-interaction formulation: penetration penalties across multiple body keypoints with velocity-conditioned stop-gradient shaping to provide the first-order training signals for 3D obstacle avoidance. To realize a deployable perception-control system, a privileged teacher trained via Soft Analytic Policy Optimization (SAPO) in a differentiable simulator is distilled into a recurrent depth-based student via DAgger, then blended with a base locomotion policy using a terrain-aware gating module for deployment. In simulation, E2DRL achieves higher sample efficiency than PPO and demonstrates superior velocity-tracking performance and 3D obstacle-avoidance capability compared with both PPO and Duality-based DCBF NMPC. We present the first sim-to-real validation of end-to-end differentiable RL for 3D obstacle avoidance on a Unitree Go2 in static and dynamic environments.

## 2. Eye, Robot: Learning Expressive Gaze Dynamics via a VLM-Guided Transformer-VAE

**arXiv ID:** [2610.19635v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19635v1)

**作者:** Yuhang Hu, Junlin Lyu, Yuan Fang, Xiaoying Shen, Lechen Zhang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Gaze coordinates attention in human interaction through coupled eye, head, and eyelid motion. A humanoid robot must choose where to look while generating a suitable movement toward that target; fixed tracking rules alone do not model the temporal structure of human gaze. We separate these tasks with an asynchronous vision-language model that selects a scene referent, a reactive perception module that maintains fixation, and a Realistic Gaze Transformer (RGT) that generates target-conditioned motion. RGT is a Transformer-VAE trained on markerless human recordings retargeted to the robot's 11-DoF joint space: self-supervised pretraining encodes recent full-head history, and autoregressive fine-tuning exposes the model to its own predictions during rollout. At inference, a four-dimensional binocular-eye trajectory conditions coordinated neck and eye motion; an auxiliary blink predictor triggers eyelid templates during playback. On a two-second benchmark in which all controllers receive the same ground-truth target, RGT-Base obtains the lowest reported overall seven-joint MSE, and physical demonstrations show the integrated targeting and control pipeline. The evidence establishes robot-space kinematic fidelity and real-time RGT inference; perceived naturalness and open-vocabulary target-selection accuracy remain questions for controlled evaluation.

## 3. Memory-Efficient Subspace Optimization with Residual Correction: From Momentum SGD to Muon

**arXiv ID:** [2610.19844v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19844v1)

**作者:** Sifan Yang, Hongyi Tao, Dingzhi Yu, Haomin Bai, Wei Jiang et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Memory-efficient subspace optimization has emerged as a practical approach to reducing the memory cost of LLM training while preserving optimization performance. Previous algorithms typically rely on the leading singular subspace of stochastic gradients, yet the gradient noise may dominate the subspace in later training stages and can prevent convergence. To overcome this limitation, we adopt a residual correction framework, which maintains the optimizer states in the subspace and adds the orthogonal residual gradient to the parameter update. We first integrate this scheme into momentum SGD to develop SMRC, which attains an $O(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate for a fixed orthonormal basis, matching that of full-space momentum SGD. Our analysis introduces a subspace virtual iterate to resolve the coupling between the momentum and the residual correction. Furthermore, we extend SMRC to a multi-stage variant which periodically recomputes bases to track the evolving dominant subspace and retains the same rate. Since each basis refresh breaks the consistency of the virtual iterates, we devise a boundary correction approach that aligns them across stages. Beyond momentum SGD, we incorporate our framework into Muon and propose PMRC, which applies the matrix sign to the projected momentum. Through a weighted momentum tracking analysis that accounts for the residual gradient, we establish an $O(T^{-1/4})$ convergence rate for PMRC, which matches the rate of full-space Muon. We also design a multi-stage PMRC with periodically refreshed bases, which maintains a convergence rate of the same order. Extensive experimental results confirm the effectiveness and efficiency of our algorithms.

## 4. Any Time, Any Space: A World-State-Centric Framework for Agentic Video Generation

**arXiv ID:** [2610.20571v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20571v1)

**作者:** Weijie Wang, Haomin Zhang, Ye Li, Akide Liu, Zeyu Zhang et al.

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Long-form agentic video generation must turn a screenplay into shots that remain spatially and temporally consistent across nonconsecutive requests. Passing prompts or recent frames between shots does not provide a shared spatial frame for revisiting a scene or a time-indexed record for replaying an earlier event. We present Any Time, Any Space (ATAS), which compiles a screenplay into shot requests with explicit location, camera, and event-time references. Before generation, it retrieves relevant evidence from a scene memory of registered static geometry and an event memory of dynamic observations indexed by scene, event, and time. After generation, ATAS reconstructs and registers the clip. It updates memory only when review and state checks accept the observation, while film delivery remains independent of memory admission. Across four video generators, ATAS improves all reported cross-shot consistency measures and achieves the highest aggregate benchmark score for each backbone. It also outperforms the compared agents and 3D-guided methods on target-image spatial revisit, leads three event-recapture criteria, and receives the highest human ratings on all six criteria. The video results and code are available on our project page: https://lhmd.top/atas/.

## 5. Time Window Optimization for Motor Imagery Classification via Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2610.16921v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16921v1)

**作者:** Huan Luo, Na Lu, Xiaopeng Wang, Ziyang Shang

**分类:** cs.NE, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Online motor imagery-based brain-computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) typically decode continuous EEG using fixed-length sliding windows that may be misaligned with neural events. Existing window optimization methods often evaluate windows independently, overlooking temporal dependencies and potentially producing fragmented or physiologically implausible selections. We propose Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning (TSE-RL), a framework that incorporates prior knowledge of MI event structure into a constrained Markov decision process for adaptive window optimization. First, TSE-RL assigns each window an explicit semantic role through Discard, Onset, Sustain, or Offset actions, extending binary retain/discard decisions to event-level alignment. Second, a finite-state machine masks invalid actions to enforce the temporal order of MI phases and promote physiologically plausible window sequences. Third, a Transformer-based history encoder captures dependencies across windows, improving training stability and computational efficiency compared with recurrent architectures. TSE-RL is plug-and-play and can be integrated with diverse MI classifiers. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate consistent improvements in decoding performance in both cross-session and cross-subject settings. Visualization analyses further suggest that the learned window selection policy aligns with neurophysiological principles.

## 7. GeoThinker-$F$: Endogenous Predictive World Representations for Fast Spatial Intelligence

**arXiv ID:** [2610.20592v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20592v1)

**作者:** Haoyuan Li, Qihang Cao, Huanyuan Zhou, Xin Hu, Sijia Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Existing geometry-aware Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) increasingly rely on external 3D encoders to provide structural priors, but executing these additional encoder paths at inference introduces substantial latency and computational overhead. We propose GeoThinker-$F$, an efficient framework that internalizes spatiotemporal perception by transforming causal MLLM hidden states into predictive latent world representations. Our key insight is that intermediate semantic representations contain sufficient information to predict useful representations of current geometry and future semantics, without requiring an exact reproduction of features from a dedicated 3D encoder. To realize this idea, GeoThinker-$F$ adopts a decoupled two-stage training strategy. In the first stage, the MLLM backbone is frozen while lightweight Latent Frame Prediction (LFP) heads learn from an offline 3D geometry expert and next-frame semantic targets. In the second stage, the external expert is removed, and the model is jointly optimized to generate and selectively integrate its self-predicted priors through a specialized Spatial-Grounded Temporal (SGT) Fusion module. Extensive evaluations show that GeoThinker-$F$ eliminates runtime dependence on external 3D geometry encoders while maintaining competitive performance across diverse spatial intelligence benchmarks. It achieves up to a $3.6\times$ inference speedup, reducing latency from $1.45\,\mathrm{s}$ to $0.40\,\mathrm{s}$ while retaining strong spatial reasoning performance.

## 8. What Matters for World Modeling in Vision-Language-Action Models?

**作者:** Qisen Ma, Yixiang Chen, Yuan Xu, Peiyan Li, Jiabing Yang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Integrating world models into Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) has emerged as a promising direction, enabling policies to anticipate future dynamics beyond reactive observation-to-action mapping. However, existing approaches vary simultaneously in their world representations, integration paradigms, and policy backbones, making it difficult to determine what truly matters for effective world model integration. To disentangle these factors, we categorize existing efforts into four representative paradigms, namely Implicit Future Alignment, Explicit Future Query, MoT Future Expert, and External World Predictor. ## 13. CLASHarness: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Conflict in Computer Use via a Controllable and Verifiable Generation Pipeline

**arXiv ID:** [2610.19128v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19128v1)

**作者:** Yuxiang Lai, Jike Zhong, Chengzhi Liu, Ming Li, Haonan Wang et al.

**分类:** cs.MA

**摘要:**

Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems to automate complex computer-use workflows, yet existing benchmarks mostly assume consistent, cooperative environments. In contrast, real-world collaboration rarely runs on consistent information: peer or sub-agents frequently report outdated states, issue contradictory instructions, or compete over shared permissions and files, forcing agents to check others' outputs and verify ground truth in the environment. Evaluating such conflict resolution remains challenging: manual authoring cannot scale across complex systems, while unconstrained LLM generation produces narrative drift, answer leakage, and unsatisfiable rubrics. We introduce CLASHarness (Conflict-Loaded Agent Scenario Harness), a controllable framework for generating verifiable multi-agent conflicts in computer use, with a benchmark of 600 multi-round tasks. By combining hierarchical scenario planning with an eight-step verified micro-pipeline, CLASHarness pre-commits decisive visual facts before rendering and compiles hybrid code and semantic graders directly from executed reference trajectories, guaranteeing every task is solvable by construction. Evaluating 15 frontier models on CLASHarness reveals three core bottlenecks. First, when sub-agent outputs conflict with the environment, weaker models blindly follow misleading information, whereas stronger models inspect visual evidence to verify ground truth. Second, resolving conflicts is much harder than finding them: while agents can spot inconsistencies, actively fixing system state drops task success significantly. Third, coordination protocols remain a universal bottleneck: even frontier models fail over 58% of closing actions, often forgetting to release shared locks or hand off tasks. CLASHarness provides a scalable, verifiable foundation for building reliable multi-agent systems.

## 18. Interlock: From Local Correctness to Task Completion in Long-Horizon Agents

**摘要:**
