## 1. From GAN-Based Models to GPT-5: A Taxonomy-Guided Study of Human Performance in Detecting Deepfake Face Images

**arXiv ID:** [2610.23144v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23144v1)

**作者:** Zhiqing Guo, Lixin Jia, Luning Sun, Xiaowei Chen, Guofeng Yu et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.CR, cs.HC

**摘要:**

Generative models for deepfakes have rapidly evolved from GAN-based architectures to diffusion models and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, empirical research on human performance in detecting deepfake face images has not kept pace. It remains unclear if (and how) detection performance varies across generative models and whether model-specific training improves detection more effectively than general-purpose training. In this paper, we propose the \emph{Deepfake Face Artifact Taxonomy} (DFAT), a cross-model taxonomy of human-recognizable visual artifacts in deepfake face images, based on which we build both general-purpose and model-specific training materials. Through an online experiment ($n = 524$), we examined human performance in detecting deepfake face images and evaluated training efficacy across five representative generative models: three GAN-based, one diffusion-based, and one MLLM (GPT-5). Our results show that participants' detection accuracy was strongly model-dependent and that images generated by more recent and advanced models were not necessarily harder to detect. After training, participants achieved significant performance gains across most conditions, and their improvement varied by model. Notably, model-specific training generally yielded greater numerical gains than general-purpose training. In a five-month follow-up test ($n = 342$), we observed no significant decline in detection accuracy and no significant increase in perceived stress. Overall, our findings provide actionable insights for designing effective, sustainable, and targeted training programs to counter the growing threat posed by deepfakes.

## 2. Beyond Reward Scoring: Online Multi-Agent Preference Learning for Multimodal Response Generation

**arXiv ID:** [2610.23256v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23256v1)

**作者:** Ziyun Zou, Jiwoo Hong, Yifan Lu, Shao Tang, Weiyi Wu et al.

**分类:** cs.AI

**摘要:**

Multimodal preference alignment often follows a score-based RLHF pipeline: a reward model is first trained to learn human preferences, then used to score on-policy responses and optimize the response model's generation. This turns preference supervision into a scalar signal for generation: a model may learn to produce answers with higher preference scores, but is rarely trained explicitly to learn the reasons underlying human preferences. We therefore ask whether making preference understanding an internal capability of the response model can improve its generation. To investigate this question, we propose \textbf{PrefMind}, an online dual-agent reinforcement learning framework with two trainable agents: a response Generator and a preference Critic. Given a pair of candidate responses, the agents analyze and discuss which response better aligns with human preference. Human labels supervise their judgments and corrections. For pairs on which both the Generator and revised Critic correctly identify the human-preferred response, the Generator produces an improved answer, and the Critic compares it against the original preferred response to provide a generation reward. Both agents are jointly updated from interactions sampled with their current policies. Through this process, \textbf{PrefMind} trains the Generator to understand human preferences via explicit judgment and correction, and to translate this understanding into better responses. Experiments across three model scales show improvements in both standalone preference judgment and downstream response generation.

## 3. Enhancing Visual Task-Redirection Attacks on Vision-Language-Action Models via Closed-Loop Optimization

**arXiv ID:** [2610.23566v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23566v1)

**作者:** Chunjin Ye, Guanying Chen, Jianjie Huang, Jianhao Chen, Quanlin Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable language-guided robot manipulation, but remain vulnerable to visual attacks that undermine user control over robot behavior. Prior studies optimize visual perturbations using fixed target supervision. However, the perturbation can change earlier actions and, in turn, the physical states reached later. As the attacked trajectory evolves, this supervision may no longer match the behavior needed at the states actually reached. Accurately matching these targets can therefore fail to advance the attacker-specified task. Even with suitable supervision, reducing average matching error does not ensure sustained task progress. The robot may still fail at a critical stage, and further updates can disrupt previously successful behavior. To address these limitations, we propose TRACO, a framework that enhances existing targeted visual attacks through closed-loop optimization. Its Execution-Driven Supervision rebuilds supervision for the states reached under attack to guide subsequent updates. Within this framework, Progress-Guided Selection focuses updates where progress stalls while maintaining supervision on the earlier behavior needed to reach that point. Experiments across VLA models and simulation environments show improved task-redirection success with TRACO, highlighting the security implications of closed-loop attack optimization.

## 4. LAW: Unifying Language, Action, and World Modeling for Agentic Physical Intelligence

**作者:** Jiajun Cao, Kai Tang, Bohua Zhang, Zezhong Qian, Ying Li et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

World-action models jointly predict actions and future world states, but long-horizon manipulation further requires language reasoning to plan subtask objectives, assess execution progress, and guide action selection. Within the LAW agent, a hybrid-expert vision-language model plans the remaining subtasks, estimates the values of and fuses actions proposed by a world-action model, and asynchronously assesses execution status to support continued execution and subtask transitions. Within the world-action model, Mixture-of-Transformer Experts integrates modality-specific and shared computation for joint video--action modeling. Replacing the WAM backbone with OpenWAM-$alpha$ further raises the average success rate to 95.56%, establishing a new open-source state of the art on RoboTwin. Despite having only 2B parameters, our VLM also outperforms variants using Embodied-R1.5 and GPT-5.5 High. On three long-horizon real-robot tasks, LAW achieves 65% average success, compared with 28.33% for $pi_{0.5}$ and 25% for Fast-WAM.

## 5. Time Window Optimization for Motor Imagery Classification via Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2610.16921v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16921v1)

**作者:** Huan Luo, Na Lu, Xiaopeng Wang, Ziyang Shang

**分类:** cs.NE, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Online motor imagery-based brain-computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) typically decode continuous EEG using fixed-length sliding windows that may be misaligned with neural events. Existing window optimization methods often evaluate windows independently, overlooking temporal dependencies and potentially producing fragmented or physiologically implausible selections. We propose Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning (TSE-RL), a framework that incorporates prior knowledge of MI event structure into a constrained Markov decision process for adaptive window optimization. First, TSE-RL assigns each window an explicit semantic role through Discard, Onset, Sustain, or Offset actions, extending binary retain/discard decisions to event-level alignment. Second, a finite-state machine masks invalid actions to enforce the temporal order of MI phases and promote physiologically plausible window sequences. Third, a Transformer-based history encoder captures dependencies across windows, improving training stability and computational efficiency compared with recurrent architectures. TSE-RL is plug-and-play and can be integrated with diverse MI classifiers. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate consistent improvements in decoding performance in both cross-session and cross-subject settings. Visualization analyses further suggest that the learned window selection policy aligns with neurophysiological principles.

## 6. Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT): Evidence-Based Text Generation via a Closed-Loop Retrieve--Reflect--Revise Pipeline on Edge LLMs

**arXiv ID:** [2610.23311v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23311v1)

**作者:** Fabian Broszio, Michael Hoffmann, Adrian Paschke

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) can generate fluent outputs that lack source support or conflict with local operational constraints. We introduce Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT), a modular architecture that organizes generation as a closed Retrieve--Reflect--Revise loop. SRCoT separates environment state, persistent knowledge, accumulated experience, and short-term memory into provenance-labeled stores. BM25 retrieval supplies local context; subsequent passes expose intermediate drafts, missing-information markers, follow-up queries, and optional claim--evidence mappings. These artifacts inform retrieval, revision, clarification, or abstention decisions. We examine the architecture through qualitative, trace-based case studies in two fully synthetic settings: emergency medical services and Munich-Sim, an emergency-operations simulation. Local prototype runs on an Apple M4-family laptop with 128 GB memory illustrate source-labeled documentation, explicit gap marking, and structured dispatch-style outputs. The traces also reveal provenance-assignment errors, incomplete evidence coverage, and unsafe assumptions that remain possible despite prompting constraints. These cases demonstrate report-level inspectability and local execution, not comparative superiority, hallucination reduction, clinical validity, or operational safety. The architecture separates retrieval and control from the model interface, but behavior across different models remains to be evaluated. SRCoT is a research proof of concept requiring human review, with its principal contribution lying in an inspectable control procedure rather than a new model.

## 7. MOSS: Multimodal Origami Swamp Robot System

**作者:** T. Lan, A. Babgei, L. Romanello, M. Kovac, S. F. Armanini

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Robotic systems for environmental monitoring and ecological surveying in wetlands and swamps must operate across diverse terrestrial and aquatic conditions. Conventional fixed-geometry wheels cannot adapt their contact geometry to these varying conditions. This work presents MOSS (Multimodal Origami Swamp Robot System), a compact amphibious robotic platform with 3D-printed origami wheels. The compact crank mechanism switches the wheel between contracted and expanded configurations, changing its width, body diameter, and fin orientation simultaneously. The contracted configuration is better suited to efficient rolling on firmer ground, whereas the expanded configuration provides greater contact and terrain engagement on softer surfaces. MOSS successfully completed configuration changes in $1.95 \pm 0.11$ s and demonstrated continuous locomotion across soil-mud-water transitions. Outdoor trials further demonstrated operation across natural deformable terrain and water. These results show that adaptive wheel geometry provides terrain-dependent locomotion benefits and enables a single compact platform to traverse substantially different terrestrial and aquatic conditions.

## 8. Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models

**arXiv ID:** [2610.23344v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344v1)

**作者:** Minhua Lin, Zijun Wang, Zewen Liu, Zhaotian Weng, Bing He et al.

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate AI research, including language-model (LM) development, offering a potential path toward recursive self-improvement. However, turning experimental feedback into a learned ability to propose effective improvements across models faces two challenges. First, training such an ability with real feedback is expensive: reinforcement learning (RL) requires evaluating tens of thousands of proposals, each potentially requiring a full model-training run. Even at ten GPU-hours per run, reward collection alone would consume hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours, motivating a cheaper surrogate. Second, surrogate feedback can become unreliable as policy optimization favors overestimated proposals when the proposal distribution shifts. To address these challenges, we propose LM2LM, which trains LLMs with RL to generate effective LM improvement proposals. To provide scalable feedback, we use a research world model (RWM), a frozen LLM that predicts proposal gains from real experimental records provided in context, as the reward model for RL. To maintain feedback reliability, we periodically execute current-policy proposals ranked highly by the RWM and add their measured outcomes to its context. Across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments, real-execution evaluations on held-out tasks show that LM2LM enables Qwen3.8-27B to outperform GPT-6-sol, even when GPT-6-sol is given 300 real experimental records in context, while requiring 33% lower data-generation cost. LM2LM also generalizes to a model family unseen during training.

## 9. CoTA++: Understanding the Repeat Curse in dMLLMs from an Information Flow Perspective

**arXiv ID:** [2610.23290v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23290v1)

**作者:** Qiyan Zhao, Xiaofeng Zhang, Xu-Yao Zhang, Shijie Han, Zhepeng Li et al.

**分类:** cs.AI

**摘要:**

Diffusion-based multimodal large language models (dMLLMs) decode by iterative denoising with bidirectional attention, which precludes the standard autoregressive key--value cache. Practical deployments therefore rely on approximate caching that reuses intermediate states across denoising steps. Such caching, however, often makes a response repeat one token at adjacent positions in a run that breaks the sentence, a failure we term the \emph{Repeat Curse}. Tracing the context-token information flow reveals three disruptions. Within a step, caching disperses the attention that anchors a position on nearby context tokens. Across steps, similarity-based refresh is not staleness-aware, so some positions are decoded against stale key--value states. Across layers, the deep-layer entropy of the context fails to converge where repetition arises. We accordingly propose CoTA++, a training-free, plug-and-play framework that counteracts each disruption where it originates: Context-Token Attention Re-anchoring re-forms the attention routing against the current context, Decode-Aware Refresh reserves part of the cache's refresh budget for the positions about to be committed, and Context-Token Entropy-Guided Voting discounts the confidence of a position by the deep-layer entropy of its context, which reorders the unmasking. The three components act at non-overlapping stages of a decoding step and compose freely with existing dMLLMs and caches. Across three dMLLMs and two caching backends, CoTA++ reduces the sample repetition rate of long responses from up to 99.6\% to at most 17.0\%, recovers most of the accuracy lost to caching on nine benchmarks, and keeps the speedup of caching at under 15\% overhead, making approximate caching reliable for dMLLMs.

## 12. LED: Improving Masked Diffusion Language Models by Lookahead Editing

**作者:** Yuyan Zhou, Zeyuan Yang, Yuxuan Song, Zhicheng Cai, Hongli Yu et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) have emerged as a promising alternative to autoregressive language models, enabling non-autoregressive text generation through parallel decoding. Existing dLLMs can be broadly categorized into two families: masked diffusion models (MDMs) and uniform diffusion models (UDMs) In this work, we theoretically show that MDMs are easier to optimize due to their mask-based transition schedule but have lower computational expressivity, whereas UDMs enable iterative state revision and greater expressivity but are substantially harder to train. This trade-off raises a natural question: can we obtain the expressivity of iterative revision without giving up the simple, easy-to-optimize transition schedule of MDMs? Motivated by this analysis, we propose Lookahead Editing (\ours{}), which augments masked diffusion decoding with a rewritable proposal state. This design introduces a rewritable proposal that uses additional compute to improve expressivity while keeping the simple mask-based transition schedule of MDMs. We fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-8B to obtain LED-\texttt{7B} and LED-\texttt{8B}, respectively. Across eight benchmarks, LED SFT yields average gains of $+11.4$ and $+7.7$ points over standard MDM SFT. Moreover, using SFT alone, LED-\texttt{8B} outperforms LLaDA2.1-16B Q-mode, which is trained with continued pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning.

## 15. MLLM Frame Subset Ensembling for Audio-Visual Video QA and MLLM-based Reranking for Ad-hoc Video Search in TRECVID 2025

**摘要:**

This paper presents the overview of the runs related to the Ad-hoc Video Search (AVS) and Video Question Answering (VQA) tracks of TRECVID 2025 on behalf of the CERTH-ITI team. For the AVS track, we introduce a two-stage framework built on foundation models. Through the combination of fine-tuning and frame subset ensembling we achieve the highest score across 3 metrics in the VQA AG task and the second highest in the VQA MC task.

## 17. Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

**摘要:**

Large language model (LLM)-based agents and multi-agent systems increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration. However, these systems are prone to deviating from the overall goal as execution unfolds, which wastes execution resources and leads to undesired outcomes, a failure termed goal drift. In this work, we propose a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift occurs. The steering agent observes the execution context, deciding when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how to intervene with a high-cost slow path. ## 18. ReLaDrift: Recurrent Language Generation via Drifting for Test-Time Scaling
