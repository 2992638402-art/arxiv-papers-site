## 1. PerceptRL: Making Visual Perception Rewardable in MLLMs from Answers to Interactions

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37471v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37471v1)

**作者:** Tongkun Guan, Zhibo Yang, Mingkun Yang, Haocheng Wang, Wei Shen et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Fine-grained visual perception remains a bottleneck during post-training of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), as perceptual errors can constrain gains from reasoning-focused optimization. Although image-caption pre-training improves perception, its influence can weaken as subsequent stages change data mixtures and objectives. Continuing caption supervision during post-training is challenging because subjective, information-dense descriptions complicate verifiable reward design. We present PerceptRL, which strengthens perception at two complementary levels. Perception RL uses a multi-agent pipeline to extract verified errors from dense image captions and convert the corresponding visual facts into QA pairs with fixed answer labels and rule-based rewards. Chain QA links correlated errors, while multiple-choice QA combines unrelated errors. A specialized route targets high-error domains with tailored verification, and a domain-agnostic general route covers errors beyond these domains. Perception Agentic RL addresses fine-grained and dense cases unresolved by single-turn Perception RL through multi-turn inspection with visual tools. We observe coordinate drift even in models trained to use tools, where incorrect crops or annotations distort evidence and propagate errors. Coordinate Injection corrects spatial operations in cold-start trajectories, while a grounding-aware process reward supervises intermediate spatial fidelity alongside final-answer correctness. Training proceeds through shared cold-start SFT, single-turn Perception RL, and multi-turn Perception Agentic RL. Across seven benchmarks under a shared tool-use protocol, PerceptRL achieves 71.6% average accuracy, surpassing the base model with the same tools and the strongest evaluated frontier VLM by 15.7 and 5.2 percentage points, respectively.

## 2. EnvSched: Environment-Aware Scheduling for Rollout-Efficient Agent Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37609v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37609v1)

**作者:** Sihan Yang, Weichen Bai, Ruichuan An, Yiming Cheng, Zhiyuan Feng et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CL

**摘要:**

As executable environments for language agents scale, generating and verifying their rollouts becomes increasingly expensive. Under finite rollout budgets, this raises an important question: can data management at the environment level make agentic reinforcement learning (RL) more efficient by organizing, evaluating, and scheduling task instances together with the environments that determine their tools, states, transitions, and verification? To address this question, we introduce EnvSched (Environment-aware Scheduling), an online scheduler that learns from rollout history. EnvSched assigns scheduling value through two complementary modeling choices: exploitation prioritizes tasks likely to provide informative reward differences, while coverage spreads each batch across underobserved environments and interaction patterns. EnvSched organizes environment--task pairs as nodes in a dual-relation graph, with within-environment edges capturing task semantics and shared tool, state, and verifier structure, and cross-environment edges capturing abstract execution and reward topology. By propagating historical rollout statistics along both edge types, it predicts within-task reward variance. These predictions guide a scheduler that allocates each batch to tasks with high predicted reward variance and underobserved environments and tasks. Environment quotas and a similarity penalty further limit concentration and redundancy, yielding informative and diverse batches without additional pilot rollouts. In RL on Qwen3-8B, EnvSched achieves an average score of 48.81 across BFCL-v3, tau-Bench, and ACEBench-Agent after 40 training steps, exceeding the highest score attained by random task selection after 120 steps. It also improves downstream performance across model sizes at the same training budget.

## 3. VLA-Stem: A Lightweight Safety Mechanism for Vision-Language-Action Models via Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37378v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37378v1)

**作者:** Bojun Zou, Wenhao Yu, Jiaohao Li, Jiaxuan Lin, Guanting Ye et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models integrate high-level reasoning with low-level action execution for end-to-end robotic control. However, most VLA models target static manipulation in unconstrained environments, overlooking spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles. Fine-tuning VLAs on obstacle-avoidance data is a straightforward solution, but collecting such data is costly, especially for dynamic scenarios, and may degrade their original task capabilities. To address these limitations, we propose VLA-Stem, a lightweight, reinforcement-learning-based safety mechanism for VLA systems. Without fine-tuning the underlying VLA policy, VLA-Stem converts its action chunks into motion references and uses trajectory-level guidance to preserve their spatial intent while allowing flexible motion and timing adjustments. Conditioned on these references, robot proprioception, and local obstacle information, the Stem policy adaptively modifies execution at inference time, preserving the original task intent while enabling responsive adaptation to spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles. For robust and sample-efficient training, we combine teacher-student distillation with curriculum learning. We evaluate VLA-Stem on reference following, static spatial constraints, and dynamic obstacle avoidance. Across six constrained simulation settings, VLA-Stem achieves 52.8% average collision-free task success versus 23.7% for the strongest safety baseline, while preserving base-policy performance in obstacle-free settings; real-world experiments further show consistent collision reductions under static and dynamic obstacles. These results demonstrate that VLA-Stem couples high-level VLA task intent with rapid, reflex-like responses to immediate physical risks, providing a brainstem-inspired mechanism for robust robot execution.

## 4. Metacognitive Decoding: Efficient Inference for Diffusion Language Agents

**作者:** Mingyu Zhao, Shuhang Lin, Ligong Han, Xiaojing Yu, Zhenting Wang et al.

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

Diffusion language models offer a promising path to efficient agents through parallel token generation. However, efficient interaction requires adapting both where computation is spent and how much text is generated. We introduce a metacognitive controller that guides diffusion generation according to the needs of the current response and the agent's interaction with the environment. It learns when to finalize tokens and how far generation should extend, reducing overall computation within responses. Our controller, a lightweight plug-in for the diffusion backbone, is trained on agent trajectories to balance correctness and generation cost. This approach connects the agent's generation process to the interaction the agent serves. Ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of our approach.

## 6. World Dreamer v5-lite: A Multi-Robot World Action Model with One Dreamer

**作者:** Guoning Wu, Zijian Cai, Yuhang Zhang, Ying Liu, Yongbin Zheng

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Multi-robot control requires integrating distributed camera views while predicting separate action sequences. Most vision-language-action and world-action models target one robot. Combining multiple views alone does not establish spatial correspondence between them. World Dreamer v5-lite is a multi-robot world-action model built around a shared world state for robot-specific action prediction. Calibrated features from a pretrained video encoder populate this state in workspace coordinates; its spatial size remains fixed as views are added. Across four real-robot tasks, it reaches 78.8% mean success, a margin of 13.8 points. It also reaches 58.08% overall on RoboCasa365.

## 7. Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning of Co-Adaptive Hip Exoskeleton Control (MARL-Exo) for Metabolic Energy Reduction

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37441v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37441v1)

**作者:** Yifei Yuan, Jakob Wolf, Ghaith Androwis, Xianlian Zhou

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Objective: This study aims to determine whether a hip-exoskeleton policy trained in simulation, by co-adapting with a musculoskeletal human model under a metabolic-energy reward, reduces the metabolic cost of walking in new users without per-user tuning, and how mechanical-power shaping affects the learned assistance. Methods: We developed MARL-Exo, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework in which a full-body human model (304 muscle actuators; randomized anatomy, gait, and speed) and a bilateral hip-exoskeleton policy are trained concurrently in two stages. The exoskeleton observes only delayed hip kinematics and its past commands; its reward includes muscle metabolic power from a physiologically based energetics model, alone (Metabolic) or with mechanical-power shaping (Metabolic + Power). Conclusion: A metabolically informed policy co-adapted in simulation transferred to users without per-user tuning and consistently reduced the metabolic cost of walking with the device, while explicit power shaping reduced the simulated benefit. Significance: Unlike per-user human-in-the-loop optimization, this approach yields a single population-level controller and allows assistance objectives to be screened in simulation before human testing.

## 8. ThinkWorld: World Imagination Reinforces Chain-of-Dynamics Reasoning

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37666v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37666v1)

**作者:** Chaehyun Kim, Chi-Pin Huang, Jisoo Kim, Min-Hung Chen, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have made substantial progress in robotic manipulation, but their generalization remains constrained by the diversity and coverage of action-labeled robot demonstrations. Human videos provide much broader behavioral and environmental experience, while learning from them faces a fundamental trade-off between scalable data and explicit action grounding. We introduce ThinkWorld, a VLA learning framework that grounds action reasoning in world dynamics and strengthens it through reinforcement learning with world imagination. ThinkWorld represents each action thought as a Chain-of-Dynamics (CoD), a sequence of discrete motion tokens that captures the intermediate dynamics connecting the current world to a future state and serves as a common action representation across embodiments. We first initialize CoD with action-labeled demonstrations, then predict the future induced by each CoD and reinforce those whose predictions better match the observed outcome. This enables videos without action annotations to provide direct supervision from observed state transitions, allowing human and robot experience to refine the same reasoning space. Experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate effective human-to-robot transfer and strong generalization to unseen tasks and environments.

## 9. VideoLVR: Latent Visual Reasoning over Grounded Video Evidence

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37613v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37613v1)

**作者:** Shilong Li, Xingyuan Bu, Hao Shi, Yun Xing, Weixin Feng et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Chain-of-thought reasoning has substantially advanced video understanding in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, language-based reasoning traces do not provide a reliable mechanism for models to revisit relevant visual evidence, leading to visual forgetting and impairing their ability to reason over video content. Existing approaches maintain visual grounding during reasoning in two main ways: they either refer to evidence using textual coordinates, which specify symbolic locations rather than the visual content itself, or reinsert cropped regions or frames into the multimodal context, preserving visual details at the cost of substantial context expansion. To address these limitations, we introduce VideoLVR, which constructs latent visual evidence from local regions at relevant video moments, thereby providing visual support for intermediate reasoning steps. To supervise latent visual evidence generation, we introduce a visual token prediction objective that formulates visual token reconstruction as prediction over a vocabulary derived from the input video, thereby unifying visual evidence reconstruction and text generation within the same token prediction framework. This shared prediction framework enables MLLMs to reason over interleaved text tokens and latent visual states while integrating evidence from different moments. We further extend VideoLVR to reinforcement learning by treating sampled visual tokens as actions alongside text tokens and jointly optimizing the policy using final-answer rewards. By efficiently revisiting task-relevant visual information during reasoning, VideoLVR improves video understanding without substantially expanding the multimodal context. Experiments on multiple video benchmarks demonstrate its effectiveness, especially on tasks requiring fine-grained visual evidence.

## 10. Gym-Anything-RT: Scaling Real-Time Environments for Vision-Language Agents

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37567v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37567v1)

**作者:** Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI

**摘要:**

AI agents have made substantial progress in domains where they have a non-trivial amount of time to deliberate between actions, such as mathematics, software engineering, and performing tasks on desktop applications. It is less clear how such models fare in settings that demand real-time interaction, such as playing a video game or operating a physical device. In these settings, the environment state may evolve as the agent is reasoning, or even as the underlying model is performing a forward pass. To gain insight into the real-time interaction capabilities of vision-language agents, we introduce Gym-Anything-RT. Our key idea is to construct visual minigames that demand a portfolio of capabilities required for interactive real-time settings. Gym-Anything-RT uses an agentic pipeline to automatically generate such environments based on a taxonomy of six capabilities required for real-time interaction, including the ability to perceive the environment and act under an evolving environment state. A key feature of Gym-Anything-RT is that it allows for controlled comparison of real-time interaction capabilities: we can run the same minigame in real time, or pause or slow down the environment to relax the requirement for real-time actions. We use Gym-Anything-RT to construct 100 real-time minigames and evaluate AI agents on them. We find large capability gaps in real-time interaction. For example, GPT-6 Astra (xHigh) achieves a pass rate of only 9.0% on the real-time minigames, which rises to 75.0% when the environment is paused. We also identify intriguing behaviors by the most powerful frontier agents: for instance, when given the ability to write code, these agents write code that bypasses the need to perceive and act in real time. We publicly release Gym-Anything-RT and the generated minigames to spur further research on AI agents in real-time interactive settings.

## 11. Scaling Laws for Tool-Use Agents via Controllable Environment Synthesis

**作者:** Andrew Szot, Samira Abnar, Omar Attia, Abbas Kazerouni, Michael Kirchhof et al.

**分类:** cs.LG

**摘要:**

We explore how to build performant large language model (LLM) tool-use agents that complete complex, multi-step tasks. Specifically, we study how data composition in synthetically generated tool-use environments affects the training of such agents. To do so, we propose the SynthAPI pipeline, which explicitly controls the diversity of generated environments along the axes of tools, tasks, and the databases on which the tools operate. By systematically varying SynthAPI's generation controls in 458,752 tasks across 849 trained models of four different sizes, we derive a scaling law that predicts downstream tool-use agent generalization based on the scale and composition of the synthesized environments. As data budgets grow, scaling tool and database diversity becomes more effective than increasing the number of tasks, and increasing data complexity yields larger gains. ## 16. Where Should the Evaluation Budget Go in LLM-Driven Agent Scaffold Evolution?

**arXiv ID:** [2610.37276v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37276v1)

**作者:** Sichun Luo, Yi Huang, Mukai Li, Haochen Luo, Guanzhi Deng et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.NE

**摘要:**

Large language model (LLM)-driven agent scaffold evolution improves agent workflows through iterative candidate generation and evaluation. Evaluation both guides subsequent search and determines the final scaffold, but evaluating every candidate on the full task set incurs substantial execution costs. We investigate how evaluation budgets can be allocated across these two roles. Our experiments show that sparse feedback can sustain productive evolution, while additional targeted evaluation improves final selection. Replay analyses further show that shared informative items and a common scoring reference support comparisons among closely matched candidates. Motivated by these findings, we propose \method, an evaluation allocation framework that combines sparse search-time feedback with targeted final confirmation. After search, \method refits the model and applies paired successive halving, reusing accumulated observations and executing only missing evaluations. Across four benchmarks and two evolution frameworks, \method achieves higher mean performance than full evaluation in most settings while using approximately half as many evaluation cells.

## 19. EvoRouter: Cost-Efficient Multi-Agent Routing via Insight-Guided On-Policy Distillation
