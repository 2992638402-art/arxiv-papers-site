## 1. TalentRL: Strategy-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Hierarchical Skill Routing

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41143v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41143v1)

**作者:** Huancheng Chen, Xiaodi Sun, Zhaoqiong Huang, Michelle Chang, Jingwen Lu

**分类:** cs.LG

**摘要:**

Natural-language skills enable large language model (LLM) agents to reuse procedures across tasks, yet most systems retrieve individual skills from a flat SkillBank without explicitly modeling the high-level strategy that governs their selection and use. We introduce TalentRL, a strategy-guided reinforcement learning framework for hierarchical routing over procedural memory. Coarse-grained talents represent recurring strategies for skill use, while fine-grained skills provide executable procedures. For each task, the policy model selects a talent that guides execution and softly prioritizes its associated skills during similarity-based retrieval. However, since task outcomes depend jointly on talent selection, retrieved skill context, and downstream execution, terminal rewards alone cannot isolate the contribution of the routing decision. TalentRL therefore trains talent routing with reward contrasts between rollouts of the same task under matched interventions on the talent-conditioned context, and optimizes downstream execution with group-relative advantages. Across seven open-domain and multi-hop question-answering benchmarks, talent-aware supervised fine-tuning improves average success rate by 7.53%, 6.71%, and 9.21% over the corresponding zero-shot baselines for Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-8B, and Llama-3.1-8B, respectively. Reinforcement learning further improves these SFT checkpoints by 7.58% to 9.32%, and TalentRL achieves the highest average success rate among all compared methods on all three backbones. On the agentic benchmarks ALFWorld and WebShop, TalentRL achieves the highest ALFWorld success rate (99.3%) and the highest WebShop score (90.7) among all compared methods.

## 2. ROSTrans: Empirically Benchmarking LLMs for ROS1-to-ROS2 Migration

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41168v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41168v1)

**作者:** Yichen Zhao, Minghao Shao, Zhuoyan Yu, Yijun Shen, Boyuan Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.SE, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Although large language models have shown strong capabilities in domain-specific code generation, their performance in robotics software development, especially for the Robot Operating System (ROS), remains largely unexplored. ROS code generation presents distinctive challenges, including complex inter-node coordination, middleware configuration, and lifecycle semantics that go far beyond standard coding benchmarks, and many important correctness properties become observable only once migrated nodes are launched and communicate. We present ROSTrans, a benchmark for evaluating LLMs on ROS1-to-ROS2 code migration, a representative and practically important setting for ROS code generation. ROSTrans comprises 108 scaffolded completion tasks drawn from a diverse set of 70+ open-source ROS1 repositories and organized into a hierarchical taxonomy assessing interface-level, behavioral-level and system-level correctness. For every task we establish an executable reference: a verified ROS2 implementation, a runtime test suite that launches the nodes and exercises their topics, services, actions and parameters, and a container ensuring reproducibility. Candidates are scored by injecting only their target files into an otherwise fixed copy of the reference package, so a candidate can neither supply nor weaken the tests it is judged by. Evaluating six frontier LLMs over five stochastic generations per task, we find pass@1 below 10.9%, and that 56.5% of all samples build successfully and then fail at runtime,and 32.0% fail runtime assertions on the migrated interfaces, a class of error no compile-only oracle can detect. Execution-guided repair further improves task success, reaching 49.1% and 47.2% for Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.5 after three rounds, respectively.

## 3. Agentic World Modeling for Agent Test-time Scaling without Environment Replica

**arXiv ID:** [2610.40927v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40927v1)

**作者:** Wendi Li, Huseyin A. Inan, Wei-Ning Chen, Yanzhi Chen, Chetan Bansal et al.

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Test-time scaling (TTS) improves large language model agents by sampling and selecting among multiple candidate trajectories, but common realizations assume that these trajectories can be executed in parallel. This assumption breaks in realistic deployments, where enterprise workspaces and cloud services are shared and persistent, costly or infeasible to replicate, and actions may introduce irreversible side effects or cross-trajectory interference. Existing generative world models avoid direct execution, but typically compress environment state into a single prompt, limiting their ability to reliably simulate transitions under partial observability. Instead, we introduce Agentic World Modeling (AWM), which reformulates next-observation prediction as a multi-turn evidence-acquisition task. For each hypothetical action, an agentic simulator adaptively queries the real environment through safe read-only interfaces, reasons over the returned evidence, and uses executable computation to construct the resulting observation without mutating shared state. Integrated with trajectory-level TTS, AWM enables parallel exploration over a single shared environment. Across $τ$-bench, AppWorld, and OfficeBench, AWM outperforms a single-shot simulation baseline by 40.07% on average, and surpasses a trained world-model baseline by 12.36% on average. These results establish interactive, read-grounded simulation as a practical foundation for safe and scalable agentic test-time scaling in shared, non-resettable environments.

## 4. LLM Detoxification via Transition-Aware Reward-Guided Representation Sampling

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41165v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41165v1)

**作者:** Aishan Liu, Yisong Xiao, Xianglong Liu, Yongxin Huang, Siyuan Liang et al.

**分类:** cs.AI

**摘要:**

Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate toxic content, motivating effective test-time detoxification. Existing representation steering methods often rely on static or coarse interventions and provide imprecise guidance because they inadequately model transitions between toxic and non-toxic generations. We propose Transition-Aware Reward-Guided Representation Sampling (TARGRS), a test-time framework that learns reward guidance over toxicity transitions in latent representation space. During exploration, progressive steering along the toxicity direction constructs trajectories from non-toxic to toxic states. Toxicity scores along these trajectories turn sparse annotations into dense, listwise supervision for a transition-aware token-level reward model. During inference, TARGRS uses this model to guide coarse-to-fine sampling along the opposite, non-toxic direction: coarse sampling identifies the intervention needed to reach non-toxic regions, and refinement selects a detoxified representation with minimal perturbation. Experiments on 10 LLMs across RealToxicityPrompts, BOLD, and AttaQ show that TARGRS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, reducing toxicity by up to 69.38% on RealToxicityPrompts. TARGRS runs nearly as fast as the original LLM while preserving its core capabilities with minimal impact. By intervening in the language decoder space, TARGRS also extends to Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), reducing toxicity by up to 64.23% across 3 representative LVLMs.

## 5. Communication-Efficient Private Federated Reinforcement Learning with Post-Quantum Authentication for Vehicular Networks

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41055v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41055v1)

**作者:** Hojat Allah Salehi, Devrim Aras, Md Jueal Mia, M. Hadi Amini, Imtiaz Karim et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CR

**摘要:**

Federated reinforcement learning supports cooperative vehicular routing without centralizing trajectory data, but model updates may reveal private information and incur communication overhead over intermittent vehicle-to-infrastructure links, particularly when post-quantum authentication is required. We study correlated private quantization, using complementary shared randomness between paired vehicles to reduce aggregation mean squared error while preserving the marginal parameter-level local differential privacy and one-bit communication cost of independent quantization. A tunable extension mixes shared and independent randomness to bound privacy leakage given paired outputs and shared-randomness side information. For this extension, Rényi differential privacy accounting establishes vector-level local differential privacy for one model upload even when pairings are revealed. Under matched vector-level privacy and identical local and global parameters, the extension yields 16.5% lower analytical aggregation mean squared error than independent quantization. We evaluate cooperative routing by coupling traffic simulation with packet-level simulation of post-quantum-authenticated vehicle-to-infrastructure communication. Compared to full-precision updates, one-bit uploads reduce authenticated uplink size by 92.4% and increase mean verified updates aggregated per round from 19.6 to 26.5. Under mobility-constrained communication at a parameter-level privacy budget of 1, correlated private quantization reduces mean travel time by 14.3% relative to independent quantization at the same budget and communication cost. At a parameter-level privacy budget of 0.3, a vehicle-origin inference attack trained on independent simulations reaches 30% accuracy with correlated private quantization and 22% with independent quantization after 100 rounds, versus 91% without privacy and 10% for random guessing.

## 6. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning of Transformer Encoders for Audio-Image-Symbolic Music Retrieval

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41512v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41512v1)

**作者:** Eliseo Fuentes-Martinez, Antonio Hidalgo-Centeno, Jorge Calvo-Zaragoza, Antonio Javier Gallego

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.SD, eess.AS

**摘要:**

Score-based multimodal music retrieval requires representations that are both discriminative within each modality and compatible across audio recordings, sheet music images, and symbolic scores. This paper investigates the effectiveness of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for adapting pretrained music encoders and examines whether modality-specific improvements translate into better multimodal representations. We compare full fine-tuning, LoRA, and QLoRA across nine pretrained audio, image, and symbolic score encoders on TriScore and the Musical Theme Dataset. Encoders are first evaluated and selected through supervised unimodal classification, using frozen-encoder linear probing as a reference. We then introduce a controlled 2x2 design crossing encoder adaptation with task-specific shared projection, comparing frozen and adapted representations before and after projection. LoRA or QLoRA is selected in 14 of 18 modality-target combinations, frequently matching or outperforming full fine-tuning while optimizing substantially fewer parameters. However, these unimodal gains do not transfer uniformly to multimodal classification. Adaptation and projection are complementary for composer classification on TriScore, largely redundant for TriScore instrument and the Musical Theme Dataset instrument and ensemble tasks, and partially interfering for composer classification on the Musical Theme Dataset. The final adapted-and-projected title representation on TriScore supports piece-level retrieval, reaching average Top-5 and Top-10 accuracies of 55.32 and 64.68, respectively. Cross-modal exclusion and neighborhood analyses further reveal strong modality-specific local structure in the projected spaces. Overall, PEFT provides an effective and computationally efficient alternative to full fine-tuning, but improved unimodal discrimination alone does not guarantee better multimodal organization.

## 7. Direct Action-Manifold Modeling for Efficient and Robust Bimanual Manipulation

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41643v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41643v1)

**作者:** Hongren Wang, Mingyu You, Zihan Li, Zhipeng Wang, Shuo Jiang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Diffusion and flow matching visuomotor policies require iterative denoising or numerical integration for multimodal action generation, introducing latency and execution mismatch. We instead consider a different formulation: conditioned on vision and proprioception, demonstrated bimanual action chunks concentrate in a structured observation-dependent region of joint-action space, making the action target more direct to model than the generative process. Based on this view, we propose the Direct Manifold Action Transformer (DMAT), which reformulates action generation as the direct modeling of clean, observation-conditioned joint-space action chunks from stochastic interpolants, thereby enabling single-step inference without relying on iterative generative inference or auxiliary trajectory-straightening procedures. Across 567 standard and out-of-distribution (OOD) trials per method, DMAT outperformed Improved 3D Diffusion Policy (IDP3) and DiTFlow3D in aggregate success rate, achieving relative improvements of 129.1% and 95.7%, respectively, while generating action chunks 2.06x and 1.34x faster and remaining robust under repeated 50 N interventions. These results pave the way for a novel generative paradigm for efficient and robust bimanual manipulation.

## 8. Bayesian Offline Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Latent World Models

**作者:** Xi Xiong, Lu Liu, Yiming Li, Jakob Foerster

**分类:** cs.MA

**摘要:**

Model-based offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) provides a promising approach to multi-agent decision-making under partial observability, where the global state is unavailable from offline data. However, limited dataset coverage can lead to substantial out-of-distribution (OOD) model errors, which may mislead policy optimization and degrade downstream performance. Existing model-based offline MARL methods typically rely on access to global states or mitigate model errors through pessimistic or conservative policy optimization, leaving the reliability and refinement of learned world models less explored. We propose a Bayesian offline MARL framework that infers latent global states from decentralized observation-action histories and iteratively refines a posterior world model using only offline data. Experiments on partially observable multi-agent variants of D4RL MuJoCo tasks demonstrate competitive policy performance across diverse offline data conditions, while MPIL-guided refinement improves world model prediction and downstream policy performance. These results support a principled connection between world model reliability, offline data coverage, and policy performance in partially observable offline MARL.

## 9. Contact-based optimization for collision-free motion planning for a million robots

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41548v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41548v1)

**作者:** Jan Kamm, Antonio Terpin, Raffaello D'Andrea

**分类:** cs.RO, eess.SY

**摘要:**

Multi-agent robotic systems have attracted growing interest in recent years, with applications ranging from coordinated drone shows to autonomous operations on land, in the air, and on water. At the planning level, robot motion is often modeled using double-integrator dynamics (with low-level controllers handling the physical dynamics during execution) with a minimum-energy objective subject to collision-avoidance constraints and limits on position, velocity, and acceleration. The complexity of the problem resides in the constraints, which couple the trajectories and make the optimization problem nonconvex. Existing numerical approaches typically discretize and optimize entire trajectories, which becomes expensive for large fleets. We exploit the structure of optimal trajectories by organizing optimization around contacts, at which robots reach the minimum allowed separation. We formulate a coupled algebraic system in contact variables and reconstruct trajectories analytically between contacts. Our algorithm, sequential contact insertion (SCI), starts from unconstrained trajectories, inserts contacts at separation violations, and jointly adjusts their times and states. Numerical experiments demonstrate planning for one million robots in minutes and thousands of robots in fractions of a second. On problems feasible for a fixed-grid sequential convex programming baseline, SCI achieves median speedups of several orders of magnitude at matching solution accuracy.

## 11. Density-Guided Bootstrap Boundaries for Latent World-Model Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41022v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41022v1)

**作者:** Shashank Gupta, Pilhwa Lee

**分类:** cs.LG, stat.ML

**摘要:**

Latent world models allow reinforcement learning agents to learn policies from imagined trajectories, yet determining the optimal depth of model reliance remains a fundamental challenge. Fixed imagination horizons apply uniform prediction depths regardless of trajectory reliability, exposing target values to compounding model error or over-relying on critic approximations. We introduce density-guided bootstrap boundaries, a trajectory-dependent mechanism that adapts the depth of imagined returns using a score-informed relative density proxy. The proxy accumulates the estimated divergence of a score-corrected auxiliary field formed by a feature displacement network and a noise-conditioned score estimator along imagined paths. We propose two gating mechanisms: a hard boundary that halts return expansion at a discrete state, and a soft boundary that continuously shifts the return recursion toward critic bootstrapping. Both variants preserve boundary value estimates and apply corresponding survival weights to actor and critic losses. Formulated independently of specific dynamics models and instantiated within Dreamer V3, we prove that the resulting gated Bellman operator is a strict contraction mapping and that early bootstrapping monotonically reduces conditional target variance. We clarify the theoretical assumptions distinguishing our expansion proxy from exact path likelihoods. Across continuous-control tasks and Atari Pong, empirical evaluations show that dynamic boundary selection enhances sample efficiency and late-training performance over fixed-horizon and auxiliary-enabled baselines.

## 13. Lineage Is Memory: Parent Inheritance and Feedback Disclosure in Short-Budget LLM-Guided Optimization

**arXiv ID:** [2610.41157v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41157v1)

**作者:** Shanglin Yang, Zhan Shi

**分类:** cs.SE, cs.AI, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Iterative language-model optimizers refine prompts and programs by evaluating a candidate, showing a proposer model a sample of failing cases, and keeping revisions that improve performance. Recent work attributes the limits of this loop to narrow single-round reflection, introducing full-set error clustering, targeted minibatches, or larger proposer models so the optimizer sees more failure modes per step. Yet every existing evaluation tests these mechanisms exclusively inside lineage-preserving search, where each revision edits a surviving parent, leaving the contribution of parent inheritance confounded with the breadth of per-round feedback. Across 1,750 pre-registered runs spanning nine prompt and program synthesis benchmarks, we separate parent inheritance from feedback disclosure under matched evaluation budgets. We find that greedy parent inheritance drives the vast majority of optimization progress, outperforming seed-reset resampling even when seed-reset proposals receive a clustered summary of all failures in every round or use a larger proposer model. While full-set error clustering and diverse failure selection improve single-step rewrites from the seed and accelerate the opening rounds of search, sequential parent inheritance rapidly closes this gap as solved error modes are retained in the candidate and filtered from subsequent failure lists. Moreover, decomposing token expenditure in GEPA reveals that proposer reflection accounts for only a small fraction of total cost whereas validation and minibatch gating consume nearly the entire budget, showing that greedy parent inheritance over raw failures captures the gains of complex reflective loops at a fraction of their compute.

## 14. Role-Aware Server Optimization for Multimodal Federated Learning with Missing Modalities

**作者:** Liuyin Shi, Wenbo He

**分类:** cs.LG

**摘要:**

Multimodal federated learning (MFL) trains a shared model by aggregating client updates without exchanging raw data. Under missing modalities, clients adapt active modality encoders and a shared prediction block (fusion and classifier) to different input subsets. This raises two server-side questions: which clients should contribute to each component, and how should their aggregates be incorporated across rounds? Experiments across datasets and fusion architectures demonstrate the effectiveness of RASO under missing modalities.

## 15. CoFiBreak: A Coarse-to-Fine Diversity Reward for Effective Multi-Turn Jailbreak

**arXiv ID:** [2610.40926v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40926v1)

**作者:** Jialin Song, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, Mingqian Feng, Chengquan Guo et al.

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

Automated red teaming trains an attacker large language model (LLM) to elicit harmful responses from a victim LLM, but reinforcement learning can collapse attackers onto a narrow set of prompts when attack success is the only reward. Such a collapse of attack diversity can bias safety evaluation toward repeatedly testing near-duplicate attack patterns rather than the underlying harmful intents. Existing methods often trade off attack success or inference cost for greater content or strategy diversity. We propose CoFiBreak, a coarse-to-fine diversity reward for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that improves attack diversity while preserving strong attack effectiveness and low inference cost. The coarse reward broadens which strategies the attacker uses by encouraging strategies underrepresented within each GRPO group, while the fine reward broadens how those strategies are realized by discouraging reuse of the same personas, framings, and output formats after harmful-intent information is removed from prompt embeddings. Diversity rewards apply only to attacks that already exceed an effectiveness threshold, so diversity cannot be gained through ineffective attacks. Since both rewards are computed only during training, CoFiBreak also retains inference efficiency. Trained with Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as the victim, CoFiBreak discovers 1.6-2.0x as many distinct successful fine-grained strategies as the strongest baseline across frontier victims on HarmBench. It reaches 98.8% ASR@10 (attack success rate with 10 generations per intent) on AdvBench with the highest diversity score among all compared methods, which is 2.1x that of the strongest-attacking baseline. The resulting attacks transfer to unseen frontier LLMs, reaching 71.0% ASR@10 on AdvBench with the unseen victim Claude-Opus-4.6. The code will be released.

## 16. When Depth Lies: Benchmarking Vision-Language Models on Mirror-Induced RGB-D Ambiguity

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Vision-language models (VLMs) have made significant progress in spatial reasoning, particularly by integrating RGB images with depth data, which is often assumed to faithfully reflect physical distances. However, mirrors violate this assumption: they distort depth readings while reproducing object appearances, making reflections difficult to distinguish from real objects. As existing benchmarks rarely consider such reflective scenarios, VLM performance in mirror scenes remains largely unexplored. In this work, we introduce a mirror-centric RGB-D dataset from reflective scenes to evaluate the spatial reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs in these scenarios. ## 17. HEMO: Harness Self-Evolution for Multi-Objective Combinatorial Optimization

**作者:** Jianghan Zhu, Cong Zhang, Ni Zhang, Yifan Yang, Yew Soon Ong et al.

**分类:** cs.NE, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Automatic operator design (AOD) has increasingly adopted large language models (LLMs) to generate problem-specific search and variation operators for multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCO). Yet most methods evolve the operators while leaving the process that discovers them fixed. This limits adaptation as new Pareto trade-offs and operator interactions change where and how the search should proceed. We introduce HEMO, which makes the AOD harness itself a self-evolving optimization target. HEMO maintains a persistent harness state and updates one module at a time, revising the logic that determines which experience to retrieve, which operators to improve, which parents to select, and how to formulate the orchestration of search steps. Experiments across eight MOCO tasks and two structurally distinct evolutionary backbones demonstrate strong performance against conventional evolutionary, neural, and LLM-based AOD baselines, together with effective transfer to related tasks. HEMO extends automatic operator design beyond evolving individual operators to improving the search process that discovers them.

## 18. Where Should the Evaluation Budget Go in LLM-Driven Agent Scaffold Evolution?

**摘要:**

Large language model (LLM)-driven agent scaffold evolution improves agent workflows through iterative candidate generation and evaluation. Evaluation both guides subsequent search and determines the final scaffold, but evaluating every candidate on the full task set incurs substantial execution costs. Our experiments show that sparse feedback can sustain productive evolution, while additional targeted evaluation improves final selection. Across four benchmarks and two evolution frameworks, \method achieves higher mean performance than full evaluation in most settings while using approximately half as many evaluation cells.
