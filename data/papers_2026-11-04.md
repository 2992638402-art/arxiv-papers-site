## 1. Training Specialist LLMs on Your Phone: Forward-Only Optimization via Block-Spectral Coordinates

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04797v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04797v1)

**作者:** Liyan Tan, Yequan Zhao, Ziyue Liu, Yupeng Su, Ruijie Zhang et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, eess.SY

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) on personal devices keep user data local, but limited memory restricts their general capability. Since most user-specific tasks do not require the breadth of frontier models, a practical solution is to adapt a shared on-device model into lightweight specialists. However, backpropagation is prohibitive in memory, and users rarely provide labeled data. We present MobileSpec, a system that trains specialists with forward passes alone and learns from automatically checked task outcomes, as in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Because forward-only updates are built from random perturbations, training is efficient only when these perturbations resemble the weight changes of RLVR, which largely preserve the singular values of pretrained weights and rotate their singular vectors. The core algorithm of MobileSpec, FuRA-S, produces such perturbations while training only the block-spectral coordinates of pretrained weights, namely the singular values of their column blocks. As we show theoretically and empirically, rescaling these blocks independently shifts their relative contributions to each weight matrix and thereby rotates its singular vectors. Since this update can be rewritten as a transformation of the layer input, FuRA-S also adapts INT4 models without modifying their weights. With specialists of only 1-5 MB that update 0.015-0.05% of the parameters, MobileSpec raises the success rate of a phone-use agent from 6-23% up to 89-100% and trains a personalized 8B model on an iPhone 16 Pro overnight. On RLVR benchmarks, FuRA-S is competitive with full-parameter evolution strategies (ES) and outperforms ES over LoRA adapters and quantized ES on INT4 models.

## 2. Language Model Augmented Decision Transformer for Multi-Task Offline Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04031v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04031v1)

**作者:** Xinbo Zhao, Yuhang Liu, Xin Zhang, Lei Le, Zhaohan Xi et al.

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Offline multi-task reinforcement learning (MTRL) aims to learn a unified policy from heterogeneous offline datasets that generalizes across tasks. However, existing offline MTRL methods often struggle with two fundamental issues: severe per-task data scarcity and limited cross-task generalization. In this paper, we propose Language-Model Augmented Decision Transformer (LaMA-DT), a unified sequence-modeling framework that leverages a single large language model (LLM) for both trajectory augmentation and multi-task policy learning. LaMA-DT uses natural-language task descriptions to guide task-aligned trajectory augmentation through a set of supervised generation and alignment objectives, and incorporates a scoring-and-filtering mechanism to retain high-quality augmented trajectories. The same LLM is then repurposed as a language-conditioned Decision Transformer and fine-tuned on a mixture of real and augmented data. Experiments on Meta-World and MuJoCo multi-task benchmarks demonstrate that LaMA-DT consistently outperforms competitive baselines, particularly in low-data regimes, showing that language-guided data augmentation substantially improves cross-task generalization.

## 3. Physical Diffusion Policy via MPM-Guided 3D Gaussian Simulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04493v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04493v1)

**作者:** Can Wang, Qiyuan Qiao, Lei Liu, Ge Yuan, Xinhui Liu et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Learning robust manipulation policies for diverse everyday objects remains challenging because objects with different materials exhibit distinct deformation and contact dynamics. While diffusion policies provide a powerful framework for modeling multimodal manipulation behaviors, they typically lack explicit physical reasoning and may produce averaged actions when facing heterogeneous objects. We present a physics-aware diffusion policy that augments visual imitation learning with object physical understanding. Our framework recovers material properties from visual demonstrations by coupling 3D Gaussian Splatting with differentiable Material Point Method simulation, where video guidance and particle-level constraints enable accurate physical parameter estimation. The estimated physics conditions are further integrated into a material-aware Mixture-of-Experts diffusion policy, allowing adaptive behavior generation across different physical regimes. For practical deployment, we introduce a physics memory bank that provides efficient physical retrieval without requiring online simulation optimization. Experiments on a wide range of simulated and real-world objects demonstrate improved manipulation success and generalization over existing diffusion-based approaches. These results show that incorporating physical knowledge is critical for scalable robot manipulation in diverse environments.

## 4. Hybrid Particle-Voxel Dynamics for Multimodal 3D World Simulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04492v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04492v1)

**作者:** Jimmy Wang, Yifan Chou, Kaifeng Zhang, Yixuan Wang, Binghao Huang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Predicting how the world evolves in response to robot actions is central to policy learning, evaluation, and planning. However, many action-conditioned world models predict future observations in 2D image or video space without explicitly modeling the underlying 3D scene, making spatial consistency across viewpoints and over long-horizon interactions challenging. We introduce 3D World Simulator, an action-conditioned multimodal world modeling framework that jointly predicts appearance, geometry, and tactile observations from a unified 3D representation. Our approach combines a hybrid particle-voxel autoencoder that compresses point clouds into compact 3D voxel latents with an action-conditioned latent dynamics model that predicts their evolution. The shared 3D representation supports geometrically consistent rendering from arbitrary viewpoints and flexible integration of spatially aligned tactile signals. Our experiments demonstrate interactive, long-horizon, multimodal prediction at 50 Hz. We then show how our model enables scalable data generation for policy training and evaluation using tactile and multi-view visual observations, including wrist-mounted and novel camera views. Furthermore, we scale our method to large datasets containing diverse objects and demonstrate model-based planning with unseen objects and robot configurations. Together, these results highlight the potential of hybrid particle-voxel dynamics as a foundation for multimodal 3D world simulation.

## 5. Density-Aware Reward Scaling: A Reinforcement Learning Framework for Reward-Proportional Sampling

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04792v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04792v1)

**作者:** Ria Shekhawat, Abhijeet Sinha, Yingtao Zhu, Dianbo Liu

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Reinforcement learning objectives that maximize expected return can concentrate probability mass on a small subset of high-reward outcomes, limiting diversity when multiple distinct solutions are desirable. We introduce Density-Aware Reward Scaling (DARS), a simple reward-processing mechanism that promotes reward-proportional sampling in continuous outcome spaces while retaining standard policy-gradient training. DARS estimates the local density of outcomes within each on-policy rollout group and inversely scales rewards by this density. The method requires no learned density model, auxiliary objective, critic, or modification to the underlying policy-gradient objective. In a controlled multimodal navigation task, DARS-GRPO avoids the single-mode collapse exhibited by GRPO and closely recovers the target mode allocation. Applied to text-to-image prompt adaptation, DARS-GRPO consistently improves image and prompt diversity across datasets, Policy LMs, and reward settings while maintaining good semantic alignment. These results show that density-aware reward scaling provides a lightweight way to improve coverage of diverse high-reward outcomes within existing reinforcement-learning pipelines.

## 6. Dory1.0-4B: A Looped Normalized-GPT

**arXiv ID:** [2611.03998v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03998v1)

**作者:** Maxim Fishman, Simeng Sun, Shantanu Acharya, Faisal Ladhak, Brian Chmiel et al.

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

We introduce Dory1.0-4B, a 4-billion-parameter looped (latent-recurrent) language model with elastic depth: a single checkpoint serves different inference compute budgets. Dory1.0-4B is built on a normalized Transformer (nGPT) backbone with interleaved sliding-window and full-attention layers, organized into an input block, a shared recurrent block, and an output block. At inference, the recurrent block can be applied a variable number of times. We pre-trained Dory1.0-4B on 6 trillion tokens. The first 4.5 trillion tokens were trained in NVFP4, and additional pre-training then extended the context length to 256K tokens. Next, we post-trained the model using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), with a difficulty-aware loop curriculum that routes harder prompts to deeper loops. Increasing the loop count improves accuracy, with the largest gains on coding, agentic, and long-context benchmarks. With two or more loops, Dory1.0-4B outperforms larger models such as Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma 4 12B, and Gemma 4 26B-A4B on average across 12 benchmarks. Looping also makes Dory1.0-4B more token-efficient on agentic benchmarks: with four loops, it generates 1.3-2.5x fewer tokens than with one loop, as the extra computation is spent in latent space rather than on longer outputs. To reduce memory during looping, Dory1.0-4B caches keys and values only from the last loop, keeping its KV-cache footprint equal to that of a non-looped model at any loop count while retaining most of the accuracy gains. We open-source the BF16, MXFP8, NVFP4, and GGUF quantized checkpoints.

## 8. Spectrally Routed Diffusion Policy for Multi-Task Robotic Manipulation

**作者:** Mingcong Li, Yihan Zhou, Jiayu Qu, Xueyang Guo, Yilin Mo

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Diffusion-based policies have achieved strong performance on single-task robotic manipulation. However, a single model trained jointly on multiple tasks consistently underperforms the per-task specialists trained on the same demonstrations. We focus on two challenges. First, the convolutional encoders commonly used in diffusion-based policies produce visual conditions that are not discriminative enough. Second, gradients from incompatible action modes update the shared denoiser's dense parameters along the same directions, causing destructive interference during joint training. On four real-world bimanual tasks, Stage 2 reaches a 43.0% average success rate and improves over Stage 1 by 19.0 points.

## 12. VIAS: Value-Isolating Activation Steering for Multi-Value Alignment and Compliance for Organisations

**arXiv ID:** [2611.03518v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03518v1)

**作者:** Avni Mittal, Avinash Anand, Dikshant Kukreja, Erik Cambria, Rajiv Ratn Shah et al.

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Organisations increasingly deploy large language model agents that must follow organisational values. We check whether activation steering, which adds vectors to a model's internal activations during generation without changing its weights, can improve compliance beyond prompting alone. Since most scenarios involve several values at once, we focus on multi-value steering. A simple way to build these vectors is to contrast responses that follow all values relevant to a scenario with responses that violate them. However, this gives nearly identical vectors for different values: they capture a general compliance signal rather than individual values, which limits their use when combining several values. We propose Value-Isolating Activation Steering (VIAS), which contrasts responses to the same scenario under two prompts, one listing all relevant values and one omitting a single target value. We keep a pair only if the target value changes from compliant to non-compliant and both responses still meet a minimum compliance level on the other values. We evaluate VIAS on PROTECT, a workplace compliance dataset where each scenario is tagged with relevant values from a 21-value taxonomy. VIAS reduces overlap between value vectors, making them better suited for multi-value steering, although this alone does not lead to independent control over each value. Combined with prompting, VIAS complies with every tagged value on 69.6% of scenarios for Qwen2.5-7B, 75.2% for Mistral-7B, and 72.6% for Phi-3.5-mini, outperforming prompting alone for all three models.

## 14. Mass Scaling Laws for Energy-Optimal Horizontal Multirotor Flight

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04748v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04748v1)

**作者:** Ayush Patnaik, Nicolas Michel, Leonard Baursfeld, Davide Scaramuzza, Xinfan Lin

**分类:** cs.RO, eess.SY

**摘要:**

Multicopters are a type of aerial robot seeing extensive real-world applications but with range and endurance as critical bottlenecks. Optimization of its energy efficiency through energy-aware flight planning (e.g. cruise speed-scheduling) has hence been an important topic. The mass of the multicopter is an important factor to consider, as it has a significant impact on efficiency, and many common applications are subject to frequent mass variation due to payload change. This work derives closed-form scaling relationships between the energy-optimal steady horizontal flight behavior of a multicopter and its vehicle mass, and validates them experimentally on two multirotor platforms. Specifically, for mass-only variation, i.e., when payload changes the vehicle mass while the airframe geometry, aerodynamic characteristics, and rotor configuration remain unchanged, we show through a combination of numerical simulation, theoretical derivation, and experimental validation that (1) the energy-optimal velocity is proportional to the square root of vehicle mass; (2) the energy-optimal pitch angle is constant with respect to mass; and (3) the optimal energy per meter is proportional to mass. The analysis shows that these three relationships arise from a common mass-similarity property of the steady-state flight and rotor-aerodynamic equations. Furthermore, the obtained scaling relationships enable simple yet effective cruise-speed planning to optimize energy efficiency and extend the range and endurance of the multicopter.

## 19. SPOD: Recovering Suppressed Temporal Evidence in Video-LLMs via Single-Pass Orthogonal Decomposition

**arXiv ID:** [2611.04728v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04728v1)

**作者:** Yangguang Lin, Quan Fang, Xiaoyan Liu, Zefan Xu

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) have achieved strong performance on many video understanding tasks, yet remain vulnerable to temporal hallucination in time-sensitive reasoning scenarios such as scene transition understanding and event ordering. Existing solutions either require costly retraining or introduce inefficient multi-pass inference and external supervision at test time. In this work, we show that temporal evidence is already encoded in Video-LLM hidden representations but is not fully exploited during standard inference. Motivated by this observation, we propose Single-Pass Orthogonal Decomposition (SPOD), a training-free framework that recovers suppressed temporal evidence through analytical hidden-state manipulation in a single forward pass. SPOD consists of two components: Frame Distinctiveness Analysis (FDA), which identifies salient frames from middle-layer hidden-state geometry, and Temporal Delta Subspace (TDS), which constructs orthogonal temporal and prior subspaces from weighted inter-frame hidden-state differences.
