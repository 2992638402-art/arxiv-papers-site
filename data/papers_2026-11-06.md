## 1. PBPO: Prefix-Block Policy Optimization for Diffusion Language Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07783v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07783v1)

**作者:** Katherine Tieu, Dongqi Fu, Si Zhang, Hanqing Zeng, Tianxin Wei et al.

**分类:** cs.LG

**摘要:**

Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) commonly decode responses by proceeding autoregressively across blocks while iteratively denoising multiple tokens within each block. This structure makes block-level policy optimization a natural adaptation of autoregressive policy optimization. However, the exact likelihood of a completed block is intractable because it marginalizes over the intermediate states and reveal orders of the denoising process. To realize the block-level formulation, we construct a prefix-block state and use a single model evaluation to define a factorized one-step surrogate for the block likelihood. The resulting block surrogate ratio is a product of its token surrogate ratios, allowing variation at individual positions to propagate across the entire block before clipping. We therefore introduce Prefix-Block Policy Optimization (PBPO), which preserves block-level conditioning but applies surrogate importance weighting and clipping separately to each token. PBPO further diversifies repeated updates by resampling corrupted views of the prompt and revealed prefix, a design motivated by masked-dLLM pre-training. Under our analysis assumptions, block-product weighting amplifies the variance of gradient contributions relative to token-local weighting. Across two LLaDA-family initializations, six benchmarks, and three generation lengths, PBPO achieves competitive performance against existing dLLM RL methods.

## 2. Learning Robotic World Models with Semantic and Visual Feedback

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07850v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07850v1)

**作者:** Hao Wu, Yuqi Li, Yizhou Zhao, Kun Wang, Xiao Luo et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Robot world models predict action-conditioned future visual states to support robotic planning and decision making. However, visual reconstruction does not fully capture task progress, semantic evaluation alone provides limited constraints on fine-grained visual errors, and homogeneous predictions limit comparative feedback. We present RoboImagine, a framework for post-training autoregressive robotic world models through candidate-future comparisons with semantic and visual feedback. Diverse search promotes diversity among candidate trajectories under shared visual observations, action sequences, and task instructions. A frozen multimodal evaluator provides task-relevant semantic preferences through pairwise video comparisons in both presentation orders, complemented by pixel- and perceptual-level visual consistency feedback that constrains local structural and appearance errors. These signals drive policy optimization through group-relative advantages. Fixed initial context and branch-specific rolling histories support long-sequence generation with bounded context and consistent conditioning between candidate generation and policy optimization. Experiments with separately trained models on RT-1, BridgeV2, and RoboCasa cover real-world and simulated manipulation. On RT-1, under matched initialization, update counts, and evaluation protocols, RoboImagine improves PSNR by 1.26 dB and reduces LPIPS by 11.93% relative to continued supervised fine-tuning. Ablations show that diverse search improves prediction quality and that combined semantic and visual feedback yields higher external multimodal evaluation scores than visual feedback alone while maintaining comparable visual fidelity. Rolling histories further improve long-horizon prediction. These results support improving visual fidelity, task relevance, and long-horizon prediction through candidate comparison and complementary feedback.

## 3. TactileLab: Sim-to-Real Dexterous Robot Manipulation with Efficient Shear-Sensitive Tactile Simulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07881v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07881v1)

**作者:** Yijiong Lin, Wenhao He, Toby Jack, Max Yang, Haoran Li et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Robotic simulators that represent shear within tactile sensing are becoming increasingly important for contact-rich manipulation. However, simulating contact shear with high-fidelity deformable or soft-body simulation is computationally demanding, limiting scalability to large-scale reinforcement learning. Here we introduce \textit{TactileLab}, a scalable multimodal tactile simulation framework built on IsaacLab, with a particular focus on a new optical-flow-based tactile representation termed \textit{simulated tactile flow}. Instead of relying on soft-body physics to model shear deformation, TactileLab uses rigid-body simulation to efficiently estimate dense tangential motion at the contact interface, producing tactile flow fields that encode shear-relevant contact dynamics. This flow field complements geometric features from a contact depth field. We further introduce a novel policy architecture for jointly processing long-horizon proprioceptive signals and rich tactile observations. Across multiple contact-rich tasks, policies trained using simulated tactile flow learn efficiently at scale and exhibit emergent contact-aware behaviours. To facilitate sim-to-real policy deployment, we then propose a novel real-to-sim tactile translation method that maps real tactile image streams to simulated tactile flow and depth fields, together with a new tactile data collection method, and validate it across diverse tactile sensor designs. Using this pipeline, we demonstrate real-world deployment on tasks encompassing dual-arm and dexterous in-hand manipulation. Overall, TactileLab provides an end-to-end sim-to-real framework for scalable tactile policy learning, enabling diverse shear-sensitive manipulation across heterogeneous tactile robotic systems.

## 4. ZeroRAG: Adaptive Semantic Peer Routing for Decentralized Retrieval-Augmented Generation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07928v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07928v1)

**作者:** Petru Neague, Quinten Stokkink, Martijn de Vos, Rafael Pires, Mathis Randl et al.

**分类:** cs.DC, cs.AI, cs.IR, cs.NI

**摘要:**

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a standard approach to enhance the reliability of large language model (LLM) outputs by retrieving documents relevant to a query and supplying them in the LLM prompt. Today's RAG systems collect, index, and serve these documents from centralized stores. In practice, however, documents are owned by organizations, devices, and individuals who cannot or will not surrender custody of them to a central party, e.g., because of privacy, copyright or regulatory compliance. Existing modern decentralized retrieval techniques either reintroduce a coordinating authority, or rely on a global data structure that requires continuous network-wide coordination (e.g., DHTs). We present ZeroRAG, a decentralized routing layer for document retrieval in RAG that performs semantic query routing across an overlay of peers with- out global data structures or centralized orchestration. Documents remain with the peers that own them, and each peer keeps only lightweight local state: a bounded set of neighbor advertisements and a cache of recently routed queries. Using these, ZeroRAG steers each query through a best-first walk that progressively rewires the overlay toward semantically relevant neighbors, so recall improves over time as the network self-organizes. We evaluate ZeroRAG using three real-world workloads and two decentralized baselines. We find that ZeroRAG outperforms baselines in terms of recall and required network messages, approaches the recall and LLM answer accuracy of a centralized document store, self-heals after catastrophic peer failures, and scales to 100 000 peers.

## 5. EDGAR: Efficient Diffusion-Driven Gaussian Avatar Refinement via Synthetic Failure

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07909v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07909v1)

**作者:** Kwan Yun, Minhyeok Bang, Colette Suhjung Koo, Junyong Noh

**分类:** cs.GR, cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.MM

**摘要:**

Recent advances in Gaussian splatting have enabled efficient and photorealistic head avatar reconstruction, yet monocular video-based or image-based methods still suffer from under-constrained geometry and texture, leading to artifacts such as blurring, stretching, and temporal instability. We present EDGAR (Efficient Diffusion-Driven Gaussian Avatar Refinement), a conditional diffusion framework designed to enhance the quality of Gaussian avatars by correcting artifacts while preserving identity fidelity. To train EDGAR, we construct EDGAR-SET, a hybrid triplet dataset composed of real-captured and synthetic-rendered samples, which simulates realistic Gaussian avatar failure cases by pairing degraded renderings with ground-truth and reference images. EDGAR refines degraded frames through a fusion block that injects identity features extracted by the self-supervised Sapiens model into a one-step diffusion backbone, enabling effective artifact removal without sacrificing efficiency. Our EDGAR model incorporates a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture to address diverse degradation types and severity levels. The refined multi-view renderings are subsequently used as pseudo-targets to optimize a single underlying Gaussian avatar, so that the final avatar can be rendered without applying EDGAR to every output frame. Experiments on three different Gaussian avatar reconstruction methods, each evaluated under three different settings, show improvements in both perceptual and reconstruction measures over the raw outputs and an SDEdit-style refinement baseline. In addition, cross-view evaluation demonstrates improved calibrated consistency, while tests under large yaw and open-mouth expressions verify robustness under extreme settings. These results collectively indicate a useful operating regime for recoverable appearance artifacts. The code is available at https://kwanyun.github.io/edgar/

## 6. Do Recommenders Need to Think in Words? Latent Reasoning for Efficient LLM-Based Recommendation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07866v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07866v1)

**作者:** Hongyang Su, Beibei Kong, Lei Cheng, Chengxiang Zhuo, Zang Li

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

LLM-based recommender systems increasingly generate chain-of-thought (CoT) text before each item prediction to activate the LLM's reasoning capacity, costing tens to hundreds of extra tokens per recommendation at inference. We ask whether inference-time reasoning artifacts (textual or latent) can be replaced by training-time supervision alone, without loss of accuracy. We propose LatentRec, a framework for LLM-based generative recommendation that replaces inference-time reasoning artifacts with training-time per-step head supervision. LatentRec has three components. A 2.5M-parameter Continuous Thought Module iterates K latent reasoning steps over the LLM's hidden state during training. Soft Token Selection bridges the ~14x norm gap between hidden states and input embeddings. A Per-step Reasoning Loss (PRL) supervises every intermediate state through the LLM's prediction head against the first token of the target Semantic ID. The trained LLM retains this accuracy without the module at inference, so the module can be discarded at deployment. On three Amazon benchmarks, LatentRec improves HR@10 by 4.0-5.0% (relative) over a CoT-distilled baseline at 18.3x lower latency (batch=1). Per-step training supervision consistently improves accuracy across three datasets, paid once at training rather than at every recommendation.

## 8. Circe: Training-Free LLM Self-Evaluation via Component Interaction Consistency

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07948v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07948v1)

**作者:** Shuyi Miao, Zheng Li, Xu Xie, Linrui Xu, Fei Shen et al.

**分类:** cs.AI

**摘要:**

As large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in real-world applications, evaluating the correctness of their responses is crucial for reliable use. However, such evaluation is challenging because reference answers are rarely available in practice. Existing reference-free methods mainly rely on output signals, which may assign high scores to incorrect responses and thus do not necessarily reflect correctness. This motivates exploring the model's internal computation for complementary correctness signals. Recent studies exploit internal states, but they mainly analyze hidden-state trajectories across layers, which record only the combined updates of attention and feed-forward networks (FFNs) and overlook how these components interact. We therefore propose Circe, a training-free self-evaluation method that assesses a response using both internal and external signals, without reference answers or repeated sampling. For internal signals, we first introduce component interaction consistency (CIC), which measures the directional alignment between attention updates in earlier layers and attention-induced FFN output changes in later layers. We further incorporate representation geometry (RG) to characterize normalized displacement and angular changes along layerwise hidden-state trajectories. For the external signal, we convert the mean negative log-likelihood of generated tokens into a bounded likelihood confidence (LC) score. Finally, we fuse CIC, RG, and LC into a unified correctness score. Experiments on five LLMs and six benchmarks across four domains show that our method distinguishes correct from incorrect responses more effectively than existing baselines, and generalizes to multilingual, multimodal, and open-world settings.

## 14. Rotation Is Token-Blind: A Structural Criterion for Post-Training Quantization of Feed-Forward Geometry Transformers

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07955v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07955v1)

**作者:** Huanfei He, Bohao Zhang, Jiaxin Zhang, Wei Sui, John See et al.

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Feed-forward 3D models use Transformers to predict camera poses, depth, and structure in one pass, avoiding iterative multi-view optimization. Long sequences and deep attention incur high compute and memory costs, motivating Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) for deployment. Existing PTQ methods mainly suppress channel-wise outliers by smoothing or orthogonal rotation. Geometry Transformers introduce a different challenge: heterogeneous token types share one activation quantization grid. A few special tokens may exert high leverage over global outputs and be updated by only a subset of blocks. Token-blind transforms, which apply the same transformation to every token, reduce overall quantization error but cannot remove differentiated degradation across token types. We propose QMapAnything, a structure-aware PTQ framework. We cast floating-point-preserving rotation and smoothing as a token-blind reparameterization group, and prove that they yield common-mode gains while leaving relative token SNR unchanged. We introduce a statistic $Γ$ that combines a special token's quantization disadvantage and output leverage to predict whether differentiated bias correction is needed beyond rotation. Experiments on geometric benchmarks show that W4A8 closely matches full-precision performance. Under W8A8, peak memory falls by $2.2$--$3.1\times$ versus FP32, with up to $3.1\times$ speedup on real hardware. These results provide a principled and efficient approach to deploying feed-forward 3D reconstruction models in resource-constrained edges.

Beyond MHR, we solve the minimax problem for regular and unrestricted distributions and prove an $O(N^{-2/5})$ learning bound in the regular nonidentifiable regime, given known bounds on the boundary parameters. Experiments on synthetic and eBay data show gains from exploiting MHR and the benefits of the robust rule when parametric models are misspecified.

## 150. Last-Iterate Convergence of Stochastic Gradient Methods for Unbounded Problems

**arXiv ID:** [2611.07334v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07334v1)

**作者:** Kevin Kurian Thomas Vaidyan, Ahmet Alacaoglu, Michael P. Friedlander

**分类:** math.OC, cs.LG, stat.ML

**摘要:**

Last-iterate convergence guarantees for stochastic gradient methods have attracted significant attention as practical implementations typically return the last iterate instead of an average of the iterates. In this paper, we study two approaches for analyzing the last-iterate convergence of proximal and projected stochastic gradient methods without bounded domain or bounded variance assumptions, where black-box variational inference serves as a running example. Under two related assumptions relaxing the bounded variance, we show near-optimal anytime rate $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T})$ for the last iterate. Moreover, when the horizon $T$ is known, we remove the logarithmic factor and obtain the optimal order $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{T})$, matching the order known under bounded variance or bounded domain assumptions.
