## 1. FewStepRL: Fine-tuning Few-step Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2611.08240v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08240v1)

**作者:** Haitao Lin, Minsi Ren, Yang Li, Xingtong Ge, Zheyuan Hu et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a standard post-training method for diffusion models, but most RL objectives roll out the current policy at every iteration, and with a many-step sampler these rollouts typically dominate the cost of training. Few-step models cut this cost, yet they model an average velocity over an interval, whereas existing RL objectives are built for an instantaneous velocity with a many-step sampler. We adapt RL objectives to few-step models systematically in three phases, namely a transformation, a decomposition and an adaptation. The transformation to $x$-space expresses each objective we consider through a common policy prediction, at the clean end by default and at an explicitly marked level for transition or flow-map objectives, so that objectives stated over densities, velocities or rewards become comparable. The decomposition writes the gradient of each of these objectives as the sum of a self-regression toward a reference and a reward augmentation, so that a method reduces, at the gradient level, to a choice of anchors and weights. The adaptation computes each anchor from a few-step model at the end of its transport or along its tangent, with the instantaneous velocity recovered from the network itself or from an auxiliary network. This yields few-step versions of FlowGRPO, AWM, DiffusionNFT, DiffusionOPSD and ReFL in one modular codebase. In our experiments, reward-gradient-based methods are the most effective when the reward is differentiable and its gradient is affordable; otherwise DiffusionNFT-style methods generally reach high rewards in relatively few iterations despite a sometimes higher per-iteration cost. Extra inference steps show no systematic aggregate benefit in our experiments, although selected outputs visibly improve with two to four times as many sampling steps as in training.

## 2. AttentionSwitch: Jailbreak Defense for LLMs via Joint Learning of Sparse Attention-Head Selection and Scaling

**arXiv ID:** [2611.08752v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08752v1)

**作者:** Doniyorkhon Obidov, Honggang Yu, Xiaolong Guo, Kaichen Yang

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CR

**摘要:**

Large language models have achieved remarkable performance across a wide range of tasks, yet they remain susceptible to jailbreak attacks that circumvent safety mechanisms and elicit harmful or unintended outputs. Although safety can be improved through model fine-tuning, such approaches can be computationally expensive and may degrade previously acquired capabilities through catastrophic forgetting. Recent jailbreak defenses have therefore explored targeted interventions on specific model components associated with unsafe behavior. However, existing approaches typically decouple two inherently related decisions: where to intervene and how to modify the selected components. Such sequential optimization may be suboptimal because the optimal intervention location can depend on the intervention applied there, and vice versa. In this work, we introduce AttentionSwitch, the first jailbreak defense to jointly optimize intervention sites and strengths within a frozen LLM. Specifically, AttentionSwitch learns a sparse set of attention heads together with query-conditioned multiplicative scaling factors, allowing the outputs of the selected heads to be either attenuated or amplified without altering the directions of their representations. Across popular language models and jailbreak attacks, AttentionSwitch outperforms existing defenses, with particularly strong results when training data is scarce.

## 3. RL-ATTACK: A Generalized Black-Box Adversarial Attack on Language Models via Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2611.08711v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08711v1)

**作者:** Taikun Chen, Yue Zhang, Wei Qian, Hui Fang

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.CR

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) exhibit vulnerabilities to adversarial perturbations that can induce unintended responses. These vulnerabilities can be identified via intentional attacks and subsequently used to improve model safety. In black-box settings where gradients are inaccessible, existing methods tend to guess adversarial transformations using external knowledge, ignoring the information provided by the target model. To mitigate this, we propose a trainable black-box framework based on reinforcement learning, which learns from the target model to generate tailored adversarial transformations. We demonstrate that our approach achieves lower post-attack accuracy, greater query efficiency and better semantic preservation on both natural language inference and generative Q&amp;A tasks. On natural language inference, it drives post-attack accuracy to 2% within 20 queries. Against generative language models, our trained attacker consistently achieves lower post-attack accuracy than the untrained attacker and wins in 4 out of 6 settings when compared to a strong optimization-based baseline. We further experiment with multiple reward designs and training settings, and we find that no design or setting is consistently dominant. Our code is available at https://github.com/taikunudel/RL-ATTACK.

## 4. s$^3$: Simple Scaling of Swarms via Cooperative Multi-agent RL

**arXiv ID:** [2611.08615v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08615v1)

**作者:** Kunal Jha, Gonçalo Faria, Shuyue Stella Li, Scott Geng, Faeze Brahman et al.

**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Groups can achieve more than individuals in the same amount of time, but only if their members coordinate: they must decide who works on what and what to share. However, this organization in multi-agent systems today usually comes from arbitrary, static harnesses designed a priori without adapting to agent behaviors or task specifications. We ask whether agents can instead learn to organize themselves. We introduce s$^3$, the first multi-agent reinforcement learning method in which agents decide for themselves how long to work alone and when to share findings or read teammates' messages, with no predefined organization or roles. Our s$^3$ agents learn to take different approaches, read each other's work, exhibit emergent roles that complement each other in the swarm, and generalize to bigger teams. We study both coordination, where agents must combine private information, and parallel exploration, where sharing discoveries can make search more efficient. With matched training and inference budgets, s$^3$, swarms evaluated with twice as many agents as in training solve over 25$\times$ more coordination problems than the strongest baseline, and in exploration, communicating agents reduce repeated work by nearly 50% and outperform best-of-N by up to 9.4 percentage points. Rather than being designed in advance, s$^3$ enables organization to emerge from a shared goal. This opens the door to capable systems built from many models that learn to work together.

## 6. Adaptive Scheduling Pipeline for Multi-Instance Asynchronous Reinforcement Learning

**分类:** cs.DC, cs.AI, cs.LG

**摘要:**

We present the Adaptive Scheduling Pipeline (ASP), an offline rollout scheduler for multi-instance asynchronous reinforcement learning (RL) of large language models (LLMs). ASP jointly reduces three sources of rollout underutilization - padding bubbles from intra-minibatch length heterogeneity, skewness bubbles from long-tail outputs, and sorting bubbles from fluctuations in training-queue occupancy induced by length-sorted dispatch - through a single offline preprocessing pass. ASP has since been used in an internal telecom-domain RL post-training workflow, reducing the reported training period from more than three months to about two months and producing a domain-enhanced model for knowledge answering and tool calling.

## 7. ResQAdam: Residual-Preserving Adaptive Optimization for Memory-Efficient Quantization Aware Training

**arXiv ID:** [2611.08924v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08924v1)

**作者:** Liqun Ma, Shengkun Tang, Liangyu Wang, Lin Zhao, Wei Luo et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Quantization-aware training (QAT) is the most reliable way to obtain accurate low-bit large language models (LLMs), but training with AdamW keeps two dense FP32 states for every weight matrix. We find that low-rank memory-efficient optimizers such as GaLore and LDAdam degrade substantially in QAT. A considerable part of the QAT momentum lies outside its top-r singular subspace, and projection-based methods discard this part at every step. We further observe that this residual remains useful when only its sign is kept. Based on these observations, we propose ResQAdam. It keeps a single BF16 momentum, applies Adam only to the r x r coordinates of the principal subspace, and adds a normalized sign update computed from the residual. We evaluate ResQAdam with two from-scratch QAT methods and three QAT fine-tuning pipelines, with weights quantized down to 2 bits. It reduces optimizer-state memory by 71% to 74% relative to AdamW, stays close to AdamW in most settings, and lowers training throughput by less than 2%.

## 11. On the Few-Shot Adaptation of Vision-Language Models: A Few Gradient Signs Are All You Need

**作者:** Nairouz Mrabah, Ismail Ben Ayed, Mahdi S. Hosseini, Eric Granger

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CV

**摘要:**

The limited supervision available for the few-shot adaptation of vision-language models makes gradient estimation particularly noisy. We find that the gradient magnitudes are severely miscalibrated, while the signs of a small top-magnitude subset remain informative. ## 13. T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

**作者:** Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

## 19. Horizon-Coupled Obstacle Guidance for Relative-Action Diffusion Policies

**arXiv ID:** [2611.09340v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09340v1)

**作者:** Guangyuan Wang, Haishan Xia, Zitao Zhang, Kejia Chen, Yuanchen Sun et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Real-world robot deployment requires adapting learned policies to unseen obstacles without collecting new demonstrations or retraining. Existing policy-level guidance often applies obstacle corrections independently at individual horizon steps, which may address local obstacle violations but fail to coordinate the action sequence into a complete, task-directed detour. How obstacle responses should be propagated across the action horizon to coordinate complete, task-directed avoidance motion remains underexplored. We introduce an inferencetime obstacle-guidance framework for pretrained relative-action diffusion policies. It rolls out cumulative relative actions into a workspace trajectory, evaluates obstacle interactions along the trajectory with an SDF-based obstacle model, and propagates each obstacle response to all contributing action increments during denoising.
