## 1. PARLEY: Evaluating LLM Collaboration Through Cooperative Asymmetric Puzzles

**arXiv ID:** [2611.14242v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14242v1)

**作者:** Javier Hernandez, Andrew D. Wilson

**分类:** cs.MA

**摘要:**

As large language models power autonomous agents, understanding how they collaborate and where they fail becomes critical. We introduce PARLEY, an extensible platform for studying LLM agent collaboration through four asymmetric puzzle tasks grounded in cognitive psychology (referential communication, joint coordination, perspective-taking, and spatial reasoning). GPT-4o agent pairs succeeded in 60.5% of conversations (N=400, 100 per task), with task-level variation (34-80%) reflecting distinct collaborative demands. From these conversations, we derived the data-driven PAC taxonomy: six failure types organized into Perception (visual interpretation and spatial reasoning), Adaptation (progress tracking and feedback integration), and Coordination (verification and grounding). Failure to incorporate external feedback was a critical indicator of unsuccessful collaboration, with agents repeatedly applying unsuccessful strategies. We also show that a taxonomy-guided intervention (N=200) improved average task success to 70.5%. These results demonstrate how PARLEY can be used to derive meaningful, data-driven insights that can in turn lead to improved agentic collaboration.

## 2. Ropedia Xperience 2: A Gold Standard for Scaling Physical AI Environments and Behaviors

**arXiv ID:** [2611.17060v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17060v1)

**作者:** Hao Li, Fangzhou Hong, Zhaoxi Chen, Hui En Pang, Kairui Hu et al.

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Large foundation models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 have recently been shown to control robots directly from visual observations. They nevertheless remain brittle on long-horizon, contact-rich tasks in real-world environments. Further progress depends on two complementary resources: demonstrations of human behavior that supervise how tasks are performed, and interactive environments in which policies can be rolled out, evaluated, and improved in closed loop. Language models have advanced along well-characterized scaling laws; the corresponding question for physical AI is how capability scales with environments and behaviors, which first requires identifying which data to scale and how to produce and reuse such data continuously. To address this question, we introduce Ropedia Xperience 2, comprising 1,589 released metric-scale 3D scans and a collection of 4,230 egocentric recordings of 74 tasks (131 hours). We further show that each capture can be repurposed for robot learning: scenes are converted into physically interactive simulation environments, human motion is retargeted to whole-body robot trajectories, and additional robot behaviors are obtained through virtual teleoperation and automated long-horizon trajectory generation. We propose this joint capture of environments and behaviors as a gold standard for physical AI data collection, laying a data foundation for establishing scaling laws over environments and behaviors.

## 5. RE-PRESENT: Evidence Triangulation across Alternative Candidate-Space Views for Efficient LLM Ensemble Inference

**arXiv ID:** [2611.14780v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14780v1)

**作者:** Juming Xiong, Weixin Liu, Kevin Guo, Congning Ni, Chongyu Qu et al.

**分类:** cs.MA

**摘要:**

Heterogeneous large language models (LLMs) exhibit complementary strengths, motivating routing and multi-model inference methods that seek to identify reliable predictions for each input. However, exploiting this complementarity remains challenging even when a correct prediction is available within the ensemble. We investigate this challenge across heterogeneous LLM ensembles and find that a simple single-round aggregation rule is highly competitive with learned routing methods, yet substantial oracle headroom remains. Further analysis shows that the evaluated single-round response signals provide limited discriminative power for identifying reliable predictions. In contrast, controlled re-presentations of the same question elicit complementary evidence about prediction reliability. Motivated by these findings, we introduce RE-PRESENT, a training-free framework that selectively re-presents disputed questions and directly aggregates candidate-level evidence across alternative views. Across seven multiple-choice benchmarks and two heterogeneous model ensembles, RE-PRESENT improves over strong single-round aggregation while offering a favorable accuracy--cost trade-off compared with self-consistency and multi-model debate.

## 6. Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards

**arXiv ID:** [2611.17287v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17287v1)

**作者:** Jiacheng Xu, Shuo He, Fuxiang Zhang, Chaojie Wang, Bo An

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) typically relies on a verifier that can reliably judge whether sampled solutions are correct. However, in many realistic settings, complete verification is generally unavailable, while cheap checks can only falsify failures. We formalize this regime as one-sided verifiability (OSV): verified negatives are reliable counterexamples, but verified positives are merely non-falsified candidates and may contain many false positives. This asymmetry makes standard RLVR unreliable, because reinforcing verified positives can directly reward shortcuts that pass partial checks. To address this issue, we propose Negative-Only Policy Optimization (NOPO), which treats verified positives as unlabeled and updates the policy only by suppressing trusted verified negatives. Specifically, NOPO combines a reference-relative negative suppression loss with a group-level adapter that scales each update by the proportion of non-falsified responses. Across unit-test, constraint-check, and LLM self-verifiers, NOPO improves pass@k over baselines on code, logic, and math benchmarks when oracle rewards are unavailable. These gains persist under inference-time scaling and cross-benchmark transfer.

## 8. SALA: Semantically Aligned Latent Actions Enable Dual-Axis Scaling of Embodied Foundation Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.17110v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17110v1)

**作者:** Jiachun Jin, Zetong Zhou, Haonan Wang, Xiao Yang, Jun Zhu et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Embodied foundation models hinge on diverse robot interaction data, which are costly to collect. Egocentric human videos offer a complementary source of experience, and latent action models (LAMs) provide a popular means of leveraging these videos for policy supervision. Compared with world-action models (WAMs), LAMs can offer greater training efficiency and potentially lower inference costs by avoiding explicit pixel prediction during policy learning and execution. However, most existing LAMs learn latent actions solely through video reconstruction, which can cause their representations to cluster by visual appearance rather than by the underlying actions. As a result, they struggle to align human actions with their robot counterparts. To bridge the gap, we introduce SALA, which emphasizes establishing the semantic correspondence for latent actions. Without requiring paired data, we use the language descriptions of unpaired human and robot clips to estimate semantic similarities and supervise cross-embodiment correspondence. Specifically, we translate these similarities into soft neighborhood structures that guide human latent actions toward semantically related robot latents, while robot action reconstruction grounds the latent space in control. The resulting shared latent action space provides a unified supervision signal for training an embodied foundation model on both human and robot videos. Notably, latent action supervision enables scaling along two axes: (1) pre-training on increasing amounts of general human video consistently reduces robot action prediction error on downstream tasks, and (2) post-training on increasing amounts of task-specific human video substantially improves real-world robot task success rates.

## 9. Programming Quantum Circuit Parameters with Large Language Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.17246v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17246v1)

**作者:** Tiago de Souza Farias, Caio Moraes Porto, Matheus da Silva Fonseca, Nicolas Armando Cabrera Carpi, Anderson de Araujo Oliveira et al.

**分类:** quant-ph, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) have recently demonstrated the ability to generate parameters for variational quantum circuits without conventional optimization. However, the conditions under which this approach is effective remain unclear. Here, we investigate a frozen, general-purpose LLM that generates complete circuit parameter vectors from symbolic circuit and Hamiltonian descriptions, with optional iterative energy feedback. We evaluate the approach on general random Hamiltonians, structured parent Hamiltonians, and molecular systems. For general Hamiltonians, LLM-generated parameters exhibit larger energy errors than conventional Adam optimization, while additional feedback generally provides limited improvement. In contrast, for structured Hamiltonians, the LLM can generate promising low-energy parameter configurations, suggesting that identifiable physical and mathematical structure facilitates parameter inference. We also show that direct LLM predictions of minimum energies can violate the Hamiltonian spectral lower bound, whereas generated circuit parameters allow independent evaluation of physically realizable energy expectation values. Finally, we investigate blind circuit interpretation and find that the LLM can predict measurement outcomes even when it fails to identify the underlying quantum algorithm. These findings highlight the role of problem structure, the limitations of iterative LLM optimization, and the importance of independently verifiable predictions in quantum parameter programming.

**摘要:**

**作者:** David R. Martin, Brooks A. Butler, Scott Nivison, Magnus Egerstedt, Mohammad Abdullah Al Faruque et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Multi-robot systems face the challenge of efficiently allocating teams of heterogeneous robots to tasks. The task allocation problem is complicated by collaborative interactions between robots where teams of robots develop emergent capabilities that enable them to complete tasks that would be inefficient or impossible for individual robots. This approach partitions the computationally intractable global optimization problem into smaller, tractable subproblems by iteratively forming clusters of robots and tasks, then optimizing assignments within each cluster. ## 16. Before AI Agents Negotiate for Us: A Competitive Tournament of Rule-Based, Search, Optimization, Learned, and Language-Model Negotiators Under Scarcity

**arXiv ID:** [2611.15838v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15838v1)

**作者:** Eduardo de Avila-Armenta, Raúl Alejandro Aquino-Ibarra, Jorge Octavio Nicolás-Díaz, Umberto Leon-Dominguez

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.GT, cs.LG, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Automated bilateral negotiation underlies two-sided matching markets such as freight brokering, where many shippers and carriers negotiate price before a matching mechanism decides who is paired with whom. Prior strategy comparisons typically use isolated one-on-one duels or a single fixed market, so they cannot show how a strategy behaves against several live rivals under a changing balance of supply and demand. We built nine negotiation architectures spanning rule-based, search-based, optimization-based, imitation-learning, and reinforcement-learning families, and ran all nine simultaneously on both sides of a Gale-Shapley matching market under a balanced condition and two scarcity conditions rotated across all 36 exclusion pairs. We also tested nine large language models (LLMs), from 1 to 72 billion parameters, under the same protocol: they lost significantly to the two strongest engines (OPT2 and ML2) in 32 of 36 comparisons and won none. These results show that isolated or single-condition evaluation can hide failure modes that a many-sided, scarcity-varying, statistically corrected tournament exposes directly.

## 17. GHA-SmellFix: Taking Action with Agentic LLMs for Detecting and Refactoring GitHub Actions Workflow Smells

Continuous integration and continuous delivery workflows are key artifacts in software projects. In GitHub Actions, they encode build, test, release, deployment, and security automation in YAML. ## 18. When and How SignSGD Outperforms SGD: A Functional Scaling Law Perspective

**arXiv ID:** [2611.16560v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16560v1)

**作者:** Zilin Wang, Binghui Li, Jia-Nan Wang, Lean Wang, Jinbo Wang et al.

**分类:** stat.ML, cs.LG

**摘要:**

We study how the sign operator interacts with gradient noise to shape the learning dynamics and scaling behavior of sign-based optimizers. In power-law kernel regression with diagonal features, we establish a functional scaling law (FSL) for SignSGD under general learning rate schedules. The FSL describes the learning dynamics through two processes: signal learning and noise forgetting. The key mechanism is noise-induced preconditioning: gradient noise smooths the sign operator, inducing an effective preconditioned SGD. Noise-curvature alignment enables this preconditioning to transform the effective Hessian from $\mathbf{H}$ to $\mathbf{H}^{1/2}$. The resulting dynamics exhibit faster signal learning but slower noise forgetting than SGD. This tradeoff yields a regime-dependent comparison with SGD: (i) SignSGD achieves improved data efficiency in the hard-task regime; (ii) both optimizers achieve the same data efficiency in the easy-task regime, but SignSGD maintains this efficiency at larger batch sizes, requiring fewer optimization steps for a fixed data budget. Power-law kernel regression experiments support the proposed mechanism and predicted scaling behavior. LLM pre-training experiments further suggest that the SignSGD FSL better captures the batch-size dependence of loss dynamics for sign-based optimizers than the original SGD FSL.

## 19. Goal Encoding in LLMs

**arXiv ID:** [2611.17297v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17297v1)

**作者:** Arnab Sen Sharma, Eric Todd, Sheridan Feucht, Andy Arditi, Rohit Gandikota et al.

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG

**摘要:**

We investigate whether and how LLMs encode goals: internal neural representations that precede and guide action, and that are flexible enough to be realized through many different execution paths. We construct a coding setting with multiple sub-tasks that allows us to evaluate goal-seeking behavior and the selection of sub-goals. In this setting, we localize an encoding of the next sub-goal in the outputs of a sparse set of attention heads. The same heads can be used to decode the LM's future actions in natural language (e.g., "I'm checking if a word is a palindrome"). We verify that this encoding is causal for next action and is plastic: transplanting it into a different environment, e.g., another programming language, triggers the same operation realized with a different sequence of tokens. The LM also sustains the encoding throughout the action's execution, which is puzzling because a transformer LM without recurrence cannot carry a state forward for more tokens than it has layers left. We propose that it does not need to. Under a non-recurrent goal-seeking (NRGS) account, the LM frequently re-reads the goal from the task specification, compares it with the progress visible in the growing context, and pursues what remains. We test the predictions of this account with targeted causal interventions.

## 20. Parameter-Efficient Conciseness Adaptation: Differentiable Length Control for Concise Clinical Reasoning in Smaller Language Models

**摘要:**

Smaller language models (SLMs) make it feasible to deploy clinical natural language processing where large proprietary models are too costly, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning allows them to be specialised on commodity hardware.
