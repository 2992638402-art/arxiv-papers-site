## 1. Macro Control and Capability Evaluation of Black-Box World Models: A Two-Regime Framework for LLM-Agent Markets

**arXiv ID:** [2611.19311v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19311v1)

**作者:** Zhiyang Niu, Chuanjie Li, Huayuan Shen, Mengmeng Zhao, Jiajia Zhang

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Large language model (LLM) agents can be organized into multi-agent markets that act as world models, mapping macro information to emergent macro dynamics while the evaluator observes only macro inputs and outputs. This paper separates a model's capability into two stages that a single aggregate score cannot separate: information simulation, from macro information to micro behavior, and macro emergence, from micro behavior to the macro outcome. We introduce a soft macro-control method that announces a public reference path day by day without touching individual decisions, orders, or matching, and a two-regime evaluation framework that measures both stages from macro observations alone. In the reference-only regime the symmetric true and flipped references share a common level R, which measures realization under the announced reference, while their paired gap B measures structural bias; in the information-injection regime external information dilutes the reference, so only the true-flipped gap Delta remains interpretable. On five single-index LLM-agent markets, announcing a target path shifts the realized path toward the announced target on both announced references under both information conditions. Relative to the no-reference path, the tracking score is higher by 0.306 and 0.415 without news and by 0.183 and 0.329 with news; within the stock/cash scan structural bias is lowest at 70/30 (B=0.042); realization peaks at a 3:7 technical share (R=0.894 versus 0.358 for technical-only); and Delta is 0.0341 with fundamental data and 0.0487 with news. Both stages use the same two observable scores, each read in the regime where it is interpretable.

## 2. FRAAC: A French Multimodal Large Language Model and Dataset forPictogram-Based Augmentative and Alternative Communication

**arXiv ID:** [2611.20094v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20094v1)

**作者:** Alexandre Audibert, François Portet, Benoit Favre, Maja Jonck Hjuler

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Pictograms are a core modality in augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), yet recent multimodal large language models (MLLMs) cannot reliably interpret or produce pictogram sequences. Two obstacles explain this gap: the absence of a standardized pictogram grammar and the scarcity of parallel text-pictogram data. We introduce Retrieval-Augmented Pictogram Selection (RAPS), an annotation method that refines a rule-based transcription system's output with LLM-based concept extraction and retrieval over a pictogram vocabulary, handling cases where hand-written rules fail (named entities, numbers, polysemous words) without requiring new rules. Applied to French, RAPS yields a synthetic corpus of 1.4 million parallel pairs, of which 300k are publicly released. In a human ranking study where annotators order four pictogram transcriptions of the same sentence, RAPS outperforms the rule-based state of the art. Moreover, we show that standard reference-based metrics separate systems of clearly different quality but fail to rank strong systems against each other. Finally, we build an instruction-tuned model that supports interaction in several AAC modalities: it accepts pictogram or text input and answers in pictograms, speech, or text.

## 3. Risk-certified prompt optimization for LLM-as-a-judge guardrails

**arXiv ID:** [2611.20156v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20156v1)

**作者:** Hussein Jawad, Alexandre Landeau, Guy Fleury Iradukunda, Paul Beaumont, Théodore Rezel et al.

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.LG

**摘要:**

LLM-as-a-judge guardrails are often controlled primarily through system prompts, yet standard prompt optimization methods target average performance rather than explicit safety constraints. This paper studies system-prompt selection as a constrained optimization problem: maximize benign pass rate while keeping the missed-attack rate below a user-specified level α with finite-sample statistical control. The framework combines LLM-based judging, automatic prompt generation, and distribution-free risk certification. This work instantiates it in two procedures: CPS and CRISP. CPS generates a fixed pool of prompts from training data, then certifies candidates on a held-out evaluation set using simultaneous upper confidence bounds and selects the certified prompt with the highest benign utility. CRISP instead integrates risk feedback into an adaptive search, using hard example-guided rewriting and a reusable-holdout interface to steer generation toward the feasible utility frontier. Experiments on four datasets covering prompt injection, system-prompt leakage, and sensitive-information disclosure show that risk-certified selection consistently respects the target missed-attack level on heldout tests, while utility improves as the permitted risk is relaxed. CRISP is especially useful when fixed-pool search fails to produce certifiable high-utility candidates.

## 7. PlanDiv: Test-Time Scaling with Latent Plans for Vision-Language-Action Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.20010v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20010v1)

**作者:** Hanbo Zhang, Lianlei Shan, Liuhaichen Yang, Zezhi Tang

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Test-time scaling improves vision-language-action (VLA) policies by drawing several action candidates and executing the best one. We find that its returns diminish quickly when the candidates are resampled from one decoder: they differ by noise rather than by plan, and for each of three resampling sources, going from four to eight candidates adds only 0.06-0.07 to the average number of completed tasks on CALVIN. We introduce PlanDiv, an action head that makes the candidates differ by plan: a lightweight planner proposes K latent plan hypotheses, a shared masked-diffusion executor decodes one action chunk per plan from a single backbone pass, and the policy executes the chunk that its own likelihood ranks highest. The same-plan baseline is PlanDiv's own checkpoint with the plan held fixed, so this gap comes from the plans, not from drawing more samples. On LIBERO-Long, four plans remove 40% of single-candidate failures, against at most 21% for matched resampling. On a real Franka arm the gain appears where a task admits more than one solution: PlanDiv is on par with a single-output OpenVLA-OFT+ baseline on a one-solution task and succeeds in 51 of 54 trials against 40 of 54 on two tasks with two solutions.

## 8. The novelty exposed: ablation, reproducibility and transparency in 40 technical reports of open-weight large language models (LLMs)

**作者:** Bahaeddin Türkoğlu

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG

**摘要:**

In 2025 alone, 48 new large language model (LLM) architectures were added to the Hugging Face transformers library, and many of them arrive with a technical report that presents the model as new. What is actually new in these models, and is it tested? We put this question to LLM technical reports. We look at three things: what the reference code shows about how new open-weight architectures differ from earlier ones; what 40 randomly sampled technical reports claim as new and whether they support it with ablation studies; and what they disclose for reproducibility and transparency. ## 9. DoF-Query Transformer for Adaptive Fusion of Egocentric Vision and Forearm HD-sEMG in Continuous Hand Kinematics

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.HC, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Continuous and accurate estimation of human hand kinematics is important not only for intuitive human-machine interaction, but also for capturing fine-grained human manipulation demonstrations that can provide action supervision for embodied-intelligence datasets and dexterous robot learning. We present a finger-unencumbered multimodal framework for continuous regression of 20 hand joint angles from egocentric RGB images and 128-channel forearm high-density surface electromyography (HD-sEMG). **分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

However, maintaining cross-view consistency requires information exchange across viewpoints, and dense cross-view attention becomes computationally expensive as the number of views and spatial resolution increase. ## 11. Bellman Residual Bracketing: Resolving the Sample Complexity of Discounted and Average-Reward RL

**arXiv ID:** [2611.19566v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19566v1)

**作者:** Matthew Zurek, Yudong Chen

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.IT, math.OC, math.PR, stat.ML

**摘要:**

We study the sample complexity of the empirical planning or plug-in approach for reinforcement learning (RL) with a generative model. We show the discounted plug-in approach finds an $\varepsilon$-optimal policy with $O(SA (1-γ)^{-3} \varepsilon^{-2} \log(SA/δ))$ samples, where $S, A, γ, δ$ are the state and action sizes, discount factor, and failure probability, respectively. Our main technical contribution is a novel Bellman residual bracketing technique which improves the efficiency of prior absorbing-MDP-based concentration analyses.

## 13. Robotic Reward Models in the Foundation-Model Era

**arXiv ID:** [2611.20741v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20741v1)

**作者:** Guoqiang Liang, Yiqi Lin, Shangchen Zhou, Wenzheng Zeng, Yanzhe Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Robotic reward models (RRMs) use foundation models to replace per-task reward engineering with reusable, task-conditioned evaluation. Yet under one name, their outputs answer different questions, from whether a task succeeded to which execution is better. This survey organizes 114 methods into five semantic families by the question their native outputs answer: Outcome, Absolute Progress, Relative, Temporal Value, and Hybrid. Within this taxonomy, we review how model design determines what an evaluator can judge, how raw experience becomes supervision, and how existing evaluation practice tests the result. How much fidelity a reward model needs therefore depends on the decision it drives. We outline directions from real-failure supervision to metrics that separate task state from chronology, and open-source RRM-Hub, a toolbox in which 14 existing RRMs can be readily applied and new evaluators tested on the same capabilities.

## 14. DiffWAM: Learning World Action Model from Consecutive Visual Difference

**作者:** Xinheng Zhang, Haoyu Xiong, Chao Jing, Xiaohan Zhang, Linshan Li et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as an important paradigm in robot learning, jointly predicting future scene states and robot actions from current observations and instructions. However, future states entangle task-related changes with large amounts of task-irrelevant content, such as static backgrounds, which weakens the model's ability to capture changes relevant to task execution. To address this, we propose Difference World Action Model (DiffWAM), a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework that enhances world representations by jointly learning future-state prediction and consecutive visual difference prediction. Specifically, it attains average success rates of 98.8%, 92.65%, and 88.3%, outperforming the state-of-the-art baseline (i.e., FastWAM) by 1.75, 1.55, and 15.0 percentage points, respectively.

## 17. QASK-Edit: Adaptive Sparse Attention Across Queries and Key Sources for Efficient Video Editing

**作者:** Hengrui Liu, Wenbo Xu, Ziqi Sheng, Wei lu

**摘要:**

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have achieved strong performance in generation based video editing, but suffer from high inference latency due to the long token sequences formed by conditioning inputs and generated videos. In this work, we view video editing as conditional generation and identify a key insight: attention demand varies significantly both across different key sources (e.g., source videos, reference images, audio, text, and generated videos) and across queries within each source. Yet there is currently no general way to measure incoherence or to tell when it matters. We develop tools to measure incoherence and characterise when it affects decisions. We introduce the incoherence ratio, an MMD-based measure of how far any updating procedure departs from coherence, computed only from the procedure's own predictions and updates, and accompany it with an anytime-valid test of coherence. In Bayesian experimental design, acquisition objectives that are equivalent for coherent models choose different data under every approximate model we study, and the incoherence ratio tracks how far incoherence distorts them. We also introduce action coherence, which captures only the incoherence that changes a decision. What a resulting disagreement costs, however, is determined primarily by predictive accuracy. Sequential decisions depend on how a model updates as well as on what it predicts, so coherence, like accuracy, should be checked rather than assumed.
