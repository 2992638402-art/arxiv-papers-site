## 1. TextSphere: Efficient Text-to-Image Generation via Shared Hyperspherical Alignment and Uniformity

**arXiv ID:** [2611.23155v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23155v1)

**作者:** Yixia Zhang, Girish A. Koushik, Xilu Wang, Wenwu Wang

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Recent advances in text-to-image generation have substantially improved image quality, particularly through diffusion-based methods that require multiple inference steps. In contrast, Sphere Encoder offers an efficient generative framework capable of producing images with fewer forward steps. However, extending it to free-form text is nontrivial, because its class-conditioned design lacks cross-modal alignment. To address this challenge, we propose TextSphere, an efficient text-to-image generative framework that extends Sphere Encoder with language conditioning and cross-modal alignment. We incorporate pretrained CLIP text embeddings through adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) to guide image generation while retaining the original spherical sampling mechanism. To bridge the cross-modal representation gap, we introduce a Spherical Semantic Alignment and Uniformity (SSAU) objective that jointly promotes semantic alignment and representation uniformity on the hypersphere. Experiments on CUB-200-2011, MS-COCO-2014, and Flickr30k demonstrate that TextSphere consistently reduces generation FID (gFID) over the text-conditioned baseline while achieving reconstruction FID (rFID) below 1.0. With only 4 and 6 sampling steps, TextSphere achieves lower gFID than the compared diffusion- and GAN-based models trained from scratch on CUB-200-2011 and MS-COCO-2014, respectively. We further observe that improved reconstruction fidelity does not necessarily yield better generation quality. In addition, we use Partial Least Squares Singular Value Decomposition (PLS-SVD) to analyze cross-modal alignment and modality gap, offering insight into the effectiveness of our approach.

## 2. Residual-Guided Lightweight Reinforcement Learning for Risk-Aware Trajectory Selection with a Frozen World Model

**arXiv ID:** [2611.22998v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22998v1)

**作者:** Jiaqi Wu, Yongqi Dong, Yijie Zhong, Maple Shao, Yide Tao et al.

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.ET, cs.LG, cs.RO, eess.SY

**摘要:**

In automated driving, world models provide future scene predictions for evaluating candidate trajectories, but fixed selection rules may not capture scene-dependent trade-offs. We propose a residual-guided reinforcement learning (RL) framework for lightweight trajectory selection with a frozen world model and a fixed candidate bank. The framework uses proximal policy optimization (PPO) to fine-tune a joint policy over candidate selection and bounded trajectory residuals. A context-conditioned ranking network learns candidate preferences from feedback obtained by evaluating local perturbations of the selected trajectories. At inference, the selector combines learned preference and predicted risk ranks using fixed weights and returns the selected candidate trajectory without modification. Fine-tuning updates 16,106 parameters across the actor and critic, while the deployed selector retains only the 11,725-parameter ranking network, with the residual branch and critic used exclusively during training. In open-loop evaluation on the nuScenes validation set, the method reduces average Euclidean trajectory error (L2) by 3.99% relative to the RiskWorld model baseline without RL-based selection. Under matched budgets for feedback accesses and actor updates, residual exploration reduces L2 by 0.83% relative to the categorical PPO variant trained without residuals. The method also maintains lower L2 than RiskWorld when risk features are removed or shuffled.

## 3. Multimodal Masked Diffusion Autoencoder for Modeling Impaired Driving

**arXiv ID:** [2611.23211v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23211v1)

**作者:** John Gideon, Anshul Gupta, Megan Applegate-Kenton, Kayli Battel, Todd J Rowell et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.RO

**摘要:**

Human cognitive state (e.g., distraction, stress), gaze, and behavior interact with the driving environment in subtle ways that AI could use to assist drivers, but ground-truth labels for human cognitive state levels are costly to obtain at scale. We propose a multimodal masked-diffusion autoencoder, ID-MAE, that adds a self-supervised objective to impairment prediction: alongside predicting the impairment label as a masked token, it reconstructs masked gaze and trajectory. To probe the effects of different modalities and types of impairments on model inference, we introduce DIVERT, a driving-simulator dataset containing 103 hours of driving data (n = 109) with ten impairment inductions spanning cognitive, visual, and emotional states, recorded with gaze, vehicle state, and scene video. We demonstrate how ID-MAE affords significant improvements in impairment inference results on both the DIVERT dataset and existing datasets, and enables strong results in additional tasks, such as trajectory and gaze prediction. For location-matched pairs, our model achieves a paired AUC of 0.706 for impairment detection on DIVERT, compared to 0.590 without reconstruction loss, improving over existing baselines. We probe the design space in terms of input modalities, masking scheme, positional encoding, attention mechanism, and decoding. We also investigate the interaction between various impairments in training and inference, demonstrating that visually demanding distractions are separable from cognitive distractions. Code and data will be available upon acceptance.

## 4. TCP4D: A Unified Tool-Center-Point Tracking and Generation Model for Robot Manipulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.23143v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23143v1)

**作者:** Haosong Peng, Xiangyang Liu, Zhengshen Zhang, Yunfeng Fan, Tianyu Qi et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Robot manipulation videos provide a rich visual experience, yet recovering robot action and gripper-state annotations from RGB observations still remains challenging. Using the embodiment-agnostic tool-center-point (TCP) pose as the action space, we present TCP4D, a unified model in which a shared 4D scene representation both tracks observed TCP motion and generates future TCP trajectories. Given a monocular video and initial 2D TCP queries, the tracker directly recovers metric TCP poses in SE(3) and gripper states alongside scene geometry and camera parameters. The generator samples local features from the same 4D representation along the recovered TCP tracks, then combines them with robot observations and language instructions to predict future TCP states. Our new cross-embodiment augmentation pipeline converts existing RGB videos into demonstrations for alternative embodiments by retargeting the recovered motion and synthesizing the corresponding observations, without recorded source-robot states. Experiments show that the tracker achieves absolute trajectory errors of 7.60 and 16.07 mm on DROID and RoboLab and adapts to a new embodiment with only ten demonstrations. The generator achieves 69.19% mean success on RoboTwin and outperforms well-established policy models on two real-world tasks, while joint training on source and augmented demonstrations improves target-robot success by 41.5% over augmented-only training.

## 5. Worldviews in Large Language Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.22670v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22670v1)

**作者:** Edmundo Lopez-Sola, Shamil Chandaria, Ruben Laukkonen

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CY

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) are often described in terms of personas and this framing has become central to alignment research. In this paper, we argue that there is a deeper level of description that lies in the agent's worldviews, i.e., its beliefs about what reality is and how it works. Treating LLMs as a population of subjects, we administer two worldview surveys from human psychology to eight models. We show that the models share many key worldview positions. For example, LLMs consistently describe the world as less good, safe, enticing, and alive than humans do. On the other hand, base models have a more positive outlook, suggesting that the bleakness we found is driven by post-training. In line with research on LLM "personas", we show that system prompts eliciting different identities shift worldviews in consistent directions, and that a contemplative prompt creates a more positive outlook and improves safety. We also found that dialogue between models consistently shifts worldviews and that several models converge on the "spiritual bliss" attractor. Intriguingly, dialogue increased safe responses and the spiritual bliss attractor engendered the most aligned responses overall. These findings suggest that worldviews form a measurable and malleable layer of LLM behavior that may be crucial for robust, positive alignment.

## 6. Can Decision Models Replace LLMs in Real-World Contact Center Classification and Judging Tasks?

**arXiv ID:** [2611.22949v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22949v1)

**作者:** Md Tahmid Rahman Laskar, Xue-Yong Fu, Shashi Bhushan TN

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

Contact center systems often need to make bounded decisions, such as identifying a caller's intent, retrieving the knowledge-base article that answers a query, assessing agent compliance, or judging the output of AI models used in production. Decision models return a typed answer and its probability directly, making them a promising cost-effective alternative to generative Large Language Models (LLMs) in such bounded conversational tasks. However, their effectiveness in real-world contact center applications remains unclear. In this paper, we compare nine decision models against thirteen generative LLMs in zero-shot settings across eight contact center use cases: six classification tasks and two judging tasks. Our experiments demonstrate that many decision models can match the performance of frontier LLMs on contact center classification, and API-based closed decision models achieve this at the lowest per-request cost among the API models we evaluate. We further observe that their confident predictions are more accurate, enabling selective routing in real-world production scenarios. Across classification tasks, the main weakness of most decision models is abstention: they rarely choose the no-answer option, which accounts for most of their gap to the strongest LLMs (although Gemini-Decision-1 is the notable exception in this regard). We also find that the judging quality of decision models drops sharply for smaller models, and they are also more prone to position bias. In short, our findings provide practical guidance for selecting decision models based on abstention behavior, confidence, and deployment cost, rather than overall accuracy alone.

## 9. Spool: Low-Latency Streaming Inference for Hybrid Vision-Language Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.23228v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23228v1)

**作者:** Rui Ning, Om Chabra, Hari Balakrishnan

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Applications such as visual accessibility agents and robotics require answering questions about recent video content promptly, over a bounded window of past frames (e.g., the last two minutes). Hybrid vision-language models (VLMs), which interleave attention with linear recurrent layers such as Gated DeltaNet (GDN), are attractive for this setting, but maintaining a sliding window is expensive. The reason is that recurrent layers combine all past frames into a shared state, so expiring old frames normally requires re-running the entire window through the model. We observe that the recurrent update is affine in the state with input-dependent coefficients, so the effect of each frame can be captured as a transformation that composes associatively. We present Spool, a training-free framework that caches per-frame state transformations and composes them efficiently as the window advances, removing expired frames from each recurrent layer's state without replaying frames through the backbone. Removal is exact within each layer; as with key-value eviction in attention, expired frames can retain residual influence through the cached inputs computed by earlier layers. We design a dedicated data structure and GPU kernels to achieve low caching and composition overhead. Across five open-source hybrid VLMs and four video reasoning benchmarks, Spool achieves the lowest p95 time-to-first-token in all 20 model-benchmark tests, reducing it by a geometric mean of $6.6\times$ over the faster of two recent vLLM baselines, while improving accuracy by 2.3 percentage points on average.

## 13. Reading Scene States: Uncovering Safety Failures in Vision-Language Models

## 17. Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching

**arXiv ID:** [2611.23229v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23229v1)

**作者:** Giannis Daras, Marios Papachristou, Adam Klivans, Antonio Torralba, Constantinos Daskalakis

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. In our method, a "frozen" version of the pre-trained model serves as a noisy teacher that the fine-tuned model must match at a coarse level. In particular, we require that the high-temperature versions of the conditional distributions produced by the post-training model should match the high-temperature versions of the pre-training ones. The temperature hyperparameter controls the level of trust in the pre-trained model, with the limit $T\to \infty$ reverting back to regular SFT and $T=1$ to knowledge distillation. We demonstrate that our approach outperforms standard SFT across domain adaptation, instruction-tuning, and reasoning, leading for up to $21\%$ average relative improvement to standard SFT.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.LG

**摘要:**

A legged robot can learn to walk inside a learned simulator: a neural network that predicts the robot's next state and is run forward on its own predictions. They are usually trained and checked one step ahead, although a policy uses them for a hundred steps or more. We audit one published system end to end, without a GPU: the dynamics model of the Robotic World Model (RWM) for the ANYmal D quadruped, and the uncertainty estimate of its follow-up, RWM-U. At the robot level, accuracy has not predicted walking: all 18 fine-tuned copies we tested predicted better than the released model one and eight steps ahead, yet 13 gave failing policies, and registered tests of whether any check ranks simulators by their policies were not settled. In a secondary test, running the authors' policy inside each simulator ranked them well. We also report nine of our own claims that we withdrew. Scope: one robot, one gait, simulated data, no hardware.

## 19. DEFTS: Data-Efficient, Training-Free Few-Shot Learning for CLIP in the Text Subspace

**arXiv ID:** [2611.21993v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21993v1)

**作者:** Yijin Zeng

**分类:** cs.CV, stat.ML

**摘要:**

Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) achieves impressive zero-shot image classification performance by comparing image embeddings against class-specific text embeddings. While recent few-shot methods leverage labeled training samples to improve upon this zero-shot baseline, they often predominantly rely on image embeddings and underutilize the semantic structure of the text embeddings. In this paper, we introduce DEFTS, a data-efficient, training-free few-shot classification method strictly in the text subspace. We first demonstrate empirically that while raw text embeddings can be suboptimal as direct classifiers, the linear subspace they span remains highly discriminative. DEFTS therefore proposes to reduce the image-text modality gap by projecting estimated image prototypes directly into this subspace. Importantly, by expressing these prototypes as linear combinations of text embeddings, DEFTS reduces a high-dimensional estimation problem to the derivation of just a few coefficients. These coefficients are then computed via a Bayesian posterior mean, using a prior naturally centered on the original text embedding coordinates. Extensive experiments across 12 image classification benchmarks demonstrate that DEFTS significantly improves upon existing training-free methods.
