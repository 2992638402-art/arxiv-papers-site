## 1. Real2Worlds2Real: Scaling Self-Improving Agentic Real2Sim2Real for Robot Manipulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.31098v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31098v1)

**作者:** Jinzhong Gu, Jiakai Chen, Xinyi Cao, Yan Shen, Hangke Sui et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Scaling robot manipulation learning remains challenging due to the high cost of collecting real-world interaction data. Real-to-Sim-to-Real (Real2Sim2Real) offers a promising route by enabling policy learning in simulation grounded in real-world observations, yet scaling this paradigm across diverse tasks and physical interactions remains difficult. We introduce Real2Worlds2Real, a self-improving agentic framework that integrates scalable simulation-world construction with policy development. Given limited visual observations and task descriptions, its tool-orchestration harness constructs faithful digital twins and expands them into diverse, physically executable digital cousins. The agent adaptively selects tools and revisits construction decisions, using visual and execution feedback to refine geometry and physical behavior. Building on these worlds, the agent orchestrates policy development by coordinating data generation, policy learning, and refinement based on task requirements and simulation rollout feedback. Across both stages, the agent distills validated procedures into reusable skills that guide subsequent tasks. Experiments demonstrate improved world fidelity and successful real-world policy transfer, with performance gains from increasing world diversity and interaction-data volume. Skill reuse further improves world-building efficiency and long-horizon task success.

## 2. From Rewarding What Models Say to How They Think: Attention Process Reward Model for LLM Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2611.30627v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30627v1)

**作者:** Kairong Han, Nuanqiao Shan, Ziyu Zhao, Xinpeng Dong, Zijing Hu et al.

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become an important component in large language models (LLMs). However, existing RL approaches focus on final outputs or textual solution trajectories, leading to coarse-grained rewards and unreliable reasoning. Such paradigms focus mainly on what the model outputs while overlooking how the model internally reasons. Therefore, can we reward how a model thinks internally rather than merely what it says? We first reveal the problem of textual reward aliasing in existing reward modeling approaches. Motivated by this, we propose the Attention Process Reward Model (APRM), which assigns internal rewards based on hidden states and attention maps to capture high-quality reasoning patterns. With only approximately 46M parameters, APRM is trained using ranking and counterfactual objectives, and its reward guides RL optimization while being periodically updated to handle distribution shifts. Integrated with representative RL methods, APRM achieved an average pass@8 gain of 2.88\%. Our results demonstrate the promise of incorporating internal reasoning signals into RL for LLMs.

## 3. Rethinking the Evaluation of Contrastive Decoding for Hallucination Mitigation in Multimodal Large Language Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.30460v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30460v1)

**作者:** Ting Chen, Geng Li, Guohao Chen, Hanjia Lyu

**分类:** cs.CL, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Contrastive decoding (CD) mitigates object hallucination in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by contrasting token distributions under different conditions to adjust token scores. However, existing generative and discriminative benchmarks primarily measure final task scores, which can be confounded by candidate filtering, changes in generation trajectories, and response biases, making it difficult to isolate the effects of the underlying contrastive adjustments. To address these limitations while complementing existing evaluations, we introduce CONCOR (Contrastive Correction Evaluation), a controlled benchmark that directly evaluates the competition between ground-truth and different types of hallucinated candidates using the Contrastive Grounding Margin (CGM). CGM also enables analysis of directional consistency, composition, and reliability of contrastive corrections. Our evaluation reveals that existing CD methods exhibit distinct correction behaviors: some consistently favor ground-truth candidates, whereas others produce strong corrections but also introduce more errors. Crucially, these differences help explain their contrasting responses to increased contrast strength, which improves some methods while degrading others. These findings highlight the importance of evaluating the contrastive correction itself, rather than relying solely on final-output performance, and CONCOR provides a controlled and interpretable framework for understanding and improving CD methods for hallucination mitigation.

## 4. RoboUniverse-10K: Toward the Scaling Era of Agentic Robotics via Real-to-Sim Environments

**摘要:**

Agentic Robotics, in which foundation models act as coding agents to control robots through executable programs, has emerged as a compelling paradigm for embodied intelligence. ## 5. Sparse Hodge Preference Optimization: Fine-Tuning Language Models toward Alignment beyond Transitive Preferences

**arXiv ID:** [2611.30135v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30135v1)

**作者:** Yiming Liu, Jianwei Ma, Zhongwei Xing, Tonghui Yu

**分类:** stat.ML, cs.LG

**摘要:**

The growing adoption of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which leverages pairwise preference data, reflects its great success in fine-tuning large language models to align with human expectations. It directly optimizes the policy from human preferences without explicit reward modeling or the complexities of reinforcement learning-based approaches. However, the classic DPO relies on the Bradley--Terry sampling model and cannot effectively capture intransitivity or cyclic structures in aggregated human preference data. In this article, we propose Sparse Hodge Preference Optimization (SHPO), a preference optimization algorithm that augments DPO with a sparse antisymmetric curl residual motivated by the combinatorial Hodge decomposition. We prove that SHPO strictly enlarges DPO's hypothesis class on comparison graphs containing at least one triangle, under some mild regularity conditions on the response representations. We further establish a theoretical guarantee for $\ell_1$-regularized estimation of the curl coefficients under fixed policy and representations. Qualitative studies on three preference datasets under the same QLoRA budget, SHPO-aligned LLM achieves satisfactory performances in preference accuracy, cyclic structure recovery, and GPT-4o-judged generation quality.

## 6. ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control

We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

## 8. VITA-WAM: Manifold-Aligned World-Action Modeling for Real-Time Robot Control

**arXiv ID:** [2611.31195v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31195v1)

**作者:** Boqi Zhao, Dechen Gao, Hanchu Zhou, Hang Wang, Junshan Zhang

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

World action model (WAM) is a promising paradigm for visuomotor control by jointly modeling world dynamics and actions. Notably, existing WAMs often rely on large generative models and iterative sampling which incur substantial latency for action generation, limiting real-time deployment. In particular, there are two root causes of inefficiency in conventional flow matching or diffusion based action models in WAMs: 1) transporting uninformative Gaussian noise into precise actions necessitating multiple ODE steps, and 2) repeatedly injecting task context through expensive cross-attention modules. Our key insight is that jointly learning and aligning the source and action manifolds streamlines the transport between them, substantially accelerating action generation. The resulting architecture also eliminates costly attention for observation conditioning during repeated denoising steps. Notably, with a single ODE step, VITA-WAM outperforms 10-step FastWAM by 21.47 pp on LIBERO-Plus and by 1.65 pp on LIBERO, achieving these gains through the FLA objective without distillation, while inferring at 55.67~ms latency, a $2.10\times$ speedup compared to FastWAM.

## 9. World Models as Native Latent Action Models

**arXiv ID:** [2611.30898v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30898v1)

**作者:** Qinyue Li, Weijie Qiao, Qingyi Tao, Zerui Gong, Zhe Yang et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.LG, cs.RO

**摘要:**

Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.

## 10. DyLoMo-DiT: Dynamic Looped Mixture-of-Experts Diffusion Transformers

**作者:** Lernie Li, Yuanliu Liu, Pengfei Xiong, Wei Zhang

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) scale model capacity by increasing both parameter count and computation. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) variants expand parameter capacity while keeping per-token computation roughly fixed, but still require substantial parameter storage. In this paper, we propose Dynamic Looped MoE DiT (DyLoMo-DiT), which reuses parameters rather than adding them: a weight-shared loop repeatedly applies the middle layers, while sparse experts allocate parameter capacity across tokens. ## 12. Quantizing Diffusion LLMs under Parallel Decoding: Commitment Risk and the Parallelism Tax

**arXiv ID:** [2611.30587v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30587v1)

**作者:** Huan Xu, Maosen Zhao, Pengtao Chen, Tao Chen

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) are served with accelerated decoders that cache context and commit, in one step, every masked position whose confidence clears a threshold. Yet post-training quantization of dLLMs has so far been calibrated and evaluated under fixed-step samplers without caching. We study W4A4 quantization under the deployed decoder and find that the decoder determines both where quantization harms accuracy and what it costs. Irreversible errors enter chiefly through forced commitments, made when no position clears the threshold: they are a minority of commitments but carry most quantization-induced flips. Quantization also levies a parallelism tax, extra forward passes per response: by compressing top logit gaps, it pushes positions below the threshold while leaving predictions largely intact. We propose DECAL, which reweights layer-wise calibration by a commitment-risk prior measured on the deployed decoder, and an argmax-preserving commit temperature that recovers the tax. On LLaDA and Dream, DECAL attains the best average accuracy among W4A4 methods, with lower run-to-run variance than the closest existing calibration method.

## 13. GlanceNav: Efficient Vision-and-Language Navigation with Streaming Memory and Counterfactual Rewind

**arXiv ID:** [2611.30900v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30900v1)

**作者:** Anh Dao, Quan-Dung Pham, Danh Vinh Le, Tuyen Pham Le, Quan Nguyen et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.RO

**摘要:**

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have become strong policies for vision-and-language navigation (VLN). However, most of them encode a sample of past frames again at every decision and are trained on isolated decisions, so computation is redundant and the usable history is short. They also learn from their own mistakes through outcome rewards that give one scalar to a whole episode. We ask whether a navigator can encode each frame only once, at a constant cost per step, and still remember what matters and learn efficiently from its mistakes. We present GlanceNav, a streaming navigator built on a hybrid MLLM. Its Gated DeltaNet layers carry a recurrent state through the whole episode, while its attention layers keep a short window of recent observations and a small bank of keyframes, selected by how much each frame changes the recurrent state. This constant-size memory keeps the cost per step fixed and allows training on whole episodes with the same memory as at inference. Because the entire memory can also be copied in constant time, we further propose Counterfactual Rewind (CoRe), a reinforcement learning stage that rewinds a drifting rollout to its closest approach to the goal, lets an expert correct a few actions, and credits decisions by the resulting change in outcome. Trained only on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE training splits, with 1,000 instructions for reinforcement learning, a 4B GlanceNav reaches success rates of 71.9% and 73.6% on R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen. On R2R-CE, it outperforms the strongest previous single-RGB method trained without external data by 9.1 points, while running at 104 ms per step.

## 19. Orthogonally Mixed Sign Coding with Progressive Residual Refinement for Transformer Weight Compression

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.IR

**摘要:**

LLM quantization reduces storage but often degrades predictive fidelity at low bit rates. We propose a training-free, CDF-free, Gaussian-CLT-motivated progressive residual-sign quantization framework that uses randomized orthogonal mixing to promote Gaussian-like residual distributions and improve sign based reconstruction of Transformer weights. Each stage independently normalizes residuals and analytically calibrates reconstruction, progressively recovering quantization errors. Prompting language models to verbalize stepwise numerical confidence during generation is an increasingly popular approach for triggering backtracking and error correction in agentic reasoning. This strategy presupposes two capabilities: (1) in-situ monitoring fidelity (does confidence drop when an error occurs?) and (2) downstream steering control (does an uncertainty token causally steer the model to repair its error?). We evaluated four open-weights models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, and Phi-4, 14.7B) across 15,200 math benchmark attempts, isolating 2,017 reasoning errors within each model's capability frontier. Applying 27,568 counterfactual continuations across these failure points, we demonstrate a consistent decoupling between monitoring and control. On monitoring, all four models fail to detect their own errors. Verbalized confidence does not distinguish mistake steps from sound reasoning, yielding near-chance within-position AUROCs between 0.500 and 0.561. Even at a model's first reasoning error, trajectory confidence remains saturated at 95.1% to 99.9%, with noticeable drops ($\ge 10$ points) appearing on fewer than 9% of errors in Llama and under 1.6% in the other three models. In contrast, models respond readily to external uncertainty: injecting a low-confidence tag (10%) at the error step significantly increases downstream correction across all models (+3.7 to +22.5 points over format-matched 95% certainty; all $p \le 0.0022$), reaching up to 38.5% under active rethinking instructions. However, injecting doubt onto already-correct steps causes models to discard valid reasoning, reducing solve rates by up to 22.8 points. Inference-time self-correction is thus bottlenecked by in-situ error detection rather than downstream repair capacity: models readily steer toward recovery when prompted with doubt, yet fail to self-monitor during generation.
