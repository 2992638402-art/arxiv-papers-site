## 1. AG-WAM: Attention-Guided World Action Model for Accurate and Efficient Robot Manipulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32722v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32722v1)

**作者:** Hongcheng Zhang, Lei Sun, Songxuan Liu, Tengteng Pan, Yuqiang Zhai et al.

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Recently developed World Action Models (WAMs) jointly predict future observations and robot actions, providing predictive context for control. However, existing WAMs typically learn implicit attention allocation with limited explicit action-relevant attention guidance, leading to limited performance. To unleash strong capabilities of WAMs, we introduce explicit attention guidance as a unifying principle and propose our Attention-Guided WAM (AG-WAM), an end-to-end framework with three plug-and-play attention guidance modules that directly reformulate internal attention paradigm in WAMs: the Content-Adaptive Predictive Attention module, the Guidance-Point Visual Attention module, and the State-Conditioned Text Attention module. Without any external plugin module adopted, our end-to-end framework with the unifying principle of explicit attention guidance boosts the accuracy and efficiency of WAMs. Without using any private embodied training data, our AG-WAM establishes new state-of-the-art performance on multiple public robotic manipulation benchmarks including LIBERO-X, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin-2.0, significantly outperforming other methods, even those using massive private embodied training data by a notable margin, meanwhile notably improving training and inference efficiency.

## 2. MetaFORGE: A Meta-Learning Framework for Harness Optimization in Automated Code Generation for Parallel Computing

**arXiv ID:** [2611.33119v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33119v1)

**作者:** Scott Piersall, Yang Gao, Shenyang Liu, Liqiang Wang

**分类:** cs.AI

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) can generate parallel programs, but their correctness and performance depend strongly on the instructions that guide generation. We present \textsc{MetaFORGE}, an execution-grounded meta-learning framework that iteratively optimizes persistent natural-language skill specifications for MPI C code generation. \textsc{MetaFORGE} combines correctness testing, compiler diagnostics, static-analysis findings, runtime measurements, and rank-level load-imbalance profiles into a scalar reward that drives textual-gradient refinement, while an external memory distills and retrieves reward-supported optimization knowledge across runs. Across six MPI tasks and three workload sizes, \textsc{MetaFORGE} improves static-analysis quality, runtime, and load balance relative to zero-shot generation and generally exceeds the evaluated \textsc{TextGrad} iterative baseline. A model-matched refinement ablation shows that the MetaFORGE advantage persists when its refinement model is reduced to \texttt{gpt-4o}. An expanded retrieval ablation across all 18 task--workload configurations shows consistent runtime benefit and broad static-analysis and load-balance benefit from retrieved context. Leakage-controlled held-out and zero-update experiments further provide evidence that optimization knowledge persists in both the transferred skill and external memory under the evaluated protocol.

## 3. BioLoom: Scaling Biomedical Vision-Language Models by Learning from the Structure of Scientific Articles

**arXiv ID:** [2611.33151v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33151v1)

**作者:** Yuwei Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Alejandro Lozano, Yuan-Sen Ting, Yuhui Yuan et al.

**分类:** cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

The development of biomedical vision-language models (VLMs) with contrastive image-text pretraining has been driven by scaling to increasingly large and diverse image-text datasets. Beyond scaling, further progress can come from learning richer representations from existing data. We hypothesize that the structure of scientific literature itself provides an additional source of supervision: figures, panels, captions, and accompanying article text encode rich relationships across modalities. Yet, treating these as independent image-text pairs overlooks their relationships. To address this limitation, we incorporate these structural relationships into contrastive image-text supervision. We preserve exact image-text matches while introducing weaker positive supervision for related pairs, such as a panel and the caption of a sibling panel. We upweight cross-article negatives of the same content type to encourage finer discrimination. We also enrich panel descriptions using visual content, original figure captions, and mentioning paragraphs. Pairing panels with these descriptions and whole figures with their original captions yields Loom52M, a dataset of 52.4 million image-text pairs spanning scientific charts and biomedical images. Using Loom52M, BioLoom (DFN-2B) achieves 56.82% domain-balanced mean accuracy across 67 zero-shot classification tasks from 43 datasets in 10 domains, compared with 48.77% for MedPMC-CLIP. On BioLoomBench with concise captions and BioMedFlickr, it achieves bidirectional mean Recall@1 of 63.20% and 10.59%, versus 46.37% and 7.55% for the respective strongest evaluated baselines, BiomedCLIP and MedPMC-CLIP. Code, datasets, benchmarks, and models are available at https://bioloom-clip.github.io/.

## 4. DexWAC: A Unified World-Actor-Critic Model for Dexterous Robot Manipulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32654v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32654v1)

**作者:** Hongjia Liu, Dacheng Zhou, Minghao Fu, Chuanjun Guo, Tianze Wang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI

**摘要:**

Robot demonstrations provide more than actions to imitate: they reveal how actions change the world and how those changes contribute to task success. Recent world-action models (WAMs) extend vision-language-action (VLA) models with the first signal by jointly predicting actions and future observations, yet this predictive ability serves only as an auxiliary loss for policy learning. Meanwhile, most policy improvement methods learn dynamics and values in separate models, increasing training overhead and limiting representation sharing between these complementary objectives. We introduce DexWAC, a unified world-actor-critic model that jointly learns an actor, an action-conditioned world model, and a bootstrapped critic in a single robot pretraining stage. Our key insight is that visual prediction encourages control-relevant features, while value supervision promotes task-relevant representations; together, they shape a representation suited to policy learning. Built on an image-editing backbone, DexWAC predicts, for each action chunk, the visual latent and proprioception at its endpoint together with its reward and continuation, so the world model alone efficiently yields complete imagined transitions without generating intermediate video frames. After adapting the world model and critic on real rollouts, we improve the pretrained actor through either PPO on imagined rollouts or critic-guided best-of-$N$ selection. DexWAC surpasses the large-scale pretrained VLA and WAM baselines in simulation and achieves 75% average success on two real-world tasks, compared with 45% for $π_{0.5}$. Its world model is more accurate and faster than video-based baselines, and ablations confirm that learning dynamics and value together improves the policy.

## 5. ReFocus: Progress-Guided Adaptive Practice for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32639v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32639v1)

**作者:** Haoyuan Li, Wei Huang, Jiehong Lin, Shengrong Zhang, Chenzhi Liu et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Online reinforcement learning (RL) enables vision-language-action policies to improve through environmental interaction. However, in long-horizon manipulation, sparse success/failure feedback provides little information about where policies encounter bottlenecks. Moreover, repeated full-task rollouts also leave these bottlenecks weakly analyzed and insufficiently practiced. To this end, we introduce ReFocus, a progress-guided multi-round RL framework that alternates between Global and Local RL to identify task-specific bottlenecks and allocate additional practice to them. Specifically, during Global RL, we decompose each task into a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of subtasks. Then a DAG-based Progress Reward is leveraged for both policy training and bottleneck diagnosis. Based on the identified bottlenecks, ReFocus selects Adaptive Practice Windows, defining task segments from an entry stage to a target stage. It allocates additional interaction to these segments during Local RL while updating the entire policy. After that, it evaluates the policy to perform the full task for the next round of RL, forming an iteration between Global and Local RL. Experiments on LIBERO-Long and RoboCasa365 show that ReFocus achieves success rates of 97.8% and 51.3%, respectively, compared with 90.6% and 39.3% for the $π_{0.5}$ baseline. Furthermore, we evaluate a real-world adaptation on three long-horizon tasks. On BreadBaker, ReFocus achieves 95% success, compared with 30% for the $π_{0.5}$. Project page: https://refocus-robotics.github.io.

## 6. TeamFlowRL: Online Policy Optimization of Decentralized Flow Policies for Multi-Agent Coordination

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32645v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32645v1)

**作者:** Guowei Zou, Beiwen Zhang, Haitao Wang, Hong Hong, Zhiquan Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Complex cooperative tasks require coordinated multi-agent behavior. Offline generative policies learn expressive cooperative behaviors from trajectories but remain limited by fixed datasets. Online reinforcement learning can improve them through environment interaction. However, fine-tuning flow policies is difficult because action likelihoods are hard to compute. We propose TeamFlowRL, a two-stage framework that pretrains multi-agent flow policies offline and fine-tunes them online. We formulate team policy optimization over the complete denoising trajectories of decentralized flow policies, avoiding intractable action likelihoods. The resulting objective weights each agent's trajectory ratio with a shared team advantage and clips each ratio separately, aligning local policy updates with the common team return. Across 51 paired configurations in seven simulation groups, TeamFlowRL improves every offline initialization, with a 47.3% average gain in normalized score. It also achieves 33.1% higher average success under exploration noise than Gaussian-policy fine-tuning across 6 bimanual tasks.

## 7. iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future Prediction

**arXiv ID:** [2611.33141v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33141v1)

**作者:** Vansh Kapoor, Max Sobol Mark, Sreyas Venkataraman, Max Simchowitz, Aviral Kumar

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

World-action models (WAMs) build policies on pretrained video-generation models, on the premise that predicting how a scene evolves helps determine how to act. However, generating future scenes is expensive for real-time control. This motivates efficient proxies for future imagination, but how to train such proxies without sacrificing action-prediction quality remains unclear. An effective proxy need not generate a high-resolution frame; it only needs to provide enough information to distinguish between possible futures. This leads to the design principle behind iWAM: rather than denoising an entire future frame, can we condition the action policy on intermediate features from a single video-model evaluation? We show that the answer is yes. Exploiting the reversible dynamics of flow-matching models, we invert each training future into the corresponding latent noise vector. However, a single velocity evaluation at this latent does not necessarily yield informative features for action prediction. We therefore learn a bidirectional flow map that amortizes future-generation computation, enabling a single model evaluation to provide effective conditioning features. At inference, we sample a latent noise vector from the prior and condition the policy using just one video-backbone forward pass. Across simulated benchmarks and real robots, iWAM recovers the generalization benefits of WAMs while substantially reducing its inference cost.

## 8. ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control

**摘要:**

Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

## 9. Understanding Clipping In Zeroth-Order Optimization

**arXiv ID:** [2611.33062v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33062v1)

**作者:** Saket Gollapudi, Elisa Bertino, Sewoong Oh

**分类:** cs.LG

**摘要:**

Zeroth-order optimization has emerged as a resource-efficient alternative to Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) when fine-tuning LLMs, thanks to the inherent low dimensionality of the loss landscape. It relies on a two-point estimate of the gradient that only accesses the function value and not the gradient, bypassing the resource-heavy backpropagation. Recently, it was accidentally discovered that per-sample clipping can improve the performance of the trained model, while running a differentially private version of zeroth-order optimization \cite{zhang2024dpzero} with a very large privacy parameter $ε$ (corresponding to very little privacy). In this paper, we systematically investigate this phenomenon and demonstrate that ($i$) the optimal choice of learning rate $η$ is inversely proportional to the clipping threshold $c$, satisfying $ηc =$ constant; ($ii$) clipping drives the zeroth-order optimization to a different solution with higher test accuracy; and ($iii$) this phenomenon is only observed with per-sample clipped zeroth-order optimization and not other zeroth-order alternatives that also provide robust estimates of the gradient. Further analyses with artificially injected label noise reveals that clipping leads to more robust predictions, which stems from the implicit down weighting of hard-to-fit training examples. Our code is available at https://github.com/SewoongLab/ClipZero

## 11. Simulator-as-Proxy World Model for Robot Manipulation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32786v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32786v1)

**作者:** Tingting Liao, Mingjia Huo, Hao Li, Zhengzhong Liu, Eric P. Xing et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG

**摘要:**

Video world models for robots are increasingly conditioned on a simulated or rendered proxy of the scene, on the assumption that the video will show what the proxy shows. Testing this assumption requires a proxy whose state is explicit and can be perturbed. We present SimPWM (Simulator-as-Proxy World Model): given a calibrated camera, it represents, from the first frame alone, the robot by its 3D model and the objects the gripper may touch as 3D points; a rule-based simulator with no trained parameters moves them under the actions, and a video model renders the result. SimPWM returns the realistic video together with the simulated state. We then freeze the simulated objects while the arm moves as recorded and check whether the video still shows the object lifted. On DROID episodes from unseen setups, the answer differs for the arm and for the objects. With each of three base video models, the arm follows the proxy (robot IoU 0.75-0.78, against at most 0.69 for end-to-end models); rendering the robot alone already gives this. With any base model, the simulated state records what the simulation did; under perturbed actions, a video that disagrees with it reveals that the video ignores the simulated state.

## 13. Towards Realistic Evaluation of Uncertainty Quantification for LLM-Based Recommender Systems

**arXiv ID:** [2611.33036v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33036v1)

**作者:** Hao Wu, Sipeng Chen, Weikai Li, Tianyi Chen, Qiancheng Yang et al.

**分类:** cs.IR, cs.CL, cs.LG, stat.ML

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) provide a flexible interface for recommendation systems. However, their issued rankings require reliable instance-level uncertainty monitoring. In this scenario, comparing uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods becomes challenging because LLM rankers lack a standard item-classification head. Their scores may depend on option labels, prompts, generation procedures, and the available user and item evidence. Existing methods derive uncertainty from different sources and apply it at different stages of the recommendation process. Some methods evaluate an issued ranking, whereas others use uncertainty to change the ranking itself, making direct comparison difficult. Here we introduce a fixed-ranking benchmark which evaluates eight heterogeneous UQ signals as record-level monitors under a shared output contract. We evaluate these signals across four recommendation datasets and two LLM backbones under a shared fixed-ranking protocol. From the results, we draw three main observations. First, UQ method ordering changes across backbones even on the same reference instances. Second, ranking-aligned Top10Mass provides the strongest scalar Error@10 detection in several evaluation conditions, reflecting effective cutoff-aligned monitoring, while its advantage narrows for popularity-tail targets. Third, scalar UQ signals are weak at identifying which control has the larger clipped NDCG@10 difference, whereas source-specific signals, including Perturbation Rank Instability and simple listwise distances, provide stronger paired-control agreement, reflecting their sensitivity to the two controlled evidence sources. These findings motivate us to evaluate ranking-failure detection, robustness across recommendation conditions, and responses to controlled evidence changes as distinct properties of UQ.

## 14. DynaMimic: A Physics-Informed World Model for Humanoid Motion Tracking

**作者:** Chaoyue Xing, Linyi Huang, Yiting He, Yixuan Yin, Siqi Hou et al.

**摘要:**

Whole-body motion tracking enables humanoid robots to reproduce diverse skills from human demonstrations. Existing model-free reinforcement learning methods either distill motion specialists into a single policy at the cost of performance or train a generalist policy directly using cluster-scale computation. ## 15. Recursive Collective Intelligence: Emergence on an Equal-Budget Ladder in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32606v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32606v1)

**作者:** Lixuan He, Shengqu Cai, Fang Wu, Shikang Zheng, Liwei Jiang et al.

**分类:** cs.MA

**摘要:**

Large language models increasingly work as teams of agents, and a team is expected to solve problems beyond the reach of any single member. Most evaluations credit a team for outperforming a single agent, without accounting for the additional computation: a team may consume many times the tokens of one agent, and a plain vote over independent answers already explains most of the reported gain. Two questions therefore remain open: When does interaction itself make a group smarter, and can a group improve its next generation by learning from its own work? We answer both with the equal-budget ladder, which grades a group against independent agents at three levels: outperforming a single agent, a compute-matched majority vote, and compute-matched pass@n. On this ladder we define Recursive Collective Intelligence (RCI) as a group whose own output, without a teacher or external labels, makes its next generation smarter at equal cost. Three findings follow. First, interaction improves collective intelligence when it moves information across agents: forwarding split facts lifts groups above pass@n at three model scales, by about a third at 27B. Second, organization emerges spontaneously: identical 27B agents form hubs and improve accuracy by 18 percentage points; encoding their organization in a prompt enables them to reach level 3 at less than half the token budget in a descriptive comparison. Third, groups recursively improve themselves: Two label-free generations progressively climb the ladder, the second 9B generation surpassing both pass@$n$ and a control trained on base-model turns; on PlanBench, HiddenBench and Hanabi, each generation outperforms its predecessor on unseen problems for four to six generations. A group therefore outperforms its members when it forwards useful information more efficiently than it spends tokens on coordination, and learns to do so from its own work.

## 16. Exploring Multi-Player Social Games as Verifiable Training Grounds for Large Language Models

The training of socially intelligent language models often relies on large language model (LLM) judges, which are expensive, noisy, and vulnerable to reward hacking. ## 18. From Local Errors to System Failures: Modeling and Benchmarking Error Propagation in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

**作者:** Wenlong Yu, Bingxu Lu, Qilong Wang, Qinghua Hu, Qianru Sun

**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI

**摘要:**

In multi-agent systems (MAS) built on large language models (LLMs), errors can propagate through intermediate messages and compromise final answers. However, outcome-based evaluation obscures how agents amplify, attenuate, or correct these errors. We propose Response-Aware Error Propagation among Agents (REPA), a structural model that connects measured local responses to system-level error propagation.
