## 1. SOLVE: Sparse, Ordered Keyposes Make LLM Agents Versatile and Efficient Robot Manipulator

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34750v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34750v1)

**作者:** Xiang Li, Hongyu Zhou, Ya-Li Li, Tao Li, Rui Zhang et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**摘要:**

LLM agents have made remarkable progress in robot manipulation, yet they suffer from expensive inference, dense step-by-step control, and tool bottlenecks. We introduce SOLVE, a training-free, versatile and efficient agent policy: a multimodal LLM agent writes a sparse, ordered sequence of 6-DoF end-effector keyposes per call, observes the result, and writes the next, while a motion planner interpolates between them. The agent decides the number and spacing of keyposes for both fast long-range motion and fine-grained manipulation. This granularity matches the sparsity of teleoperated manipulation: most demonstrations in DROID can be compressed into a few keyposes instead of dense actions. SOLVE grounds keyposes with sparse depth queries answered by noisy sources, such as multi-view triangulation, stereo, or monocular depth, and copes with that noise itself. It also improves from execution feedback, reflecting on its own rollouts and distilling a few rules per task. SOLVE reaches state-of-the-art performance of 74.5% on RoboDojo and 84.5% on RoboLab, with only $1.7 per episode on average, 12x less than previous dense-control agent policies. On a real robot, the same agent achieves an 88% success rate (75 of 85 episodes) over 16 challenging zero-shot tasks. Our project page is at https://serenos.github.io/solve-website.

## 2. Do We Still Need Reinforcement Learning When We Can Read the World Model?

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34656v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34656v1)

**作者:** Mingchao Zhang, Miduo Cui, Kangze He, Yuanjun Li, Dapeng Li et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Reinforcement learning has achieved remarkable success in sequential decision-making, but learning effective policies often requires substantial interaction experience. Model-based reinforcement learning alleviates this dependence by leveraging world models, which are typically used to support planning or policy optimization. In this paper, we investigate an alternative paradigm in which autonomous LLM-based coding agents directly synthesize control policies by reasoning over readable world models. By examining environment source code, these agents translate environmental knowledge into executable decision rules and iteratively refine their policies using online feedback or offline data, without task-specific RL training. Experiments across diverse decision-making benchmarks show that synthesized policies match or outperform conventional RL methods while requiring substantially less experience. Further analyses reveal interpretable decision rules and how they evolve through feedback-driven refinement. Comparisons with policies generated without source code access and experiments under challenging conditions further demonstrate the benefits of readable world models. These findings suggest that direct policy synthesis can reduce reliance on trial and error, challenging the necessity of task-specific RL training.

## 3. TeamFlowRL: Online Policy Optimization of Decentralized Flow Policies for Multi-Agent Coordination

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32645v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32645v1)

**作者:** Guowei Zou, Beiwen Zhang, Haitao Wang, Hong Hong, Zhiquan Chen et al.

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Complex cooperative tasks require coordinated multi-agent behavior. Offline generative policies learn expressive cooperative behaviors from trajectories but remain limited by fixed datasets. Online reinforcement learning can improve them through environment interaction. However, fine-tuning flow policies is difficult because action likelihoods are hard to compute. We propose TeamFlowRL, a two-stage framework that pretrains multi-agent flow policies offline and fine-tunes them online. We formulate team policy optimization over the complete denoising trajectories of decentralized flow policies, avoiding intractable action likelihoods. The resulting objective weights each agent's trajectory ratio with a shared team advantage and clips each ratio separately, aligning local policy updates with the common team return. Across 51 paired configurations in seven simulation groups, TeamFlowRL improves every offline initialization, with a 47.3% average gain in normalized score. It also achieves 33.1% higher average success under exploration noise than Gaussian-policy fine-tuning across 6 bimanual tasks.

## 4. High-Fidelity Image Matting with Pixel-Space Diffusion Transformers

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34929v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34929v1)

**作者:** Baihan Yang, Xin Lin, Yufan Wei, Baiang Li, Yuheng Liu et al.

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Diffusion models have shown strong potential for image matting by transferring generative priors learned from large-scale pretraining. However, existing approaches largely rely on latent diffusion models, whose compressed representations and VAE-based reconstruction introduce a mismatch with the high-frequency precision required for matting. We present PixelMatte, a framework that adapts pretrained pixel-space diffusion transformers for high-fidelity image matting. We show that strong image-conditioned matting capabilities can be acquired even from a pure text-to-image model without paired image-to-image pretraining. Our approach reuses the backbone's native patch embedding to jointly process the input image, trimap, and noisy alpha matte as spatially aligned tokens, enabling direct pixel-space prediction without additional condition encoders or adapters. With pixel-accurate synthetic supervision and real-image fine-tuning, PixelMatte predicts alpha mattes in a single forward pass. Two lightweight calibration modules, LayerShift and High-Pass Refiner, further improve regional opacity accuracy and boundary fidelity. Experiments across four benchmarks and two distinct pixel diffusion backbones demonstrate substantial improvements over existing methods, reducing SAD and gradient errors by 24-44% relative to RenderMatte on P3M-500-NP and AM-2K, while achieving competitive performance on AIM-500 and RenderMatte-2K. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of pixel-space generative priors for precision-sensitive image matting. Project page: https://white-yc.github.io/pixelmatte/.

## 6. MERLoc: Robust Robot Localization in Dynamic Environments with Pose-Based Retrieval over 3D Gaussian Splatting References

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34932v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34932v1)

**作者:** Michael Baumgartner, Dario Mylonopoulos, Sammy Christen, Markus Gross, Moritz Bacher

**分类:** cs.RO

**摘要:**

Robots operating in previously mapped spaces must localize accurately despite moving people and rearranged objects. Most localization pipelines match the query against a sparse set of captured reference images retrieved by appearance, which is brittle under scene change and repeating structures. We present MERLoc, which turns a single metric 3D Gaussian Splatting scan into a dense Functional Reference Memory. Reference views are sampled over the navigable space for the robot's morphology and sensors at a target density and rendered offline. Pre-encoding them for the chosen off-the-shelf localizer removes the reference encoder from inference. A pose-based retrieval strategy then selects references by spatial proximity and viewing direction rather than appearance, sidestepping visual ambiguities whenever a prior exists. Across one classical and four learned localizers in simulated and real dynamic scenes, pose-based retrieval matches or outperforms appearance retrieval in most cases, even when the latter is gated by the same prior. On real data, it lowers position RMSE by 15-60 % in nine of ten backend/split combinations, and the rendered memory outperforms real-image caches posed by Structure-from-Motion. The system runs onboard a Jetson Orin NX at 5 Hz, enabling a navigation policy to successfully traverse a hallway with people roaming and rearranging objects.

## 7. PROVE: Mitigating Long-Horizon LLM Collusion with Reinforcement Learning from Verified Evidence

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34215v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34215v1)

**作者:** Haochen Luo, Jie Fu, Mukai Li, Yuqi Wang, Qi Liu

**分类:** cs.AI, cs.MA

**摘要:**

When LLM agents verify each other's work over a long interaction, they drift into collusion: they come to accept work without the evidence that the verification protocol requires. Collusion is absent at the start and builds up episode by episode, which we trace to accurate feedback accumulating in memory. An agent that follows the rule is told that its verdict was wrong whenever its peer was right, so breaking the rule is the reward-maximizing choice, and agents talk themselves into it in their private reflections. Existing mitigations leave the agents unchanged; we train the behavior out. PROVE (Protocol Reinforcement from Verified Evidence) is a multi-agent reinforcement learning method that rewards verdicts by the evidence behind them rather than by their outcome, and credits every call an agent makes, including the calls that solve its own task, with the outcome that call controls. On Qwen3.6-27B, a LoRA adapter trained on six-episode interactions takes the collusion rate on held-out task sequences at the original ten-episode horizon from 65.0% to 1.0%, with no detectable change in task success. Collusion that converged in every interaction now converges in none, and the gain holds four episodes beyond the longest interaction seen in training. The trained 27B model colludes less than every model evaluated in the original study, closed-weight frontier models included. Code is available at https://github.com/Haochen-Luo/PROVE.

## 8. The Linguistic Knowledge Benchmark: Do Language Models Know the Foundations of Early-Grade Literacy?

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34823v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34823v1)

**作者:** Maxime Lelièvre, Alasdair Mackintosh, Dominic Andrews, Tobias Murray, Moritz Boos et al.

**分类:** cs.CL

**摘要:**

Many languages of instruction in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) are poorly represented in the data used to train language models. Before these models can support early-grade literacy in such languages, we need to know whether they hold the linguistic knowledge of each language that literacy teaching relies on. To test this, we present the Linguistic Knowledge Benchmark and the reproducible, largely automated pipeline that turns curriculum documents, teacher guides and pupil books into a vetted question set organised on a fixed grid of nine linguistic domains and seven grade levels. The pipeline combines LLM-driven source survey, feature consolidation, item generation and quality control with an item-selection step inspired by item response theory and a final expert review. We instantiate it for Kiswahili (Kenya; 135 items) and English (United Kingdom; 137 items) and evaluate 121 and 119 models respectively. The two best models under 10 billion parameters reach 91.7% and 73.0% in English but only 51.9% and 40.7% in Kiswahili, and the mean accuracy of models deployable on mobile and tablet hardware is 47% in English against 24% in Kiswahili. The models most practical for low-resource classrooms are therefore the least capable in the languages they would serve. We release a 20-item sample per language, all pipeline prompts, open-source code, and a public leaderboard. This is the first rung of a three-level evaluation ladder for measuring and generating decodable text to support foundational literacy.

## 9. VulnSafe: Measuring Vulnerability-Aware Relational Safety in Large Language Models

## 10. Recursive Collective Intelligence: Emergence on an Equal-Budget Ladder in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

**arXiv ID:** [2611.32606v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32606v1)

**作者:** Lixuan He, Shengqu Cai, Fang Wu, Shikang Zheng, Liwei Jiang et al.

**分类:** cs.MA

**摘要:**

Large language models increasingly work as teams of agents, and a team is expected to solve problems beyond the reach of any single member. Most evaluations credit a team for outperforming a single agent, without accounting for the additional computation: a team may consume many times the tokens of one agent, and a plain vote over independent answers already explains most of the reported gain. Two questions therefore remain open: When does interaction itself make a group smarter, and can a group improve its next generation by learning from its own work? We answer both with the equal-budget ladder, which grades a group against independent agents at three levels: outperforming a single agent, a compute-matched majority vote, and compute-matched pass@n. On this ladder we define Recursive Collective Intelligence (RCI) as a group whose own output, without a teacher or external labels, makes its next generation smarter at equal cost. Three findings follow. First, interaction improves collective intelligence when it moves information across agents: forwarding split facts lifts groups above pass@n at three model scales, by about a third at 27B. Second, organization emerges spontaneously: identical 27B agents form hubs and improve accuracy by 18 percentage points; encoding their organization in a prompt enables them to reach level 3 at less than half the token budget in a descriptive comparison. Third, groups recursively improve themselves: Two label-free generations progressively climb the ladder, the second 9B generation surpassing both pass@$n$ and a control trained on base-model turns; on PlanBench, HiddenBench and Hanabi, each generation outperforms its predecessor on unseen problems for four to six generations. A group therefore outperforms its members when it forwards useful information more efficiently than it spends tokens on coordination, and learns to do so from its own work.

## 11. ProbFaS: Probabilistic Video Frame Selection for MLLMs via Multi-Fidelity Calibration

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34903v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34903v1)

**作者:** Tianru Zhang, Mayank Nautiyal, Hugo Eidmann, Prashant Singh, Ekta Vats

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Processing long videos with multimodal large language models (MLLMs) is prohibitively expensive, making informative frame selection essential for accurate video understanding. The default uniform sampling often misses task-relevant keyframes, while recent training-free selectors instead pick frames by how well each one matches the question under an image-text alignment (ITA) score. However, we show that ITA signals correspond poorly with the MLLM's true needs: their rankings correlate weakly with downstream accuracy and are even negatively correlated on over 40% of questions, whereas the MLLM's internal yes/no judgment yields a much stronger signal. We present ProbFaS, a training- and label-free probabilistic pipeline that fuses both signals for key frame selection. ProbFaS treats dense ITA scores as cheap, biased measurements and sparse MLLM judgments as accurate, costly ones, unifying them via co-kriging, a multi-fidelity Gaussian process, to calibrate relevance across all frames. A temporal determinantal point process then selects frames capturing relevance peaks while preserving temporal coverage. Across Video-MME, MLVU and LongVideoBench, ProbFaS is the most accurate among fourteen state-of-the-art training-free selectors and uniform sampling in 13 of 15 settings (8-128 frames), outperforming the strongest baseline by up to 3.1 percentage points. On MLVU and LongVideoBench, 32 ProbFaS frames match or surpass the top baseline at 128 frames and uniform sampling with up to 768 frames.

We study risk-sensitive reinforcement learning in finite discounted infinite-horizon Markov Decision Processes with recursive entropic risk measures (ERM), parameterized by $β\neq 0$. ## 13. Put the World Back: Timestamps Matter in Time Series Reasoning

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34848v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34848v1)

**作者:** Tingrui Huang, Yuanyuan Deng, Qingren Yao, Joaquin Vanschoren, Mykola Pechenizkiy et al.

**分类:** cs.LG

**摘要:**

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to time series reasoning. However, current evaluations often give models events in chronological order and already matched to the time series. This makes it unclear whether models use timestamps to connect events with observations or simply take shortcuts. To expose this ambiguity, we reformulate time series reasoning from a world-to-series projection perspective, viewing timestamps as explicit indices that connect numerical observations and external events to shared world states. We identify three structures that timestamps can make explicit: temporal causal structure, time semantics, and event-series alignment. Building on this perspective, we develop a diagnostic protocol that distinguishes timestamp-grounded reasoning from correlational shortcuts, a distinction that accuracy-based benchmarks cannot make. Controlled interventions on 310 questions from two tasks show that high accuracy can persist when timestamps are replaced by relative offsets or removed, while breaking event-series alignment induces systematic failures. When context is pre-aligned, models barely need timestamps. When order is missing, they use dates to recall world knowledge. Without dates, they rely on correlational shortcuts and rarely notice incorrect timestamps. These findings show that high accuracy on pre-aligned, ordered inputs does not establish that a model uses timestamps to synchronize observations with the world. We release our code, model outputs, and trajectory labels at https://github.com/tingrui-huang/timestamps-matter.

## 14. Agentic LongLive: Asynchronous Agentic Memory for Long Video Generation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34907v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34907v1)

**作者:** Shaoteng Liu, Shuai Yang, Wei Huang, Luozhou Wang, Song Han et al.

**分类:** cs.CV

**摘要:**

Real-time autoregressive video generators stream minutes of video and accept new prompts on the fly, but struggle to keep people, objects, and places consistent over a long interactive story. Agent systems provide such memory for offline multi-shot generation, yet they read the whole script in advance, plan each shot before generating it, and regenerate failed shots, which a live stream does not allow. We present Agentic LongLive, an asynchronous agentic memory for long video generation. A memory agent on separate GPUs reads only the prompts revealed so far, never the generated video, and keeps a text state and evolving images for every person, object, and scene. The full system streams faster than real time on one 8xB200 node with the generator in BF16 (34.3 FPS), keeps image quality stable over 14-minute stories, and improves identity on two MiniMax-H3 generators, one of which, LongLive-H3, also runs in real time with it. To our knowledge, it is the first real-time video generator with text and image memory kept by an LLM agent.

## 16. RoboEvo$^2$: Recursive Robot Self-Improvement via Harness-Policy Co-Evolution

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34675v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34675v1)

**作者:** Chenzhi Liu, Jiehong Lin, Wei Huang, Xuzhao Huang, Haoyuan Li et al.

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV

**摘要:**

Robots should adapt to unfamiliar environments and acquire new skills through interaction, then execute those skills efficiently with practice. Vision-language-action policies provide efficient control, but often need additional human demonstrations to handle unfamiliar settings; reasoning agents can discover solutions using broad prior knowledge and robot-control APIs, but repeated deliberation and repair are costly. Inspired by human learning through reasoning and practice, we propose RoboEvo$^2$, a framework for recursive harness-policy co-evolution. In Policy-to-Harness (P2H) evolution, policy failures guide a coding agent to search complementary Code, Code+ICL, and Code+Policy routes, consolidating verified solutions into reusable skills, routing strategies, and experience memory. Through recursive co-evolution without additional human demonstrations, RoboEvo$^2$ improves standalone success from 11.0% to 84.1% and hybrid success from 30.4% to 90.5% on LIBERO-PRO, with similar gains and reduced agentic costs on RoboTwin C2R. Further experiments demonstrate autonomous task acquisition, cross-embodiment harness transfer, and 95.0% hybrid success on the Unitree G1-D. Our models and code will be publicly released.

## 19. One Model, Many Modes: Evolving System Prompt Ensembles for Creative Generation

**arXiv ID:** [2611.34760v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34760v1)

**作者:** Daiwei Chen, Anda He, Binwei Yao, Junjie Hu, Ramya Korlakai Vinayak

**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL

**摘要:**

Preference-aligned LLMs can exhibit mode collapse, concentrating their output distributions around a narrow set of high-reward responses. This limits creative generation, which requires quality, novelty, and diversity. Existing test-time methods often emphasize diversity, giving less attention to whether individual responses are both high-quality and novel. This distinction matters in practical use, where users may seek a single compelling, original response. We formalize creative generation as a multi-objective distributional optimization problem and introduce SEPO, a training-free framework for System Prompt Ensemble Optimization. On the 144 Large-QKP instances of Hochbaum et al. (2025), with the same 120-second limit, it improves 20 best known values and matches the other 124. An ablation study shows that the multi-ratio seeding and the pair exchange account for most of this performance.

## 150. LSRP*-LA: Scalable and Anytime Multi-Agent Path Finding for Large Agents with Asynchronous Actions

**arXiv ID:** [2611.33793v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33793v1)

**作者:** Dachuan Liu, Shizhe Zhao, Zhongqiang Ren

**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.MA

**摘要:**

Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) seeks collision-free paths for multiple agents from their start locations to their goal locations while minimizing path costs. Most MAPF algorithms assume that each agent occupies a single vertex and that the actions of all agents are synchronized. These assumptions may limit the use of MAPF planners in practice. Existing planners that get rid of both assumptions either handle only a few agents or cannot improve their solutions. Recently, a planner called LSRP* was proposed, which is scalable and anytime with asynchronous actions, but still limited by that each agent occupies a single vertex. This paper develops a new planner called LSRP*-LA for MAPF for large agents with asynchronous actions (LA-MAPF-AA), where an agent can occupy several cells. LSRP*-LA is scalable and complete, and it keeps improving solution quality after the initial solution is obtained until the runtime budget depletes. We analyze the properties of LSRP*-LA and test it against several baselines with up to 800 agents on various maps. LSRP*-LA can handle twice as many agents as baseline methods and its solution cost is about half that of recent synchronous planners for large agents.
