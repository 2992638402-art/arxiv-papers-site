## 1. ECMR: Evidence-Aware Deliberation Control for Multi-Agent LLM Reasoning

**arXiv:** [2610.17869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17869)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.MA
**作者:** Yuan Liu, Haoyue Peng, Fanliang Hu, Lei Huang, Wenli Cai

### 📄 论文摘要

Multi-agent Large Language Model (LLM) systems typically commit to a deliberation protocol before inference. A fixed protocol cannot allocate computation differently to resolved and unresolved instances, and it may mistake repeated support for independent confirmation. We study this failure as semantic false consensus: a confident group answer supported by narrow, duplicated, or correlated rationales. We introduce ECMR, an evidence-aware controller over frozen role-conditioned LLMs. At each step, ECMR summarizes answer concentration, support diversity and independence, contradictions, role behavior, verification history, and remaining budget, then selects whether to propose, challenge, verify, arbitrate, diversify, correct a role, or stop. We formulate this decision as finite-horizon budgeted control and derive sufficient conditions under which state conditioning, risk-triggered verification, and role correction improve cost-adjusted utility. Across a declared held-out evaluation of 9,000 records from MedQA, CaseHOLD, and FinQA, ECMR-Full increases pooled accuracy from 62.1% to 84.9%, reduces false-consensus rate from 40.7% to 17.0%, and raises evidence-support rate from 58.0% to 88.7%. State shuffling loses 11.7 accuracy points with only 0.7 fewer rounds, while harder traces shift from early stopping toward arbitration. ECMR-Full uses the largest token budget, yielding the strongest absolute reliability rather than uniformly superior token efficiency.

## 2. A Multi-Agent LLM System for Automated Tuning of Specialized Neural Network Models

**arXiv:** [2610.13663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13663)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.MA, cs.NE
**作者:** Ivan Moloshnikov, Dmitry Kunitsyn, Alexander Sboev, Maksim Skorokhodov, Roman Rybka et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Many machine learning approaches are two-level: an expert selects a class of methods, within this class the choice of key components is critical and usually manual. This paper addresses the automatic tuning of these components and proposes a framework transferable across models of different nature. The multi-agent large language model (LLM) framework has three roles---analyst, coder, and recorder~---with an experiment log and a sandboxed execution environment. Unlike systems that optimize the entire ML script or search architectures in a discrete space, the framework operates over an open but semantically narrow space: the agent generates code only for the specified components, leaving the overall network structure unchanged. The approach is validated in two directions: tuning a classification spiking neural network with correlation learning, and solving high-order differential equations with Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN). Across five tasks---spiking classification on MNIST and Digits, and boundary-value problems for the generalized nonlinear Schrödinger equation (GNLSE) and Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (KS) equations---the agent outperforms the baselines: classification accuracy is 13 percentage points (MNIST) and 6 percentage points (Digits) higher; the error against the analytical single-soliton solution is reduced approximately 2491-fold; the invariant-preservation error drops approximately 325-fold in the selected GNLSE run with a Gaussian perturbation and by more than 100-fold on average over repeated initializations; and the mass drift drops 14-fold for the KS problem with a Gaussian perturbation. The framework thus tunes key components on tasks of different nature without changing its internal structure.

## 3. When Extremists Enter LLM Societies: Investigating Extreme Behavior in Multi-Agent Systems

**arXiv:** [2610.19863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19863)
**分类:** cs.MA
**作者:** Guo Chen, Huaijin Xie, Ziwen Li, Reed Li, Yu Lu et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly serve as computational actors in multi-agent social simulations. While existing work emphasizes representative personas and average-case behavior, social science shows that persistent tail actors can disproportionately shape collective dynamics. We study this regularity in controlled LLM-based multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS). In these systems, such actors cannot be reduced to fixed scalar states: prompts, natural-language histories, and backbone generation mediate their behavior and influence. We first analyzed three properties of extremists in sociology and model the LLM extremist based on LLM-native perspective. We introduce EXAM, an EXtremist Agent Modeling framework that capable of configuring extremist agents for specific tasks and precisely controlling their population proportion, spatial distribution, interaction protocols, and measurement interfaces. Across four scenarios: congressional election, financial crisis, epidemic public opinion, and iterated prisoner's dilemma, we observe that small persistent tail groups produce measurable, backbone- and scenario-dependent collective shifts. EXAM shows how this social-science regularity manifests through language-mediated mechanisms specific to LLM-based social simulation.

## 4. Optimizing Team Behavior via Extremum-seeking Control in Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv:** [2610.18808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18808)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI, cs.RO, eess.SY
**作者:** Svar Rajankumar Patel, Kshitij Jerath

### 📄 论文摘要

In cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), agents must learn to coordinate their actions to achieve shared objectives. A key indicator of effective coordination is behavioral diversity, i.e., the agents' potential to enact distinct yet complementary control policies given the same set of observations. Although behavioral diversity can enable role specialization and richer joint behaviors, its appropriate level is highly context- and task-dependent and often costly to tune. We propose Adaptive Diversity Control (ADiCo), a two-timescale framework that adapts behavioral diversity online during training. On a fast timescale, Diversity Control (DiCo) regulates system diversity toward a target value. On a slower timescale, an Extremum Seeking Controller (ESC) adjusts this target using only mean episodic returns, without requiring an explicit model or analytical gradient of the diversity-performance landscape. We evaluate ADiCo on six configurations across two cooperative MARL tasks, where it maintains task-appropriate diversity levels: low diversity when homogeneous policies are beneficial and higher diversity when heterogeneous policies are required. Through this adaptation, ADiCo matches or exceeds the mean performance of DiCo (best grid-searched diversity target) and Unconstrained DiCo (diversity allowed to emerge without target regulation). These results demonstrate that incorporating classical feedback control into MARL training enables effective coordination by providing a systematic alternative to task-specific manual selection of the diversity target.

## 5. CLASHarness: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Conflict in Computer Use via a Controllable and Verifiable Generation Pipeline

**arXiv:** [2610.19128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19128)
**分类:** cs.MA
**作者:** Yuxiang Lai, Jike Zhong, Chengzhi Liu, Ming Li, Haonan Wang et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems to automate complex computer-use workflows, yet existing benchmarks mostly assume consistent, cooperative environments. In contrast, real-world collaboration rarely runs on consistent information: peer or sub-agents frequently report outdated states, issue contradictory instructions, or compete over shared permissions and files, forcing agents to check others' outputs and verify ground truth in the environment. Evaluating such conflict resolution remains challenging: manual authoring cannot scale across complex systems, while unconstrained LLM generation produces narrative drift, answer leakage, and unsatisfiable rubrics. We introduce CLASHarness (Conflict-Loaded Agent Scenario Harness), a controllable framework for generating verifiable multi-agent conflicts in computer use, with a benchmark of 600 multi-round tasks. By combining hierarchical scenario planning with an eight-step verified micro-pipeline, CLASHarness pre-commits decisive visual facts before rendering and compiles hybrid code and semantic graders directly from executed reference trajectories, guaranteeing every task is solvable by construction. Evaluating 15 frontier models on CLASHarness reveals three core bottlenecks. First, when sub-agent outputs conflict with the environment, weaker models blindly follow misleading information, whereas stronger models inspect visual evidence to verify ground truth. Second, resolving conflicts is much harder than finding them: while agents can spot inconsistencies, actively fixing system state drops task success significantly. Third, coordination protocols remain a universal bottleneck: even frontier models fail over 58% of closing actions, often forgetting to release shared locks or hand off tasks. CLASHarness provides a scalable, verifiable foundation for building reliable multi-agent systems.
