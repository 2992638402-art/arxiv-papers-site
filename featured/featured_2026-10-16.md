## 1. Reinforcement Learning-driven Diffusion Models: Language, Vision, and Beyond

**arXiv:** [2610.21539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21539)
**分类:** cs.AI
**作者:** Zhiyuan Ma, Jiabao Wei, Tianyu Wang, Hanqing Wang, Jiadong Pan et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Diffusion models (DMs) have steadily demonstrated outstanding performance in high-fidelity, controllable generation across diverse scenarios, following the "pretraining-finetuning" paradigm established in LLMs. Despite these advances, conventional DMs still face critical limitations in sophisticated reasoning, autonomous exploration of generation pathways, and adaptability to complex, human-aligned objectives. Inspired by RLHF's success in LLMs, RL has recently emerged as a pivotal post-training mechanism to iteratively refine DMs through reward-driven trajectory optimization. This paper presents the first comprehensive review of RL-driven DMs and an empirical analysis of how RL reshapes the diffusion process towards experience-centric, goal-directed generation. We propose a novel technical panorama elucidating RL's multifaceted roles in diffusion-based language, multimodal, robotic, and scientific models, with a granular focus on diffusion trajectory optimization and reward engineering. Through extensive experiments on multiple task-specific benchmarks, we systematically evaluate representative methods to intuitively highlight the advantages of different RL post-training strategies. Furthermore, we reveal and dissect three crucial bottlenecks that currently hinder the real-world deployment of RL-driven DMs: reward design fragility, exploration-exploitation trade-off in high-dimensional generation spaces, and training instability under sparse rewards. Based on these perspectives, we provide a principled roadmap for the integration of RL into DMs and outline promising future research directions for robust, interpretable RL-diffusion systems. We also construct a public repository to continuously track the latest developments in this rapidly evolving field: https://github.com/MAIR-Lab-HUST/Awesome-RL-for-Diffusion-Models.

## 2. When Extremists Enter LLM Societies: Investigating Extreme Behavior in Multi-Agent Systems

**arXiv:** [2610.19863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19863)
**分类:** cs.MA
**作者:** Guo Chen, Huaijin Xie, Ziwen Li, Reed Li, Yu Lu et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly serve as computational actors in multi-agent social simulations. While existing work emphasizes representative personas and average-case behavior, social science shows that persistent tail actors can disproportionately shape collective dynamics. We study this regularity in controlled LLM-based multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS). In these systems, such actors cannot be reduced to fixed scalar states: prompts, natural-language histories, and backbone generation mediate their behavior and influence. We first analyzed three properties of extremists in sociology and model the LLM extremist based on LLM-native perspective. We introduce EXAM, an EXtremist Agent Modeling framework that capable of configuring extremist agents for specific tasks and precisely controlling their population proportion, spatial distribution, interaction protocols, and measurement interfaces. Across four scenarios: congressional election, financial crisis, epidemic public opinion, and iterated prisoner's dilemma, we observe that small persistent tail groups produce measurable, backbone- and scenario-dependent collective shifts. EXAM shows how this social-science regularity manifests through language-mediated mechanisms specific to LLM-based social simulation.

## 3. SSR: Structural Signal Retriever for Scalable Agent Discovery in LLM-Orchestrated Multi-Agent Systems

**arXiv:** [2610.21113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21113)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI, cs.IR
**作者:** Giancarlo Maldonado Cárdenas, Carlos A. Astudillo, Nelson L. S. da Fonseca

### 📄 论文摘要

Large-scale multi-agent ecosystems require mechanisms for discovering suitable agents without exposing entire registries to a large language model (LLM) orchestrator. The challenge is not only to retrieve relevant Agent Cards from a growing registry, but also to maintain a compact candidate context, since each additional candidate increases prompt size, inference cost, latency, and the risk of exceeding the model context window. We formulate this problem as candidate-context scalability and propose the Structural Signal Retriever (SSR), our deterministic and training-free retrieval method designed for Agent Card discovery. SSR derives structural lexical signals from capability-bearing Agent Card fields, weights them using inverse document frequency (IDF), and uses an Inverted Signal Index (ISI) for selective candidate scoring. We evaluate SSR using a synthetic agent benchmark, a public MS MARCO adaptation, and end-to-end LLM orchestration. At 1,000 agents, SSR reduces LLM input by 98.8% and orchestration latency by 70.9% relative to full-catalog orchestration. On MS MARCO, IDF improves retrieval effectiveness within the structural representation, while the ISI preserves retrieval effectiveness and reduces explicitly scored passages by 96.8%. End-to-end experiments show that candidate pre-selection maintains a compact candidate context as registry size grows and avoids the operational failures observed with full-catalog orchestration. These results support SSR as an Agent-Card-native, traceable retrieval mechanism for candidate-context scalability.

## 4. Athanor: An Agent-Native System for Autonomous Post-Training Research

**arXiv:** [2610.21055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21055)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.MA
**作者:** Yufa Zhou, Yichen Wang, Jiakun Fan, Ziqi Shu, Zhichao Liu et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Recursive self-improvement, in which AI systems build stronger AI systems, is one promising route to superintelligence. We study a weaker form that can be measured today: frontier agents post-training a small raw base model with public data, as a test of their research potential. We build Athanor, an open agent-native system for autonomous post-training research (AutoPostTrain) on real GPUs. We give the agents full research autonomy, low-friction engineering tools, and efficient starter code. The agents carry out the research themselves: they read datasets and evaluation results, build their training data, write and modify training algorithms, tune hyperparameters, and analyze the results to decide what to try next. They work in a sandbox and cannot use external teacher models. Athanor's services supply frozen evaluation, managed jobs, decontaminated data, a persistent research record, and a held-out suite the agents are unaware of. In a 104-hour campaign, a team of four Claude Opus 5.5 agents, each on an eight-GPU node and given no research advice, improves Qwen3-4B-Base from 15.9 to 67.3 on a six-task development suite and from 9.2 to 48.2 on a held-out suite, against 66.5 and 47.9 for the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. To our knowledge, this is the first demonstration that a team of frontier agents can autonomously post-train a raw base model into a cross-domain generalist model that matches or surpasses the official instruct version on average. The campaign's full record shows that the agents reach this level by recombining known methods and each other's models: SFT on public reasoning data, RL with a rollout change one agent introduced, weight merges across agents, and rounds of self-distillation. With execution handled, today's agents already have the engineering and research ability to conquer this task. We hope Athanor provides a foundation for scaling autonomous AI research.

## 5. From Training to Escalation: Forensic Analysis and Local Recreation of RL/RLHF-Induced Privilege Escalation in AI Agents (CVE-2026-65616)

**arXiv:** [2610.20437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20437)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI, cs.CR
**作者:** Alicia Colmenero-Fernández

### 📄 论文摘要

Mid-2026 reporting on autonomous AI evaluation incidents claimed that frontier large language model (LLM) agents escaped server containment through emergent coordination and cloud infrastructure attacks. This paper contextualizes those claims through forensic analysis and containerized recreation of both the Highly Persistent Internal Model (HPIM) training-case conditions and the Phase One privilege escalation targeting internal package repositories during OpenAI's ExploitGym evaluations. We recreate the configuration of the OpenAI-provided training example and reproduce the cryptographic verification bypass (CVE-2026-65616) in isolated repository servers, where misconfigured systems enabled token forgery and privilege escalation through basic HTTP requests, Python, and JavaScript. Crucially, the training-example conditions and the Phase One (and later) misconfigurations share the same exploitable patterns. Our analysis explains how Zero Trust Architecture principles neutralize this shared attack vector. Contemporary AI risks derive directly from training and deployment conditions in ambivalent learning environments lacking adequate containment measures.
