## 1. GARDEN: Generalist Autonomous Reward Design Through Evidence Navigation for Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv:** [2610.37926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37926)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.MA, eess.SY
**作者:** Dogan Urgun

### 📄 论文摘要

Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) enables teams of agents, such as robots and autonomous vehicles, to learn coordinated behavior, but what such teams learn is largely determined by their reward function. Because task success alone provides too little guidance for learning, intermediate rewards are usually designed by hand, and this effort must often be repeated whenever the environment or the team changes. Large language models (LLMs) can automate the effort by writing reward functions as code, yet the resulting rewards are typically designed and tested in a single setting. To enable their reuse across settings, GARDEN (Generalist Autonomous Reward Design through Evidence Navigation) is proposed, in which one reward program is developed for a family of related scenarios and transferred, unchanged, to scenarios that were not available during its design. During development, environment code, training records, and agent behavior are investigated by an LLM reward engineer, and draft rewards are tested in trial training runs, while the learning algorithm and the rule for selecting the final reward are held fixed. On unseen scenarios of the Overcooked and SMAX benchmarks, task returns were increased by 58.2% and 52.5% over the default rewards on two kitchen layouts, and the mean win rate in two combat scenarios was raised from 51.0% to 89.9%. Under matched candidate and probe budgets, this exceeded the 84.2% and 79.2% obtained with fixed diagnostic feedback and Eureka-inspired reward reflection. These results show that a single autonomously engineered reward can be reused effectively across related cooperative scenarios.

## 2. MRC-MMAgent: Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Role Collaboration in Multimodal Tool-Using Agents

**arXiv:** [2610.39324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39324)
**分类:** cs.CV
**作者:** Shengqin Wang, Jie Jin, Xiaofan Li, Wentao Yan, Yu Cheng et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Multimodal tool-using agents augment vision-language models with access to external information and capabilities, enabling them to solve knowledge-intensive and visually complex tasks that cannot be addressed using visual context alone. However, existing reinforcement learning pipelines largely train single-role agents to search and answer using terminal rewards, overlooking how humans inspect retrieved evidence, reformulate queries, and revise conclusions during search. Consequently, they fail to optimize this self-corrective process over complete interaction trajectories. We propose MRC-MMAgent, a multi-role collaborative reinforcement learning framework that incorporates trajectory assessment and continuation directly into the executable agent loop. First, the framework establishes a multi-role collaborative loop that integrates solving, inspection, and continuation, enabling the agent to acquire evidence through tools, evaluate the preceding trajectory, and resume problem solving when the current answer is insufficiently supported. Second, we introduce a role-coupled learning objective that combines task success, interaction validity, and successful correction with full-trajectory policy optimization across the decisions generated by all roles. Extensive experiments show that, using only the original baseline training set without constructing additional datasets, our method consistently outperforms the corresponding base models and achieves leading or highly competitive performance across benchmarks.

## 3. Seeing and Imagining as One: Unified Video Generation and 4D Perception for Embodied World Modeling

**arXiv:** [2610.39232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39232)
**分类:** cs.CV, cs.RO
**作者:** Haitang Feng, Zhuoman Liu, Zhennan Wang, Sheng Wang, Juelin Li et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Embodied world models should not only imagine plausible futures, but also perceive the geometric, semantic, and motion structure of the evolving world. We present GenSight, a unified embodied world model that brings future video generation and dense perception into a single pretrained video diffusion transformer. GenSight uses a unified spatiotemporal representation for video, depth, embodiment segmentation, and first-frame-relative 2D flow. Built on a shared DiT backbone, GenSight uses lightweight task modulation to predict all modalities within the same architecture. All tasks are jointly optimized within a unified velocity prediction space. At inference, GenSight supports two complementary modes: multi-step future video generation with aligned dense predictions and single-step dense perception from an observed video. By unifying modality representation, model architecture, and prediction space, GenSight avoids separate modality-specific pipelines and couples appearance, geometry, semantics, and motion in a shared representation. It promotes cross-modal consistency and allows dense perception to benefit from the spatiotemporal priors learned through video generation. Trained on embodied manipulation data spanning multiple robot embodiments and human-hand interactions, GenSight demonstrates strong performance in both video generation and dense 4D perception, highlighting the effectiveness of unifying seeing and imagining for embodied world modeling.

## 4. Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Global Climate Policy Cooperation

**arXiv:** [2610.37870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37870)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.GT, cs.LG
**作者:** Anne Moll-Elsborg, Oskar Bohn Lassen, Francisco Camara Pereira

### 📄 论文摘要

International climate policy requires coordination among countries whose mitigation decisions impose local costs but generate global benefits. Classical game-theoretic and integrated assessment approaches can study these incentives, but typically rely on prescribed behavioural assumptions or equilibrium concepts. Recent work has therefore explored Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning as a way to model climate-policy cooperation as an emergent outcome of repeated strategic interaction. We extend an existing MARL climate framework with a multi-stage negotiation mechanism in which agents propose agreements, accept or reject commitments, and face penalties for non-compliance. This allows us to study how agreement design affects learned mitigation behaviour, compliance, and climate outcomes. In an idealized setting with four homogeneous agents, we find that non-binding agreements do little to overcome free-riding, while moderate non-compliance penalties improve cooperation and reduce warming. However, excessively strong penalties discourage ambitious commitments, revealing a trade-off between enforcement and participation. We position the framework as a methodological contribution for testing agreement design rather than a predictive model of real negotiations.

## 5. Ask to Evolve: A Unified Harness for Lifelong Embodied Navigation with Hybrid Action Space and Persistent Memory

**arXiv:** [2610.39446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39446)
**分类:** cs.RO
**作者:** Kai Sheng, Liuyi Wang, Jinlong Li, Haojie Dai, Chengju Liu et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Giving embodied agents the freedom to act autonomously, initiate interactions, and understand their surroundings through experience is a step toward bringing them to life in the physical world. Realizing this vision requires action, interaction, and memory to work together, so that each encounter can inform current decisions and contribute experience for future tasks. We present EvoLife, a unified navigation harness that enables a multimodal embodied agent to choose how to move, when to ask, and what experience to carry forward. Its hybrid action space supports waypoint navigation, local discrete adjustments, pixel-based navigation, and object-directed navigation. Active interaction allows the agent to seek clarification or assistance when it encounters ambiguity or difficulty, while persistent spatial memory binds observations and dialogue to places, retaining useful experience to guide subsequent visits. We evaluate eight benchmarks across four task families: vision-language navigation, object navigation, interactive instance navigation, and continuous multi-task navigation. With an average of only 1.87 active interactions per episode, our method achieves a first-visit success rate (SR) of 100% across four 100-episode sets spanning vision-language navigation and object navigation. Complete-sequence SR reaches 98% on 50 LHPR-VLN sequences and 94% on 50 GOAT-Bench sequences. In the R2R revisit setting, success weighted by path length (SPL) increases from 82.23% to 93.31% with retained experience. Multi-day physical navigation further demonstrates exploration, proactive help-seeking, and revisiting remembered places. Together, these results advance the vision of bringing embodied agents to life through autonomous action, proactive interaction, and the accumulation and reuse of experience.
