## 1. Agentic World Modeling for Agent Test-time Scaling without Environment Replica

**arXiv:** [2610.40927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40927)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.MA
**作者:** Wendi Li, Huseyin A. Inan, Wei-Ning Chen, Yanzhi Chen, Chetan Bansal et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Test-time scaling (TTS) improves large language model agents by sampling and selecting among multiple candidate trajectories, but common realizations assume that these trajectories can be executed in parallel. This assumption breaks in realistic deployments, where enterprise workspaces and cloud services are shared and persistent, costly or infeasible to replicate, and actions may introduce irreversible side effects or cross-trajectory interference. Existing generative world models avoid direct execution, but typically compress environment state into a single prompt, limiting their ability to reliably simulate transitions under partial observability. Instead, we introduce Agentic World Modeling (AWM), which reformulates next-observation prediction as a multi-turn evidence-acquisition task. For each hypothetical action, an agentic simulator adaptively queries the real environment through safe read-only interfaces, reasons over the returned evidence, and uses executable computation to construct the resulting observation without mutating shared state. Integrated with trajectory-level TTS, AWM enables parallel exploration over a single shared environment. Across $τ$-bench, AppWorld, and OfficeBench, AWM outperforms a single-shot simulation baseline by 40.07% on average, and surpasses a trained world-model baseline by 12.36% on average. These results establish interactive, read-grounded simulation as a practical foundation for safe and scalable agentic test-time scaling in shared, non-resettable environments.

## 2. MAPLE: A Multimodal Mobile Agent with Playbook-Learned Experience

**arXiv:** [2611.01351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01351)
**分类:** cs.LG, cs.MA
**作者:** Jun Wang, Aurora Wei, Lucas Spangher, Ming Zhang, Eugene Ie et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Autonomous agents powered by vision-language models (VLMs) show great potential in navigating mobile graphical user interfaces (GUIs) to complete complex tasks. However, existing paradigms face fundamental scalability bottlenecks: fine-tuning requires massive high-fidelity datasets or prohibitive training time and compute, while gradient-free approaches rely on custom static heuristics or costly trial-and-error trajectory exploration where feedback is deferred until the end of execution. To address these limitations, we introduce MAPLE (Multimodal Agent with Playbook-Learned Experience), a novel gradient-free multi-agent framework that enables dynamic, step-wise experience sharing and execution-time prompt evolution. Unlike traditional action-reflection architectures that depend on costly, post-hoc self-reflection, MAPLE enables agents to cross-reference and distribute runtime insights in real time at each operational step. This dynamic exchange eliminates blind trial-and-error looping and steers agents along direct, grounded interaction paths. Extensive evaluations on the AndroidWorld and MobileWorld benchmarks demonstrate that MAPLE achieves state-of-the-art performance on complex, long-horizon mobile tasks, while proving scalable and adaptable across novel environments.

## 3. SCOUT: Planning under Occlusion via Object-Centric World Model Rollouts

**arXiv:** [2611.01600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01600)
**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV
**作者:** Amir Hossain Raj, Xuesu Xiao

### 📄 论文摘要

Robots manipulating in cluttered spaces must often plan from partial views where foreground objects occlude the target, surrounding geometry, and usable free space. Reactive policies often miss these hidden constraints, while exhaustive search becomes computationally prohibitive in dense clutter. We present SCOUT, an occlusion-aware planning framework that bridges high-level deliberation with grounded physical rollouts. SCOUT leverages an object-centric scene representation to reconstruct hidden geometry and derive a relational planning state capturing blocker hierarchies and accessibility corridors. To navigate the vast action space in clutter, an LLM proposes structured action candidates which are then rigorously evaluated via Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) using a learned action-conditioned world model. Evaluations on 'Reveal' and 'Placement' tasks show that SCOUT outperforms Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, embodied agents, and 3D world models, with significant gains in long-horizon tasks requiring reasoning over occluded geometry. We further validate the framework's robustness through real-world robotic manipulation in a cluttered shelf environment.

## 4. From Single-Agent Self-Discovery to Multi-Agent Adaptation: Scaling Laws of Partner Feedback and Convention Rigidity in Meta Reinforcement Learning Algorithms

**arXiv:** [2611.01742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01742)
**分类:** cs.MA
**作者:** Haomin Luo, Ersha Li

### 📄 论文摘要

A key milestone toward Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) is replacing hand-crafted optimizers with meta-learned update rules (such as Disco103 and OPEN) that discover how agents learn. Yet when a self-discovered RL model trains with adapting teammates, does co-evolution sustain capability gains or trap agents in brittle conventions? Across 1,800+ runs in continuous collective robotics (VMAS) and discrete coordination (Overcooked), we establish five laws of multi-agent adaptation. First, four-cell counterfactual partner swaps expose an evaluation illusion: apparent team progress often masks focal regression (+1.066 team gain concealing -0.119 focal loss) and inflates transfer by +0.649 to +1.210. Second, a 288-run physical-coupling sweep ($α\in [0, 1.5]$) reveals a linear scaling law ($β= +0.255$, $p = 0.000488$, Spearman $ρ= 1.0$): live reciprocal feedback keeps Disco103 invariant (AUC 0.297--0.322), whereas severing feedback triggers a monotonic collapse (-0.172 to -0.490), with OPEN establishing a sensitivity spectrum (Disco103 &gt; OPEN &gt; MAPPO). Third, extending navigation training eightfold (1.05M steps) enables Disco103 to overtake MAPPO (+0.087 team return, +0.133 bank gain), while decoupled training boosts cross-partner completion by +10.16% (56.25% vs. 46.09%). Fourth, in Overcooked, MAPPO's self-play conventions induce a 28.13% cross-partner collision rate, whereas Disco103 cuts collisions to 12.77% and achieves 3.33x higher bank return (9.96 vs. 2.99; net gain +6.43), further improved by decoupled meta-adaptation (+1.39 with scripted partners). Finally, perturbation pulses ($p=0.00098$), auxiliary entropy diagnostics (8.485 nats), and 48 multi-lifetime meta-evolved rules ($L_2 \in [2.11, 4.91]$) trace these dynamics to partner-gradient blindness and auxiliary drift, informing multi-agent self-discoverd algorithms.

## 5. ORION: An Outcome-Driven LLM Advisor for RL-Based Multi-Domain 6G Slice Placement

**arXiv:** [2610.41563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41563)
**分类:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.MA, cs.NI
**作者:** Mahmoud S. Mayaleh, Fetia Bannour, Nour-El-Houda Yellas

### 📄 论文摘要

Deploying 6G network slices across administrative domains requires an online decision for each arriving request: which domain hosts each virtual network function, how traffic crosses domain boundaries, and whether the slice is admitted. This joint decision problem is NP-hard under continuous arrivals. Experiments on a parametrized family of multi-domain topologies show that no fixed planning strategy dominates across all regimes: the performance ranking reverses between topology classes, and feasible placements exist for most rejected requests but remain undiscovered. These findings identify the plan layer as the decisive performance lever. We introduce ORION, a hierarchical orchestrator in which two LLM agents translate service intents into structured requests and generate abstract placement plans. A cooperative multi-agent RL coordinator selects the cross-domain partition, treating the plan as a persistent soft prior, while per-domain agents place the functions. ORION main contribution lies at the plan layer: an outcome-driven episodic memory that records, for each completed attempt, the network-condition profile, the proposed plan, and its verified feasibility outcome. The memory feeds verified successes and labelled failures back to the planner, thereby closing the adaptation loop using deployment feasibility rather than a scalar reward assigned to the planner's own proposals. To isolate the effect of the plan quality, we evaluate the planner on held-out topology families while keeping the downstream selection and execution mechanisms fixed. Under this controlled setting, the proposed memory improves admission by $4.6$--$11.5$ percentage points over the same planner without memory, while importance-based retention accounts for most of the improvement over a first-in-first-out store.
