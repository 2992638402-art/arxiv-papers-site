1. Macro Control and Capability Evaluation of Black-Box World Models: A Two-Regime Framework for LLM-Agent Markets

arXiv: 2611.19311 分类: cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG, cs.MA 作者: Zhiyang Niu, Chuanjie Li, Huayuan Shen, Mengmeng Zhao, Jiajia Zhang

📄 论文摘要

Large language model (LLM) agents can be organized into multi-agent markets that act as world models, mapping macro information to emergent macro dynamics while the evaluator observes only macro inputs and outputs. This paper separates a model's capability into two stages that a single aggregate score cannot separate: information simulation, from macro information to micro behavior, and macro emergence, from micro behavior to the macro outcome. We introduce a soft macro-control method that announces a public reference path day by day without touching individual decisions, orders, or matching, and a two-regime evaluation framework that measures both stages from macro observations alone. In the reference-only regime the symmetric true and flipped references share a common level R, which measures realization under the announced reference, while their paired gap B measures structural bias; in the information-injection regime external information dilutes the reference, so only the true-flipped gap Delta remains interpretable. On five single-index LLM-agent markets, announcing a target path shifts the realized path toward the announced target on both announced references under both information conditions. Relative to the no-reference path, the tracking score is higher by 0.306 and 0.415 without news and by 0.183 and 0.329 with news; within the stock/cash scan structural bias is lowest at 70/30 (B=0.042); realization peaks at a 3:7 technical share (R=0.894 versus 0.358 for technical-only); and Delta is 0.0341 with fundamental data and 0.0487 with news. Both stages use the same two observable scores, each read in the regime where it is interpretable.

2. Infrastructure-Based Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning with Attention for Highway Ramp Merging

arXiv: 2611.20078 分类: cs.LG, cs.MA, eess.SY 作者: Chenhao Tong, Steffen Müller

📄 论文摘要

On-ramp merging is a typical safety and throughput problem on highways. Without proper coordination, human drivers and self-interested autonomous vehicles may make risky manoeuvres and cause accidents or congestion. With recent advances in connected autonomous vehicles (CAVs) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), vehicles can be trained to coordinate for safe and efficient merging. However, in dense traffic, forming an effective observation of the traffic state is challenging. For example, observing only the neighbouring vehicles may lead to locally optimal decisions, while observing the entire traffic can introduce much irrelevant information. In this paper, we propose a transformer-based MARL vehicle control model to address these problems. A roadside infrastructure controller receives the states of all CAVs via vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication and sends control commands back to them. An attention-based observation encoder is designed where each vehicle can filter the relevant vehicle information from the global traffic state. The proposed model is evaluated in SUMO against three baselines: a model that observes only the neighbouring vehicles, a model that encodes the global traffic information, and the rule-based SUMO model. The results show that our model achieves the highest traffic flow, safety and smoothness. Furthermore, in a four-hour simulation with dynamic traffic demand, our model successfully avoids traffic jams, while other baseline models fail at high traffic flow. We also show that our model remains effective when human-driven vehicles are present. We further show that our model, trained in ideal communication conditions, can still remain collision-free and maintain throughput under realistic V2I communication errors. Finally, we visualise the relevance weights that an ego vehicle assigns to other vehicles in multiple traffic situations.

3. Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

arXiv: 2611.18543 分类: cs.AI, cs.MA 作者: Tony H. Zhang, Connor Mattson, Daniel S. Brown

📄 论文摘要

Demonstrations are an effective way to accelerate single-agent reinforcement learning (RL), but extending this idea to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is non-trivial as it is difficult and often impossible for a single person to teleoperate multiple agents simultaneously. Round-Robin Behavior Cloning (R2BC) addresses this by letting a single demonstrator control one agent at a time while the others execute their current policies. However, the resulting imitation policies are limited by the quality of the demonstrations. We propose Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (IBMARL), a framework that extends Imitation Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning (IBRL) to cooperative teams and uses R2BC to train the imitation policies. At each time step, every agent receives one action proposal from its imitation policy and one from its RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects among the per-agent combinations of the two. A gated imitation penalty, a soft Q-filtered behavior cloning loss, regularizes each RL policy toward its imitation policy only where the critics value the imitation action more, so that after training each agent executes its RL policy alone, without the centralized critics. On four cooperative multi-agent RL benchmarks, IBMARL surpasses its imitation policies $2.6$-$4.2\times$ faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks, is the only method to surpass it on the other two, and exceeds the demonstrator itself on two of the four. With real human demonstrations IBMARL still surpasses imitation learning alone, and does so more than twice as fast as the best baseline.

4. SAFER: Navigating the Exploration-Exploitation Dilemma in World-Model-Based Planning for Autonomous Driving

arXiv: 2611.20107 分类: cs.RO, cs.AI 作者: Yansong Qu, Zilin Huang, Zihao Sheng, Sikai Chen, Samuel Labi

📄 论文摘要

An autonomous vehicle must foresee the consequences of its actions, especially in the rare, time-critical situations where crashes happen. A world model offers this foresight, but only within the coverage of its training data, which poses an exploration-exploitation dilemma. Within this coverage a planner can hardly improve on the data, and beyond it the model's errors mislead it. Logs of competent driving rarely contain the failures that would widen the coverage, and agents that learn from interaction gain them only by failing themselves, without being shown what to do instead. We propose SAFER, a framework that learns offline from failures that others make, judged by an expert, and keeps learning online from its own driving. Offline, partially trained agents and scripted, recoverable perturbations Stage failures in simulation, and an expert Annotates every visited state with its action and value, so that much of what the planner explores is covered before it drives. In planning, a chunked latent world model Foresees the consequences fast enough for real-time use, and a risk-aware sampling-based planner Evaluates candidate maneuvers by their return minus a bounded collision penalty. Online, a loop continuously Refines the model wherever this driving leaves the coverage of the offline data. In closed-loop evaluation against the compared methods, SAFER reaches the highest route completion and reward per step on the CARLA Leaderboard routes, 0.95 and 0.77 from a privileged bird's-eye view, and the highest episode reward in MetaDrive, 53.4 in regular and 49.0 in safety-critical traffic. Project page: https://ys-qu.github.io/safer-website.

5. JEPA4R: Embodied JEPA World Models with 4D Point Planning

arXiv: 2611.19771 分类: cs.RO, cs.CV 作者: Jiarui Dong, Zhiheng Liang, Yanjun Li, Zeyu Zhang, Heejeong Nam et al.

📄 论文摘要

Joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) world models predict future embeddings instead of pixels and plan in latent space. Two challenges remain: 2D images leave the metric geometry that manipulation acts on implicit and entangled with appearance, and what these models imagine stays hidden, as they are mostly judged in simulation by short-horizon latent planning. We present JEPA4R, a JEPA world model that observes single-depth-camera point clouds, predicts and plans in their latent space, and acts through a policy head on its frozen latents; we call this 4D point planning. A decoder on the frozen world model shows what it imagines as 4D point clouds, with a mean point error within 1.1 cm over 200 steps. Under the policy-learning protocols of OGBench and CortexBench, JEPA4R leads pixel JEPA models on OGBench Cube-Single, Cube-Double, Cube-Triple and Scene, and ranks first on MetaWorld and Adroit Relocate with 98.4% and 88.0%, above the runner-up, R3M, at 96.0% and 65.3%. Trained only in a digital twin, JEPA4R and its policy heads run zero-shot on the real arm with both a gripper and a dexterous hand.
