## 1. Making the World React Realistically: A Closed-Loop Interactive World Model for Autonomous Driving

**arXiv:** [2611.22691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22691)
**分类:** cs.RO, cs.AI
**作者:** Jia Hu, Yang Chang, Panqu Wang, Zhijun Fu, Chang Liu et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

World models (WMs) generate driving scenarios beyond recorded logs, enabling the training and evaluation of autonomous driving systems in unseen interactions. However, existing WMs struggle to ensure both behavioral realism and visual fidelity, since they overlook the relationship between vehicle interactions and the ego visual observations. We propose a closed-loop interactive WM that couples behavior modeling and visual rendering. First, imitation-learned background-vehicle (BV) policies are fine-tuned with multi-agent reinforcement learning in the closed loop against an end-to-end ego planner that reacts to the rendered images, so that their interaction behaviors match those in real logs. Second, the viewpoints exposed by these interactions guide a per-Gaussian diagnosis of each 3D Gaussian vehicle asset, which attenuates view-dependent colors on under-observed Gaussians and fills never-observed surfaces with generated Gaussians, while preserving the observed appearance. Third, signal lights are rendered according to the driving intentions of the vehicles, by an editor that learns the light states unobserved for a vehicle through counterfactual fine-tuning with a light-state classifier. On long-tail closed-loop scenarios, our method reduces the divergence from real-world interaction behaviors by 85% and improves novel-view vehicle PSNR by 2.0dB over the state-of-the-art WM. The significance of BV light signaling in WMs is shown, as the rendered signal lights conveying BV driving intentions indirectly impact ego driving behaviors in a quarter of the scenarios.

## 2. OAK-Mind: Zero-Training Mental Health Change Inference from Passive Sensing via Knowledge-Grounded Multi-Agent LLMs

**arXiv:** [2611.22248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22248)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI, cs.HC, cs.LG
**作者:** Yishu Ji, Zhihan Jiang, Will Ke Wang, Yuang Fan, Anind K. Dey et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Wearable and smartphone sensors capture behavioral signals associated with mental health, yet inferring mental health outcomes from these signals has traditionally relied on supervised models requiring labeled data and offering limited insight into their predictions. Large language models (LLMs) offer new opportunities to reason over behavioral evidence, but existing LLM-based approaches to passive sensing have largely continued to rely on task-specific fine-tuning or sensor-specific pretraining. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{OAK-Mind}, a zer\textbf{O}-training, multi-\textbf{A}gent, \textbf{K}nowledge-grounded LLM pipeline for inferring mental health changes from passive sensing data. The pipeline organizes sensing data into behavioral dimensions including sleep, physical activity, location mobility, and smartphone use, and orchestrates specialized agents grounded in mental health literature to interpret longitudinal behavioral changes. It synthesizes their assessments and applies questionnaire-based symptom rating to infer changes relative to an individual's baseline assessment with human-readable rationale grounded in observed behavioral patterns and domain knowledge. Across datasets and prediction tasks, while without task-specific training, OAK-Mind achieves comparable or better performance than the most predictive supervised baselines trained on additional labeled samples. It also demonstrate consistent improvement upon direct prompting across four tested LLM backbones. Ablation analyses support the benefits of combining behavioral interpretation with symptom rating, distributing analysis across domain-specific agents, and incorporating domain knowledge. Our findings highlight the potential of knowledge-grounded, multi-agent LLMs for training-free inference of mental health changes from passive sensing data.

## 3. Can Decision Models Replace LLMs in Real-World Contact Center Classification and Judging Tasks?

**arXiv:** [2611.22949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22949)
**分类:** cs.CL
**作者:** Md Tahmid Rahman Laskar, Xue-Yong Fu, Shashi Bhushan TN

### 📄 论文摘要

Contact center systems often need to make bounded decisions, such as identifying a caller's intent, retrieving the knowledge-base article that answers a query, assessing agent compliance, or judging the output of AI models used in production. Decision models return a typed answer and its probability directly, making them a promising cost-effective alternative to generative Large Language Models (LLMs) in such bounded conversational tasks. However, their effectiveness in real-world contact center applications remains unclear. In this paper, we compare nine decision models against thirteen generative LLMs in zero-shot settings across eight contact center use cases: six classification tasks and two judging tasks. Our experiments demonstrate that many decision models can match the performance of frontier LLMs on contact center classification, and API-based closed decision models achieve this at the lowest per-request cost among the API models we evaluate. We further observe that their confident predictions are more accurate, enabling selective routing in real-world production scenarios. Across classification tasks, the main weakness of most decision models is abstention: they rarely choose the no-answer option, which accounts for most of their gap to the strongest LLMs (although Gemini-Decision-1 is the notable exception in this regard). We also find that the judging quality of decision models drops sharply for smaller models, and they are also more prone to position bias. In short, our findings provide practical guidance for selecting decision models based on abstention behavior, confidence, and deployment cost, rather than overall accuracy alone.

## 4. Addressing Exogenous Variability in Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv:** [2611.22067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22067)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.LG
**作者:** Seongmin Kim, Woohyeon Byeon, Jiwon Jeon, Seungyul Han, Youngchul Sung

### 📄 论文摘要

Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) often fails under exogenous variability such as shifts in opponents or environment regimes that cannot be controlled by the team but alter the transition dynamics. We formalize this challenge as Exogenous Dec-POMDP (ED-POMDP), in which an episode-level exogenous regime, selected independently of the team, indexes the dynamics, and the global state is partitioned into components that are directly responsive to the current team action and components that are not directly action-responsive at the current step. This formulation exposes mediated influence: while team actions do not directly update the latter at the current step, they can shape their later evolution through induced changes in the former. Based on this, we propose LEICA, a CTDE-compatible algorithm that learns history-conditioned context representations for both components and shapes policy updates using agent-wise, influence-weighted counterfactual intrinsic rewards. Across SMAX benchmarks with opponent strategy shifts, LEICA consistently improves both training performance and generalization to unseen opponents' strategies over existing baselines, supporting the usefulness of influence-weighted shaping under exogenous train-test regime shifts.

## 5. Multi-Agent Memory Inconsistency: Where Factual Conflicts Disappear in LLM Agents

**arXiv:** [2611.22245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22245)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.MA
**作者:** Hejia Zhang, Zhongming Yu, Yimin Ding, Wei-Ting Chen, Hengjia Yu et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Multi-agent delegation lets LLM systems distribute evidence collection across workers with separate contexts. This creates a consistency challenge: facts retrieved by workers may be missing from the evidence available to the lead for a joint conclusion. We introduce a benchmark and evidence-flow profiler for studying multi-agent memory inconsistency through controlled factual conflicts in Harvey LAB document-review tasks. Pooled across three models with comparable generation spending, requiring delegation increases retrieval of both conflicting claims from 50.2% to 68.7% relative to single-agent execution, yet conflict detection falls from 16.2% to 13.2%. Within this setting, workers report both claims for only 35.2% of retrieved pairs, and pooled matched comparisons show higher detection than single-agent execution when one worker reads both claims but lower detection when the claims are split across workers. Worker-to-lead handoffs are a central target for improving conflict detection in multi-agent LLMs.
