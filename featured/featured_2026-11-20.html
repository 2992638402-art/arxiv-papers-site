1. CoSlice: Coordinated Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for 5G Network Slicing Allocation

arXiv: 2611.23909 分类: cs.NI, cs.LG, cs.MA 作者: Pengxin Wang, Nakjung Choi, Jingdi Chen

📄 论文摘要

Radio Access Network (RAN) slicing is a shared resource allocation problem: each slice seeks to satisfy its Service Level Agreement (SLA) with minimal radio-resource consumption, while all slices compete for a limited resource budget. This creates both an intra-slice trade-off between SLA assurance and resource efficiency and an inter-slice conflict over shared resources, calling for specialized yet coordinated control. In this work, we present CoSlice, a coordinated multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for inter-slice physical resource block allocation in Open Radio Access Network (O-RAN). CoSlice uses preference-conditioned slice agents for fast resource allocation and an LLM-based coordinator to adapt their objectives using global network observations at a slower timescale. We formulate the problem as a multi-objective partially observable stochastic game (mo-POSG), establish the existence of a Preference Correlated Equilibrium, and derive a sublinear regret bound for the learning procedure. We implement the algorithm by using a two-stage training procedure that consists of multi-objective pretraining and joint optimization. We build a network slicing evaluation environment using ORANSlice and its radio-frequency simulator (RFsim), supporting realistic protocol-stack execution and application traffic across multiple slices. Experimental results show that CoSlice reduces both average and tail SLA violations compared with other centralized and multi-agent baselines under diverse slice workloads.

2. Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation

arXiv: 2611.26444 分类: cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV 作者: Yuzhi Huang, Jie Wu, Fanding Huang, Junchao Lin, Weichen Zhang et al.

📄 论文摘要

Embodied intelligence aims to let robots perform complex physical tasks with human-like adaptability and reliability, which requires three complementary capabilities: history-based cognitive reasoning, precise and timely physical execution, and the composition of reasoning and execution through environmental feedback. Yet existing benchmarks either evaluate reasoning and execution within the same tasks or assess them separately without their composition, so they reveal little about whether a failure comes from reasoning, execution, or their composition. We introduce Mind2Act World, a diagnostic benchmark of 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five real-world application domains. Each task has three difficulty levels and two scene settings (Clean and Random), yielding 180 configurations. The tasks form three views: Mind tests decisions based on past observations, Act tests precise and timely execution, and Mind2Act combines both in closed-loop tasks where action outcomes guide later decisions. Comparing the three views shows whether a system lacks a capability or fails to compose capabilities it already has. A diagnosis harness further attributes the failed episodes of three representative systems to Mind, Act, or their composition and summarizes each system in a profile card. Our evaluation of multimodal agents, vision-language-action models (VLAs), and world-action models (WAMs) shows that competence in a single view does not carry over to Mind2Act tasks. Even the strongest agent succeeds in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes but in only 12.0% of Mind2Act episodes. The strongest agent retains task state but struggles with sustained contact, whereas end-to-end policies execute practiced motions but retain little task state. These findings point to composing reasoning and execution through environmental feedback as the main open problem.

3. EmbodyAgent: Post-Training Embodied VLM Agents via Distillation, SFT, and RL

arXiv: 2611.26196 分类: cs.RO 作者: Wei Dai, Jinkun Liu, Jiayi Chen, Han Liu, Wenbo Ding et al.

📄 论文摘要

Vision-language models (VLMs) can act as closed-loop robot-use agents (RUAs) by writing and executing control code through tool interfaces. However, turning an open-weight VLM into a high-performing embodied agent requires a complete post-training stack: out-of-the-box open VLMs fail completely at interactive tool contracts, strong frontier teachers are accessible only as black-box endpoints without exposed weights or token probabilities, and supervised imitation alone plateaus under rollout distribution shift. We present EmbodyAgent, an end-to-end post-training framework with three tightly coupled pipelines: (1) a black-box data synthesis pipeline that queries a strong teacher under a forced think-then-code contract and filters trajectories by task success; (2) an SFT pipeline that distills the explanation-and-program targets into an open-weight 9B VLM with both vision and language encoders trainable; and (3) an online RL pipeline that optimizes student-generated episodes via group-relative advantage with a runtime-error-penalized outcome reward. All three pipelines share one primitive-free Python execution tool exposing only raw visual observation and seven-dimensional end-effector control, with no action decoder and no interface shift across stages. Under a strictly separated evaluation protocol on ten LIBERO Spatial tasks (disjoint state splits, greedy test-time decoding, training-only safeguards), held-out success on 100 test states rises from 0/100 (base 9B VLM) to 14/100 (after SFT) and 84/100 (after RL), while training rollout success climbs from 8.4% to 87.7% and median model calls per episode drop from 36 to 11. We further identify a self-reinforcing runtime-error mode induced by outcome-based RL on executable code, and establish a failure-category monitoring and sampling-side curation protocol that stabilizes training.

4. Attacks and Defenses in Decentralized Multi-Agent Systems with Hop-by-Hop Task Decomposition and Delegation

arXiv: 2611.25056 分类: cs.CR, cs.MA 作者: Matthew D. Laws, Maria Lazou, Alina Oprea, Cristina Nita-Rotaru

📄 论文摘要

Decentralized multi-agent LLM systems enable independently administered agents, each with its own operator, model, memory, and configuration, to collaborate on complex tasks without centralized coordination. Agents autonomously decompose tasks, execute subtasks and delegate remaining work to peers, enabling scalability and eliminating single points of failure. However, decentralization also introduces significant security challenges, as no single entity has complete visibility into task execution or the authority to enforce correct behavior across agents. In this paper, we systematize threats from malicious agents in decentralized multi-agent systems and propose mitigation techniques. We first design and evaluate attacks against integrity, availability, and confidentiality, demonstrating that a single malicious agent can cause up to 100% task failure, inflate token consumption and runtime by up to 7.7x and 3.2x, respectively, and exfiltrate up to 94% of relevant prior tasks from benign agents. We then present a cryptographic protocol that produces an irrefutable verified execution trace of every action taken in the system and develop an auditor that analyzes these traces to detect and attribute malicious behavior. Evaluations on HiddenBench using gpt-6.1-sol and Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 show that our protocol incurs negligible latency overhead, while the auditor detects and correctly attributes 98% of attacks, including those that comply with the protocol.

5. Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI)

arXiv: 2611.26553 分类: cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.MA 作者: Mengxiao Wang, Mahbub Alam, Kalyan Nakka, Nitesh Saxena

📄 论文摘要

Agents based on large language models (LLMs) can now delete files and send payments on behalf of their users. News media reports routinely describe failures of these agents (e.g., Replit's coding agent deleting a production database), but a report often only says what went wrong and rarely (in)validates the incident or finds its root cause. Incident catalogs collect these reports, and benchmarks record whether agents fail, but neither replicates an incident nor tests the cause its report might identify. In this paper, we present ROGUE-AI (Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation), a multi-agent framework that closes the above gap and rebuilds each reported incident offline together with a matched control that lacks only the blamed cause, and runs agents on both. Our results suggest that ROGUE-AI is able to reproduce the reported harm in 89% of its 108 attempted replications. Agents built on five current GPT, Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek models made the reported mistake in 18% to 33% of runs, against at most 1% without the blamed cause. Of the 72 incidents with real-world harm, 62 had no underlying attacker, and in these, agents did what their task asked for but missed an ordinary hazard, such as a database shared with the live site. Replications without an attacker caused more harm than replications of prompt injection for all five models. Defenses against prompt injection look for injected instructions, but these replications contain none. Further, a warning after each tool output that names the hazard and its consequence lowered harm for all five models, whereas a note of the same length that does not mention the hazard left most of the harm in place. Built from real incidents, the ROGUE-AI replications form a reusable audit framework that vendors can rerun before each new release.
