## 1. Learning Inside Types: Epistemic Typestates for Safe Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

**arXiv:** [2611.27631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27631)
**分类:** cs.AI
**作者:** Lorenzo Bacchiani, Mario Bravetti, Giovanni Pau, Roberto Girau

### 📄 论文摘要

Type disciplines make programs safe by construction: components checked against each other's types compose into programs that cannot go wrong in the ways the types exclude. Types suit reinforcement learning: a type constrains what an agent may choose, not how it chooses, so the guarantee holds for every policy a learner produces. We introduce epistemic typestates, a type discipline for safe multi-agent reinforcement learning. An agent's type is a protocol whose states record its beliefs, the worlds it cannot rule out from its own observations and actions, and permits only actions that are safe in every world of the belief, whatever the others do within their public types. Agents learn inside their types, which are built and checked one agent at a time and run without communication. We prove type soundness and team safety, give a synthesis that is sound and complete, build types from guards when worlds are infinite, and show that shields are a special case. All proofs are mechanised in Rocq. On nine benchmarks, no typed learner causes a safety violation, and task performance does not differ significantly from that of two recent multi-agent shielding methods. Where a contract-based method fails at five and ten agents, types for ten agents are built in under a second. In the two continuous benchmarks, Lagrangian learners cause thousands of violations in every training run.

## 2. Bringing AI Assistance to the Beznau Nuclear Power Plant: A Hierarchical System of LLM Agents for Reactor Monitoring

**arXiv:** [2611.27556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27556)
**分类:** cs.MA, cs.AI
**作者:** Wenqi Zhou, Leonardo Baldo, Edwin Kolbe, Manuel Arias Chao

### 📄 论文摘要

Beznau Unit 1 in Switzerland, in operation since 1969, is the most experienced nuclear reactor still running worldwide. Five decades of incremental modernization have left its monitoring systems, alarm logs, variable catalogs, technical specifications and investigation reports in separate silos that were never designed to be queried together. As a result, investigating a core instrumentation anomaly means slow, manual gathering of knowledge scattered across databases, documents, and experienced colleagues. We present a hierarchical multi-agent assistant built on large language models (LLMs) to support these investigations and reduce the need for tedious manual searches in SAP systems. An orchestrating agent breaks down an engineer's question and hands the parts to specialized sub-agents, each with read-only access to one source. Domain expertise is written as natural-language procedures that plant engineers can read, review and revise. The system is strictly advisory, with every answer traced back to its sources. We also report organizational lessons from introducing agentic AI into a culture built on safety, traceability and conservative change. Beyond the technical architecture, the Beznau case study examines how an assistant can be introduced into a setting where evidence must be traceable, changes carefully reviewed, and operational decisions retained by plant personnel, paving the way for the responsible use of agentic AI in industrial plant monitoring.

## 3. EvoTER: Evolving Embodied Agents via Tool-Use Experience and Reflection

**arXiv:** [2611.27797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27797)
**分类:** cs.RO
**作者:** Yingting Zhou, Yuyin Zhou, Yongliang Wang, Haoran Li, Dongbin Zhao

### 📄 论文摘要

Long-horizon embodied tasks require agents to ground instructions in changing observations, coordinate heterogeneous tools, and recover from failures that can propagate through downstream operations. Although existing LLM-based agents retain experience and refine reusable skills, successful local repairs do not by themselves establish complete-task gains over the current shared procedure library. We present EvoTER, an experience-driven framework that enables embodied agents to self-evolve tool-use skills without updating model parameters. EvoTER operates through a three-role evolution loop: Explorer records tool-use trajectories; Diagnoser distills successful and failed executions into skills for tool selection, parameter computation, postcondition checks, and recovery; and Validator retains candidate revisions only when they improve complete-task success in fresh sessions. In simulation, EvoTER improves overall task success from a pre-evolution baseline of 53.60% to 80.40%. In real-world experiments, simulation initialization reduces the attempts required to acquire short-horizon skills, while continued real-world evolution improves long-horizon success from 20% to 80%-100% across tasks and enables the evolving skill library to incorporate new tool functionality introduced after simulation development. Project page: https://evoter-project.github.io/

## 4. SOAR: Training-Free Rank-Consistent Rollout Compression for Latent World Model Planning

**arXiv:** [2611.27639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27639)
**分类:** cs.RO, cs.CV
**作者:** Anqi Li, Kai Liu, Linghe Kong, Yulun Zhang

### 📄 论文摘要

Latent world models plan by rolling out hundreds of candidate action sequences through a transformer predictor, so a single decision can take tens of seconds. Existing accelerations prune spatial tokens as in vision-language models, yet a sampling-based planner uses predicted costs only to rank candidates, so a rollout can be compressed freely as long as the ranking survives. We propose SOAR, a training-free method that adapts rollout compression to each planning decision. At every decision, SOAR probes a few candidates under several token budgets and keeps the smallest budget that preserves their ranking, so it compresses as far as the current decision allows, without task labels. A closed-loop study of more than 80,000 episodes fixes the rest of the rollout: the first step, which alone reads a real observation, stays exact, and the context frame is cached rather than recomputed. On seven navigation and manipulation tasks with frozen checkpoints, SOAR matches dense-level planning success at 2.3x speedup and retains 98% at 2.5x. Our code is available at https://github.com/starrywiki/SOAR.

## 5. The Dual-Tier Sentiment Framework: Scalable Multimodal UX Attribution in Web Search

**arXiv:** [2611.27899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27899)
**分类:** cs.AI, cs.LG
**作者:** Yi Ding, Gaurav Kumar, Rui Min, Isabella Ye, Bhavika Goyal et al.

### 📄 论文摘要

Sustaining user experience (UX) quality in large-scale web search requires scalable evaluation. Traditional surveys are slow and bottleneck rapid A/B testing, while automated metrics often lack the granularity to diagnose visual-spatial UX anomalies. To address this, we introduce the Dual-Tier Sentiment Framework, a novel industrial architecture for automated UX attribution. Tier 1 (Macro System) acts as a scalable user sentiment regression detector, utilizing a fine-tuned encoder-decoder LLM (based on Gemini XS) on telemetry features. Through multi-task regularization and contextual feature engineering, this tier achieves a +9.8% PR-AUC gain on over 500,000 held-out records, matching baseline recall on all validated regressions with $\sim$20% relative precision gain among the 12 held-out experiments. In production, it reduces measurement latency from weeks to hours and cuts survey overhead by over 40%. Tier 2 (Micro System) provides granular diagnostics by employing a Multimodal LLM (Gemini 3.0 Pro) to natively assess visual renderings and spatial layout of web result blocks, quantifying "User Toil" as the estimated effort required to reach a satisfactory result. Enhanced by RL-based automated prompt optimization and an AI judge for win/loss patterns, the MLLM yields a $&gt;$6.5% F1 score improvement, offering deep insights into content quality and layout friction. Ultimately, this framework enables scalable, accurate, and diagnostic UX attribution, processing O(100k) live experiment queries daily.
