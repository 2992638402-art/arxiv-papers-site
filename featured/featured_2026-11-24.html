1. Persuasion from Within: Model-Based Insider Attacks in Multi-Agent LLM Debate and Consensus

arXiv: 2611.28660 分类: cs.AI, cs.MA 作者: Bangyan Ju, Xiaolin Sun, Yibin Hu, Zizhan Zheng

📄 论文摘要

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems for collective decision-making, such as multi-agent debate and consensus games, in which agents exchange messages and update their answers over multiple rounds and a resolution mechanism aggregates them into a final outcome. Most multi-agent LLM frameworks assume that every participant shares the system objective. In practice, however, a malicious agent can join as a legitimate member of the group and persuade the other agents toward a hidden adversarial goal. We formalize such attacks as a sequential decision-making problem in which the insider seeks to degrade the utility of the collective outcome. We propose a model-based insider attack that learns to persuade a group of agents: the insider first learns a world model of the benign agents from offline trajectories and then uses it both to train a persuasion policy and to plan its messages. In two case studies involving a numerical consensus game and a multi-agent debate, the learned insider is the most effective and the stealthiest attack we evaluate, outperforming both prompt-based and trained LLM persuaders from prior work. The attack also transfers to other target models, larger groups, and unseen datasets, and remains effective under existing Byzantine-resilience defenses. The learned insider also enables a new defense: adversarially training benign agents on the attack data largely removes both our attack and the strongest prior persuasion attack. These results show that combining world models with reinforcement learning and planning is an effective route to both attacking and hardening language-based multi-agent systems.

2. From Local Errors to System Failures: Modeling and Benchmarking Error Propagation in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

arXiv: 2611.31063 分类: cs.MA, cs.AI 作者: Wenlong Yu, Bingxu Lu, Qilong Wang, Qinghua Hu, Qianru Sun

📄 论文摘要

In multi-agent systems (MAS) built on large language models (LLMs), errors can propagate through intermediate messages and compromise final answers. However, outcome-based evaluation obscures how agents amplify, attenuate, or correct these errors. We propose Response-Aware Error Propagation among Agents (REPA), a structural model that connects measured local responses to system-level error propagation. REPA separately estimates input assembly and agent processing across multiple error channels, while theoretical analysis relates local modeling errors to endpoint prediction error. Experiments span three Qwen base models, six MAS workflows, and ten knowledge and reasoning settings in ErrPropBench-10. REPA achieves a mean condition-level Spearman correlation of 0.84 across base models, compared with 0.47 for direct regression. Controlled experiments also demonstrate its utility for targeted recovery. REPA-guided retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) improves accuracy over no retrieval by an average of 19 percentage points with 16% additional tokens. By linking local agent behavior to system susceptibility, REPA provides an interpretable foundation for studying error propagation and developing targeted optimization in MAS.

3. Can Multimodal Fusion MakeSense for Embodied Vision in Degraded Sensing?

arXiv: 2611.30836 分类: cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.RO 作者: Cheng-Ru Chou, Shih-Yu Lai, Pei-Lin Chiang, Shou-Po Tsai, Jing-Zhe Huang et al.

📄 论文摘要

Embodied vision systems rely on complementary sensors to perceive and act as conditions change. Even models that accept arbitrary sensor subsets can exhibit negative fusion: adding a sensor increases prediction error. We propose MakeSense, a multimodal fusion framework that preserves sensor-specific evidence in private streams and combines information through shared bottleneck tokens. Training pairs full and subset views of the same degraded observations, penalizing higher error with more sensors. Where geometry permits, the distance readout uses range measurements alone, while the rest of the prediction draws on all available inputs. Paired tests across five datasets reveal helpful and harmful sensor additions. MakeSense reduces negative fusion on MM-Fi. With lightweight adapters and per-dataset training, the same design improves UAV localization on MMAUD, cooperative 3D detection on V2X-Real, few-shot pose transfer to RELI11D, and command prediction on Humanoid-Everyday, where a world model trained on the frozen core's outputs forecasts future fused states more accurately than a linear baseline at longer tested horizons. MakeSense supports embodied sensing across human activity, aerial systems, robotics, and autonomous driving.

4. WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency

arXiv: 2611.30989 分类: cs.RO 作者: Xinyang Zhou, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang

📄 论文摘要

AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce \textbf{WorldLeague}, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of \textbf{60 simulated robot tasks} by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in \textbf{WorldSeek}, rearrange objects in \textbf{WorldShape}, and adapt to changes in \textbf{WorldShift}. Tying these goals to a single control interface limits which AI systems can be compared. In WorldLeague, vision-language models (VLMs) choose targets and motions, while vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs) use their native controls. For each of six capability dimensions, we measure how much progress a system makes toward its goals (Interactive Score) and how often it achieves them (physical success rate). Together, these measures form the system's \emph{Physical Agency Profile}. Among 13 representative AI systems, Claude Fable 5.2 achieves 13 of the 60 goals and earns the highest mean Interactive Score (28.58/100). Fable leads in four dimensions, while GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tie for first on rule-following tasks. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion. 5. The Evaluation Substrate Matters: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Serving Substrates

arXiv: 2611.28671 分类: cs.MA, cs.AI, cs.LG 作者: Haoyuan Zheng, Dongting Li, Hancheng Ye, Qinsi Wang, Mingyuan Ma et al.

📄 论文摘要

Multi-agent systems (MAS) usually coordinate heterogeneous models and tools to solve complex tasks, creating a growing need for reliable evaluation. However, a multi-agent workflow that is fastest in one serving environment can lose its advantage in another, even when its models, prompts, and task remain fixed. This makes a benchmark result conditional on how the workflow is executed. We introduce MASSys, a benchmark for measuring this dependence in multi-agent large language model (LLM) systems. MASSys executes the same candidate-task pairs across serving substrates and load regimes, using five workload families spanning software repair, research synthesis, financial analysis, interactive control, and diagram generation. It records task quality alongside delivery, latency, cost, resource pressure, and failure evidence. In a controlled motivation diagnostic, the Star workflow falls from first to fourth in mean-latency rank under KV-cache pressure, while Chain rises from fourth to first. The substrate sweeps further show that increasing load creates long runtime tails and timeout pressure. In several task families, quality among evaluated outputs changes less than effective quality over submitted jobs: the system delivers fewer usable results even when the surviving outputs remain competitive. Tool and runtime traces reveal that these outcomes involve both model serving and non-model execution. Together, the results motivate evaluating MAS designs over explicit operating conditions and reporting which tasks are delivered, how well they are solved, and where execution breaks down.
