## 📝 摘要

We analyze distributional drift in nonstationary weighted risk minimization by decomposing the excess risk into estimation, drift, and approximation components. The drift error is commonly bounded through discrepancy-type quantities that control changes of the risk over classes of predictors. We instead relate the drift error directly to the movement of the best predictor within the model class and analyze its behavior in parametrized regression models. Our analysis shows that discrepancy-type quantities can be overly conservative: unlike our oracle drift, they may scale at a larger order than the resulting excess-risk contribution and can respond to changes in the risk surface that do not affect the optimal predictor. For linear regression, we characterize this gap sharply and combine the resulting drift bounds with estimation guarantees. We further extend the analysis to nonparametric regression.
