## 📝 摘要

Population health stratification frameworks, such as the Italian DM77, organize patients by clinical need and care pathways but offer limited guidance on which care changes to prioritize under constrained resources. We present PROMETHEUS (Proactive Resource Optimization through Multi-treatment Effect-based Targeting in Healthcare Environments Using Stratification), a framework that formulates population health stratification as a multi-treatment causal ranking problem. Its objective is to order eligible patient-treatment opportunities by expected incremental benefit rather than obtain perfectly calibrated individual treatment-effect estimates. Each clinically admissible care intensification is compared with maintaining the patient's current pathway. Treatments are learned jointly to produce a globally comparable ranking across patients and care profiles, within domains sharing a reference condition, outcome, follow-up horizon, and benefit scale. This separates clinical need, treatment benefit, and resource-constrained allocation. PROMETHEUS combines doubly robust causal signals with a Siamese neural ranking model and contrastive representation pretraining. The shared representation captures similarities across care transitions, supporting prioritization under capacity constraints. Synthetic experiments and semi-synthetic experiments using real administrative covariates from ASL Benevento evaluate ranking quality, allocation value, robustness, and failure modes. The results support the framework's ability to recover generated benefit orderings and prioritize care opportunities in the evaluated settings. PROMETHEUS provides a preclinical approach to actionable stratification; validation with observed treatment-outcome data is required before clinical use.
