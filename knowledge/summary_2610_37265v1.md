## 📝 摘要

Detecting harmful content in Arabic is challenging because of dialectal variations and the semantic overlap between different harm categories. This paper presents a two-stage approach for classifying Arabic user prompts into seven categories of harmful content. First, GLiNER is used to extract harm-related entities from each prompt. The extracted entities are then added to the original text and passed to a fine-tuned MARBERT classifier for final classification. The approach combines explicit entity information with the contextual understanding of a pretrained Arabic language model. The system was evaluated on the development set of the ArGuard 2026 shared task. The results achieved a macro-F1 score of 0.886 and an accuracy of 0.897, demonstrating promising performance in fine-grained harmful content detection. The system reported an accuracy of 0.793 and Macro-F score of 0.735 on a blind test set for subtask 1. For subtask 2, it achieved an accuracy of 0.770 and Macro-F score of 0.754. The analysis also showed that distinguishing between related categories remains challenging. These findings suggest that integrating NER with Arabic language models is a promising approach to harmful content detection.
