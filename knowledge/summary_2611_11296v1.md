## 📝 摘要

Mobile robots operating in homes need reliable 3D perception of nearby furniture and surrounding structures, yet recovering this information on both sides of the robot's path with a single forward-mounted millimeter-wave radar remains challenging because of near-field wavefront curvature, viewpoint-dependent target visibility, and limited elevation diversity. This paper presents a motion-assisted framework for reconstructing structures on both sides of a robot's trajectory. Our near-field model relates scene reflectivity to stepped-frequency multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) observations across frequencies and robot positions using spherical paths from transmitter (Tx) to scatterer to receiver (Rx). Bilateral Tx combining provides wideband directional weighting, while sliding-aperture back-projection coherently focuses the measurements using near-field propagation distances. Top-K power fusion combines local images with different visibility. The reconstruction provides lateral-forward support for multi-layer height extraction, followed by guarded adaptive height refinement using the original MIMO channels. Experiments in controlled, office/study, and corridor environments produce mean lateral-forward localization errors of 4.65, 5.63, and 5.98 cm, respectively, with peak-to-background ratios (PBRs) exceeding 13 dB. The framework recovers bilateral 3D scattering structure from one forward pass using a single radar, without relying on learned representations of the scene.
