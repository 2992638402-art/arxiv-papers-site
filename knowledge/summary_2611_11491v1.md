## 📝 摘要

Many studies of animal vocalizations require clean recordings, but animals are usually recorded in the field, with background noise from weather, vehicles, and other animals. Animal vocalization denoising is challenged by the scarcity of clean training data and the extreme diversity of animal vocalizations. Classical spectral gates leave residual noise, especially without voice activity detection (VAD), while detector-free neural denoisers designed for bioacoustics remove less noise. We show that a general-purpose source separation foundation model, SAM-Audio, removes more noise than either of these types of methods, when used without training and prompted with time spans from a simple animal VAD. To turn this heavy method into a practical tool, we distill it into CallCleaner, a 19M-parameter denoiser that needs no detector and runs ~90x faster than real time on a laptop CPU. We find that SI-SDR has limited utility in our setting as it penalizes regenerated output and conflates noise removal with call fidelity, a trade-off our separate metrics reveal. CallCleaner leaves 11 dB less noise around the call than Biodenoising, the leading neural bioacoustic denoiser with public weights, and among fast, CPU-capable systems it pushes the Pareto frontier of noise removal vs. call fidelity, without requiring a detector. We release code and weights for CallCleaner, which enables fast, private, local denoising of single-caller recordings without specialized hardware.
