## 📝 摘要

The growing complexity of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has fragmented the literature, obscuring underlying architectural choices behind diverse implementations. This fragmentation makes it difficult to evaluate critical modeling dimensions in isolation, identify how components affect the resulting system dynamics, and compare, reuse, or combine contributions across the literature. This paper proposes a modular perspective for modeling, analyzing, and implementing MARL systems. We decompose them into eight functional modules: agents, environments, learning, reward modeling, training and execution modes, communication, modeling of other agents, and evaluation criteria. For each module, we clarify its role and review representative approaches. We instantiate this decomposition in MARLKIT, a framework whose modules are explicit and interchangeable. MARLKIT demonstrates how the proposed perspective supports transparent experimental assumptions and the controlled variation and combination of MARL mechanisms. Experiments illustrate its ability to prototype, compare, reuse and combine modules while making differences between configurations explicit. Overall, the paper provides a structured perspective on MARL modeling and implementation, offering a common language for making modeling choices explicit, supporting clearer descriptions of MARL approaches, and supporting more controlled experimentation through the systematic isolation and combination of MARL mechanisms.
