## 📝 摘要

Volumetric CT super-resolution (SR) models are commonly trained on synthetic or physically acquired pairs of low-resolution (LR) and high-resolution (HR) volumes. However, synthetic pairs miss real acquisition effects, whereas registered physical pairs retain inter-scan noise, artifact, and microstructure differences---causing direct voxelwise regression, and even naive synthetic-real mixing, to oversmooth fine porous structures. We introduce the Physically-anchored Implicit Network (PIN) with Real-Pair Dual-Anchor learning, which decouples physical-pair supervision into two complementary roles. The HR anchor teaches fine structure from registered HR through pair-conditioned spectral replay, while the LR anchor constrains the prediction from acquired LR via affine-calibrated low-pass projection. Together with exact synthetic supervision in one shared volumetric implicit model, PIN learns from both physical scans without forcing the acquired-LR reconstruction to match registered HR voxel by voxel. Inference requires only acquired LR. Across 30 external baseline configurations on real CT, PIN achieves the lowest full-volume LPIPS, reduces average symmetric surface distance by 42.3-49.5% and bone-volume-fraction error by 52.2-53.4% relative to the three strongest baselines for each metric, and lowers the erased reference-pore volume fraction from 37.3-72.9% to 10.4%. Code, pretrained models, and evaluation scripts will be publicly available.
