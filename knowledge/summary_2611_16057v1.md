## 📝 摘要

Can the functional architecture of a Transformer attention head be given a biological construction? More specifically, could a suitable group of pyramidal columns in posterior temporal language regions (PTLRs) constitute the biological construction of a neuromorphic mechanical realization of an attention head, under a mathematical instantiation that need not coincide with its standard Transformer instantiation? This paper investigates this hypothesis by examining possible correspondences between the functional organization of attention heads and neurophysiological mechanisms operating within and across pyramidal columns involved in human language processing. Our analysis builds on a mechanical-structural description of Transformer attention heads as systems of simple neural networks linked through output-input and output-weight interconnections. We examine the functional organization of an attention head and the input-dependent transformations it produces, and ask how the corresponding processing relations could be constructed in biological neural tissue. We then identify candidate neural mechanisms of signal transmission and modulation within and across pyramidal columns that may jointly support such a construction. On this basis, we propose that a suitably organized group of pyramidal columns in PTLRs could constitute a biological construction of a neuromorphic mechanical realization of an attention head. Whether the required functional organization actually exists in PTLRs remains an open empirical question. The hypothesis does not require the biological system to reproduce the standard mathematical instantiation of Transformer attention: the same functional architecture may be mechanically realized and mathematically instantiated in a different way, consistent with its biological construction.
