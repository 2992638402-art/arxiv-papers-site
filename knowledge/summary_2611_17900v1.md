## 📝 摘要

Vision-language models (VLMs) often exhibit overconfidence, providing firm answers even when visual evidence is insufficient. A prominent case is referential ambiguity in Visual Question Answering (VQA), where multiple entities in an image are plausible referents of a question. Addressing such cases requires recognizing when ambiguity is present: ambiguous questions can be handled by asking for clarification or enumerating plausible referents, while unambiguous questions should receive a direct answer. Current VLMs struggle with this distinction, often defaulting to a single interpretation. To investigate whether activation steering can serve as an inference-time mechanism to elicit such behaviors, we introduce AmbiSteer, a lightweight framework to selectively elicit ambiguity-aware behavior. Across three VLMs, we find that steering alone has limited and model-dependent effects, while prompting is generally more effective. Combining prompting and steering substantially increases clarification and enumeration behaviors, but also elicits them on unambiguous cases. AmbiSteer addresses this using a linear classifier to predict input ambiguity and condition the steering direction accordingly, increasing the proportion of inputs handled appropriately from 59.0% to 77.0% on average across models. Our work sets the ground for lightweight approaches to reduce model overconfidence in uncertain multimodal settings.
