## 📝 摘要

We study probabilities of causation in nondeterministic causal models, where mechanisms may remain intrinsically stochastic even after all latent variables are fixed. Focusing on binary treatments and outcomes, we develop a generic identification program that represents observational and interventional data as moment constraints on latent stochastic mechanism types and computes minimal/maximal values of counterfactual quantities via constrained optimization. Within this framework, we derive sharp analytic bounds on the probabilities of causation under several assumptions tailored to nondeterministic models. Resulting identification regions are often more informative than those attainable under deterministic alternatives. We illustrate the practical relevance of these results in a case study on personalized treatment decisions and experiments on semi-synthetic datasets. Our results clarify how classical bounds relate to nondeterministic causal semantics, generalizing previous works and providing a unified route from qualitative structural restrictions to computable partial identification regions.
