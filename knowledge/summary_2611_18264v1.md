## 📝 摘要

Answer continuation often returns a diagnosis already stated in unfinished clinical reasoning. We study this behavior in MamaReason, adapted from dense Gemma 4 31B, on MamaBench's 217 paired maternal and child-health presentations. An explicit-commitment extractor identifies a diagnosis in 75 of 94 failed RL first passes and 75 of 93 failed SFT first passes. The continuation matches that diagnosis in 67/75 RL cases and 58/75 SFT cases, using diagnosis strings alone. Reading these commitments without additional generation recovers 30 of the 40 RL pairs restored by continuation under the archived correctness labels. Recurrence controls distinguish routine mentions from repeated answer selection. An offline second-commitment trigger measures the tradeoff between earlier readout and paired accuracy. The resulting decomposition separates diagnostic content already present in a trace, completion of an accepted answer, and paired diagnostic correctness. For clinical language-model evaluation, these are distinct outcomes that become visible when the same reasoning trace is examined through multiple readouts.
