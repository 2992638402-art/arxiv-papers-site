## 📝 摘要

Experiments near vehicle handling limits must keep three questions apart: what a maneuver requests, what the closed-loop system executes, and whether the task is completed. We present DynLimit, a simulation platform that organizes this process end to end. Scenarios are composed from road, friction, obstacle, and reference objects; planners and controllers attach through a shared observation and control interface; a vehicle adapter runs either a license-free four-wheel model or an external dynamics solver; and synchronized records feed task evaluation, replay, and response inspection. The platform carries a task-oriented evaluation method that pairs a prescribed reference demand with a sampled passing set and event-aligned diagnosis. Across eight task families, three friction environments, and three reference-assisted baseline chains we executed 450 runs: 360 nominal, 18 locally refined, and 72 with prescribed initial offsets. Method differences depend on the task family, equal aggregate pass counts arise from different passing cells, and local refinement narrows two sampled transitions without locating a continuous boundary. Two case comparisons show why sideslip or tire-utilization peaks cannot replace task constraints. The three views therefore stay distinct in every reported run: an outcome can be traced from the prescribed demand, through the executed response, to the task rule that decided it. Code is available at https://github.com/Yang2581/dynlimit
