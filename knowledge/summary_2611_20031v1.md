## 📝 摘要

We investigate when adaptive tool selection helps identify a shared equation across clients with different input coverage. Query-ATLAS (query-conditioned Agentic Tool-based Learning for Aggregating Symbolic Equations) uses an LLM to guide symbolic search from client errors and previous proposals in a simulated distributed system. A four-architecture comparison reveals task-dependent opportunities: relative to fixed PySR-LLM, validation acceptances rise from 3/12 to 7/12 on CRK3 and from 11/12 to 12/12 on oscillator2, while MatSci3 favors the fixed workflow. Across 72 settings in a post-hoc six-task subset of eight development tasks, Query-ATLAS achieves 64 validation and 64 in-distribution passes, versus 62 and 59 for the fixed workflow; both pass 47/60 eligible out-of-distribution tests. The fixed workflow retains lower median errors and cost. Broader query coverage emerges as a promising benefit, motivating task-aware tool selection and client-aware fitting for scientific agents.
