# Mandarin Wh-Indefinites Follow Nonveridicality in Multilingual LLMs, Unlike English any

## 📝 摘要

Multilingual LLMs learn mostly from English. Do they carry English polarity licensing over to other languages? English any is licensed by downward-entailing (DE) contexts, Mandarin wh-indefinites such as shenme 'what/something' by nonveridical ones, and the two classes come apart: perhaps licenses shenme but not any, while only licenses any but entails existence. We test this dissociation in a pre-registered study of twelve open LLMs (0.5B-9B, four families), using 48 matched bilingual frames in 21 contexts and a log-probability difference-in-differences normalised by negation. All 11 models that pass the measurement gate license Mandarin wh-indefinites under possibility, necessity and evidential adverbs, at 0.42-2.33 times the strength of negation, while English any gets nothing in the same contexts. An exploratory extension to Qwen2.5-32B shows the same. The English side is weaker: any under only, first and regret is licensed but at a fraction of negation, so only 3 models show the full double dissociation in sentence probabilities. When prompted to choose between sentences, most gated models show it. In exploratory directional ablations, a direction that carries English licensing, held-out classes included, does not reach Mandarin licensing in most models. The models do not simply apply the English rule to Mandarin.
