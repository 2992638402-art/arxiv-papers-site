## 📝 摘要

Heterogeneous LLM debate is often attributed to collective reasoning that creates capability beyond any member. In the matched panels we study, the aggregate gain is dominated by capability transfer: a peer supplies task competence and receivers absorb a measurable fraction. Across nine matched directions, honest benefit and first-exchange adversarial exposure open when the peer has a positive same-task advantage, and honest gain is closely described by the empirical decomposition $ΔA_d^{\mathrm{hon}}\approxκΓ_d^+ρ_d$, the product of that advantage and the receiver's reception. Two fixed-receiver directions provide held-out validation without refitting: reception remains stable within uncertainty as a larger peer advantage produces a larger gain. A matched, single-direction open-answer MuSiQue scope probe reaches the negative-gap side of the same gate. Qwen-7B trails the Gemma-3-12B receivers by 10.78 points; despite 62.50% reception, it produces only +1.17 points of controlled first-exchange gain. Reception cannot substitute for positive aggregate peer advantage. When an influential peer is assigned an adversarial role, the same influential peer slot removes the borrowed gain and can push the panel below homogeneous debate. Across all five full-reference settings, the best observed majority correctness does not exceed the influential member's best observed accuracy, despite unused complementary answers. Heterogeneous debate is therefore a measurable transfer decision with a measurable failure mode, not a benefit conferred by diversity alone.
