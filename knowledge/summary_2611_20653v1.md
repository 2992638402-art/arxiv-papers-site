## 📝 摘要

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are often evaluated by converting model outputs into discrete answers. The conversion protocol determines which scores become predictions and can strongly influence the reported results. We introduce RAMP (Readout Aware Multimodal Protocol), a systematic framework that compares direct yes/no token logits, surface-form scores, and typed or reversed A/B interfaces under fixed model checkpoints and paired visual questions. On 500 POPE adversarial questions, typed versus bare readout changes accuracy by $-20.2$ percentage points for SmolVLM-256M, $-16.4$ for TinyLLaVA-1.1B, and $+1.8$ for Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Paired transitions reveal extensive prediction changes even when the net accuracy difference is small: 79 of 104 binary ChartQA items change correctness for SmolVLM-500M, despite a $+1.0$-point net difference. We further analyze prompt wording, candidate forms, option position, cross-task effects, and calibration. An independent offline replay reproduces 16,140 first-token decisions from saved raw scores with exact agreement. Our findings establish answer readout as a measurable component of multimodal evaluation and provide a reproducible protocol for analyzing model comparisons.
