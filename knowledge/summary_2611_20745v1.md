## 📝 摘要

Deep-learning-based seismic interpretation is constrained by scarce high-quality annotations, and most existing models are trained separately for individual tasks. We present SeisDFE, a 7B-parameter seismic foundation model for dense feature extraction, pretrained on 5.34 million 2D images derived from more than 500 seismic volumes. A three-stage pretraining strategy, combining discriminative self-supervised learning, Gram feature anchoring, and multi-resolution adaptation, improves feature discriminability while preserving spatial coherence. With its encoder frozen, SeisDFE supports seismic facies classification, salt detection, fault detection, and relative geologic time estimation. It outperforms existing seismic foundation models on all four tasks and a natural-image-pretrained model on all but fault detection, with the largest gains under scarce supervision: using only two annotated sections of the F3 dataset, it improves whole-volume mIoU by 12-19 percentage points over the baselines. The learned features remain effective with task-specific decoders and extend to synthetic-to-field transfer and sparse 3D geobody interpretation. These results show that large-scale seismic pretraining organizes geological information into dense representations that are directly reusable across interpretation tasks.
