## 📝 摘要

Motif discovery is a process where DNA sequences are searched for the short patterns, called motifs, that transcription factor (TF) proteins bind. However, the motif lengths are often unknown and motif attribution often competes with a near-identical motif of a related protein. In this work, we pair a motif genotype whose length emerges from an evolvable gate with MAP-Elites archives organized by three descriptor pairs, and validate every motif against the full HOCOMOCO database. On five liver ChIP-seq datasets spanning distinct confounder landscapes, a $(μ + λ)$ baseline differing only in survivor selection collapses onto a single motif and never ranks the target first across three datasets, while every MAP-Elites archive does so in at least 13 of 30 seeds on every dataset. Against the motif discovery tool MEME, run on the same sequences and validated identically, the archives recover the target signal as reliably on four datasets and more robustly when motif width is constrained. Neither method ranks the target above its closest relatives on HNF4A and JUND over the full width range, but narrow archive motifs rank HNF4A first in up to 43% of seeds, where MEME misses the motif in all settings. Diversity preservation improves our ability to recover consistent motif signal, although motif annotation remains a challenging problem for all methods we tested.
