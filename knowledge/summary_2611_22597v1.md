## 📝 摘要

Promotional events shift traffic composition, purchase behavior, and the scale of conversion probabilities on different time horizons. We present \textbf{COTTI} (\textbf{CO}ntrastive \textbf{T}raining and \textbf{T}ime-aware \textbf{I}nference), an industrial recommendation framework that separates historical representation learning from event-time score adjustment. Cross-Period Contrastive Alignment (CPC) regularizes paired histories of the same user with adaptive consistency weights. Active Re-weighting (RW) controls the influence of promotional examples through historical-interest distance. Adaptive Boundary Alignment (ABA) combines stochastic prediction variability with a temporal event kernel to adjust logits while keeping predictor parameters fixed. We analyze the weighted objective and bound ABA-induced probability and ranking changes. On Fliggy data, COTTI attains an offline AUC of 0.8127, compared with 0.8101 for TEN and 0.8123 for TEN with ABA. Cross-category analysis reveals improved discrimination alongside declines in paid-item recall for novel categories. End-to-end measurements give a p99 latency of 38.9\,ms and throughput of 14,700 requests/s; enabling ABA adds 2.2\,ms to p99. A ten-day online experiment against the serving HDR model yields relative gains of 3.14\% in conversion rate and 2.68\% in revenue per mille. Together, these results characterize the trade-off between aggregate prediction, novel-category discovery, and serving cost in promotional recommendation.
