## 📝 摘要

Optical neural networks (ONNs) are attracting significant attention as alternative platforms for machine learning (ML). In an ONN, an optical module often takes over part of the computation inside a digital ML pipeline. How much of the task does the optical module itself perform, and how much does it contribute to any claimed system-level advantages, such as throughput or energy? This paper offers a simple, function-oriented evaluation methodology. Whatever its internal physics, the optical module appears to the surrounding digital pipeline as a mapping between real-valued tensors. We therefore evaluate it in three steps: (i) define its boundary in the pipeline, as real-valued tensors in and out; (ii) replace it with standard digital modules to identify those that match the optical system's performance on the same task and metric; and (iii) state system-level claims, such as speed or energy, per inference against the performance-matched digital module. We summarize steps (i) and (ii) in an Expressivity Card and demonstrate its use on published ONN systems. This helps isolate the optical module's contribution and provides a concrete digital reference for assessing its claimed system-level advantages, without the need to understand the physics. While we focus on optical systems as the most prominent example, the same approach applies to any physical analog computing platform used in ML, wherever a physical module takes over part of a digital pipeline.
