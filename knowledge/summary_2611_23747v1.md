# A Comparison of Heuristic, Human-Sensitive, and Self-Play DRL Policies in Human-Agent Teaming

## 📝 摘要

Dynamical Perceptual-Motor Primitives (DPMPs) offer a tractable framework for generating human-like movement dynamics in artificial agents (AAs) and have been used to support coordination in human-AI teaming tasks. Traditionally, DPMP controllers rely on heuristic (rule-based) decision policies derived from human behavior to guide goal selection and action sequencing. Although effective in structured tasks, such heuristics can be difficult to generalize to more complex settings, motivating interest in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches for learning decision policies for human-AI interaction. A key challenge, however, is ensuring that DRL policies remain aligned with human strategies, particularly when agents are trained through self-play. To examine this issue, we compared three target-selection policies governing DPMP-based AAs in a first-person herding task: (1) a human-derived heuristic policy, (2) a self-play DRL policy (SP-DRL), and (3) a human-sensitive DRL policy (HS-DRL) trained alongside a heuristic agent. Results showed that heuristic agents most closely matched typical human-human coordination patterns, followed by HS-DRL agents, whereas SP-DRL agents deviated most. These findings highlight the importance of human-informed training when developing DRL-based teammates and suggest that simple heuristic policies may remain an effective approach for designing human-aligned AAs capable of human-AI collaboration.
