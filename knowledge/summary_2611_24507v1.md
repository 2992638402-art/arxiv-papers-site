## 📝 摘要

While the notions of sphere packing and sphere covering underlie the most widely used criteria for space-filling design in computer experiments, namely, the maximin and minimax approaches, they suffer from major drawbacks in high dimensions. First, when the dimension $d$ is very large, deploying a space-filling design requires an extremely large number of design points, making the very notion of a space-filling design questionable. Second, these two design characteristics are highly sensitive to boundaries, particularly when the design domain is a hypercube (the case considered in this paper), and especially to its vertices. This sensitivity is particularly acute when the design size is less than $2^d$. Instead of considering the maximin or minimax criterion, we consider here the quantization error of the uniform measure on the hypercube. This error has three key advantages: it accounts for the entire distribution of distances from a random point $U$ to the design, is less sensitive to boundary effects than the maximin and minimax criteria, and, thanks to Zador's celebrated theorem, enables a smooth and natural transition to uniform designs as the design size tends to infinity. Building on recent results on the construction of optimal random quantizers for spherically symmetric distributions (arXiv:2610.11772) we propose a method to generate random designs of a given target size $n$ with small $L_s$-mean quantization error. The support of the resulting distribution is then used to construct designs, possibly incrementally, with sizes up to $n$. We demonstrate that, for moderate $n$, designs supported on a fractional factorial subset of the vertices of a small hypercube perform exceptionally well---and significantly better than designs uniformly distributed over the entire hypercube. We also explain how to account for projections onto lower-dimensional subspaces.
