## 📝 摘要

Visuomotor policies have demonstrated that imprecise low-cost robots can autonomously complete complex manipulation tasks. However, most inexpensive manipulators rely on compact and rigidly position-controlled servos for actuation, which complicate simulation, operation, and safety. This work introduces TinyARM-X, a framework for easily manufacturable, configurable, and torque-controllable low-cost (&lt;350 USD) serial manipulators. By using a single hardware/software architecture, a &lt;7 Degree of Freedom (DoF) TinyARM can be manually reconfigured to an operational &gt;3 DoF robot within 10 min. Actuator modeling and dynamic compensation enable torque-controlled applications like mixed-reality impedance control teleoperation and simulator force feedback rendering. Experiments include mechanical benchmarking and real-world force-aware telemanipulation. The TinyARM-7 has a payload of 33.5 g, a max speed of 0.77 m/s, and a reach of 210 mm. Moreover, with dynamic compensation, the robot has a backdrive-breakaway force of 1.4 N and a backdrive-running force of 0.7 N. Additionally, Cartesian-Impedance Control (CIC) attains a translational stiffness of &gt;15 N/m. Lastly, the TinyARM-X stack is open-sourced to disseminate accessible desktop-scale torque-controlled robotic manipulation to the community.
