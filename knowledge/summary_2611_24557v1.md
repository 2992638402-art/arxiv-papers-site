## 📝 摘要

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are built using well-understood mathematical operations, yet their complex nature poses significant challenges to intuitively understanding their decision-making processes. This work introduces a method for visualizing and measuring ANN decision boundary geometry directly in the high-dimensional input space, avoiding distortions found with traditional projection-based techniques. We analyze boundary curvatures and distances in diverse inputs, architectures, and training methods. Boundary distances grow smoothly and slowly with consistent curvature as the size of the subspace increases, suggesting that decision boundaries are close to input samples within large, continuous subspaces. We also quantify how much boundary distances and curvatures reduce near dataset samples after adversarial training. Training methodology has a significantly stronger impact on boundary distance and curvature than architecture or test-set accuracy. These findings are contextualized within prior work, supporting or refuting several previously proposed theories of boundary behavior. Our analytically and empirically supported methodology lays the foundation for future studies to improve network interpretability and understanding.
