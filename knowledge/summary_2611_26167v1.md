## 📝 摘要

Tight-space navigation requires a robot to clear obstacles and stop inside its goal despite uncertainty. We present GeoDiff, a motion-program planner coupling original-goal stopped-set inclusion, witness-guided terminal repair, and factored proof compilation. An exact feasible-translation characterization guides repair; request-local reuse reduces repeated geometric work. We establish conditions for compiler equivalence and reach-and-stop soundness. Across 11,440 floorplan-derived jobs, GeoDiff certifies 61/139 tasks at 5-cm tolerance within 15 s, versus 44/139 for the stop-set baseline. The strongest observed external certificate adapter certifies 13/139 tasks from 119/139 native routes, separating route availability from certification. On tasks derived from public BARN maps, GeoDiff certifies 202/270 at 5-cm tolerance for a Jackal-shaped body, versus 3/270 for the portal baseline. Development analyses distinguish emitter savings from solver coverage, and 1,420 route-tracking episodes characterize the execution interface. These results show how terminal reasoning and proof reuse increase finite-budget certification coverage while preserving the original physical task and goal.
