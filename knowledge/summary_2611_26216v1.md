## 📝 摘要

Given a single text and a simple zero-shot prompt (no web access, tools, candidate lists, or writing samples), frontier models can answer "who wrote this?" at three increasingly specific levels: (1) whether an AI or human wrote it, (2) if an AI, which lab, and (3) if a human, often exactly who. The latest models (Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Carbon) match trained detectors like Pangram while also explaining their predictions in natural language, and Opus 5.5 names the correct lab for 98% of texts generated by models from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google. Most strikingly, Fable 5.2 correctly names the author of 39% of public peer reviews published in Oct-Nov 2026 (78% on a subset of reviews from Nature), and identifies the username behind 69% of comments from active Redditors (with &gt;500 posts over the last year). Accuracy depends on exposure to an author's writing in pretraining data and on reasoning effort, and simply asking a model how familiar it is with someone predicts whether it can identify their writing. For anyone who has published enough writing online, frontier models present a greater threat to anonymity than commonly assumed, and defenses are limited.
