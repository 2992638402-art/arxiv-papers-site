## 📝 摘要

Compared to single-robot deployments, multi-robot teams can significantly improve search-and-rescue (SAR) mission success by covering large areas in parallel. To collaborate effectively, these robots must be registered in a common reference frame. Standard registration methods typically rely on external infrastructure or significant map overlap, but real deployments require infrastructure-free, stationary initial alignment for immediate operation. While global LiDAR point cloud registration addresses this, existing single-scan methods often fail silently in aliased or degenerate environments. Approaches scaling beyond two agents also remain rarely evaluated in real-world scenarios. We propose a centralized, deployment-ready framework for pre-mission multi-robot registration. Our system performs pairwise coarse-to-fine alignment and actively rejects impossible pose hypotheses via deterministic plausibility checks. We validate the framework on a real-world custom dataset of four synchronized robots in various environments with ground truth. The checks reject more than a third of the incorrect pairwise registrations while falsely rejecting less than 1% of the correct ones, and reduce the number of team registrations with a wrong robot pose by 38%. Dataset and code are publicly available.
