## 📝 摘要

Vision-language models (VLMs) demonstrate broad generalization across tasks and domains. However, comparable generalization in robot manipulation remains challenging. Heterogeneity in robot embodiments, task formulations, geometric conventions, and action interfaces fragments interaction experience across benchmarks and hardware. We rethink robot skill learning through alignment: Can heterogeneous interactions across benchmarks and embodiments jointly improve one shared skillset for robot manipulation? We propose UniCraft, a framework for learning robot manipulation skills by aligning heterogeneous settings. Specifically, UniCraft represents observed scene geometry using consistent coordinate conventions and aligns sensing and control interfaces across robots. It organizes tasks from different benchmarks in a canonical format, allowing interaction feedback to jointly refine shared skills and the orchestration policy that guides their selection and composition, without any demonstrations or model weight updates. We interleave tasks from 3 benchmarks and 6 embodiments, collecting 2,700 learning rollouts per agent model. During training, UniCraft improves pooled task success on unseen scenarios and tasks by 9-28 percentage points on LIBERO-PRO, 10.8-13.3 on RoboTwin 2.0, and 3.1-14.4 on RoboCasa365 (Epochs 1-3). After training, with the learned skills frozen, UniCraft demonstrates zero-shot transfer across 4 new robot embodiments and 5 additional agent models on robosuite and RoboDojo.
