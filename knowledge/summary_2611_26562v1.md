## 📝 摘要

Barrett's esophagus dysplasia grading suffers substantial inter-observer variability, particularly for low-grade dysplasia, complicating clinical decision-making. We conducted a retrospective multi-institutional diagnostic accuracy study of a foundation model-based deep learning system for dysplasia grading, using 969 H\&amp;E-stained whole slide images from four institutions. Patch-level classifiers trained on expert annotations identified diagnostically relevant regions, followed by attribute-aware multiple instance learning for slide-level classification as non-dysplastic Barrett's esophagus (NDBE), low-grade dysplasia, or high-grade dysplasia. On a held-out test set (n = 130), macro-averaged AUC was 0.96 (95\% CI 0.93--0.98). For binary dysplasia detection, sensitivity was 98\% (80/82) and specificity 81\% (39/48), with a negative likelihood ratio of 0.030 and quadratic-weighted \kap{} of 0.83 against expert consensus. No high-grade dysplasia case was misclassified as NDBE. Performance was consistent across institutions without stain normalization, indicating that stain-robust foundation-model representations may remove the site-specific preprocessing that has constrained prior systems to institution-specific deployment.
