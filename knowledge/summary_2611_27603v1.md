## 📝 摘要

When interacting with objects of unknown physical properties, humans sometimes probe, over-exert, stagger, and adjust through iterative adaptation instead of succeeding immediately. We argue that this process is an expressive but largely overlooked aspect of human-object interaction in motion synthesis. Both kinematic data-driven methods and physics-based controllers tend to produce flawlessly executed motions: the former lack sufficient data for learning fine-grained adaptation, while the latter use reinforcement learning (RL) and privileged object dynamics to learn robust, deterministic behaviors. We propose ExpHOI (Expressive Human-Object Interaction), a test-time latent adaptation framework built on an RL-trained physics-based control policy. During policy training, we encode diverse object dynamics contexts into a structured latent space. At test time, Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) iteratively optimizes the latent code for an object with unknown physical properties. Temporary mismatches between this code and the true object dynamics produce an expressive progression from tentative, error-prone trials to proficient interaction. Adaptation behaviors inevitably come with occasional failures, so a high-level planner is introduced to handle failed cases to maintain full, consistent animations. We demonstrate our framework's effectiveness on a large-object transportation task, where such adaptation is especially pronounced. To train our framework, we introduce a mass-diverse object dataset with rich physical-property annotations. Across carrying and push-pull, our method pairs over 90% success with markedly higher posture diversity, and is rated the most lifelike in our user study on animation liveliness.
