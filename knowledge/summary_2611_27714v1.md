## 📝 摘要

Adaptive decision systems in education and health choose interventions sequentially and are often required not to leave a protected group too far behind. We study the fairness-constrained optimal value: the largest population value attainable by a policy in a smooth parametric class subject to constraints that are smooth functions of group specific counterfactual functionals, such as value gaps, floors, max-min welfare and budgets. For multi-stage, non-Markov data under sequential ignorability, a sensitivity analysis of the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker system shows that this value is pathwise differentiable, with efficient influence function equal to the Lagrangian combination of the influence functions of the group values. The same analysis gives the influence functions of the optimal policy, the multiplier and the group values it delivers. A cross-fitted doubly robust estimator is efficient despite its data-dependent feasible set and yields confidence intervals for the constrained value and the price of fairness. When the constraint binds, any plug-in fair policy violates the population constraint, by a margin that vanishes as the sample grows, with probability tending to one half; tightening the estimated constraint by a one sided confidence margin certifies fairness at a small cost in value. Exact checks, simulations and a synthetic case study illustrate the theory.
