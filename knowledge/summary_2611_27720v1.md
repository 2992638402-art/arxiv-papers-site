## 📝 摘要

We introduce PAWS, a parametric model of canine shape and pose, learned from 3D scans of real dogs. Capturing the full diversity of dogs is challenging, as different breeds vary in size, proportion, and body shape. To capture this range, we scanned 31 dogs across multiple breeds and sizes performing a range of natural movements. From this dataset, we build a parametric model that combines an anatomically faithful skeleton with a linear shape space learned from the 3D scans. Unlike previous models, our model is learned from real dogs in varying poses, allowing for realistic modelling of canine pose and shape. We demonstrate that building on PAWS provides a robust framework for downstream tasks, such as surface reconstruction and image regression. In particular, we show that a limited number of anatomically valid degrees of freedom is sufficient to accurately estimate dog pose and shape.
