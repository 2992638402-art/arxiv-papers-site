## 📝 摘要

Anti-money laundering (AML) review selects suspicious accounts and reconstructs transfers. Downstream graph neural networks (GNNs) rank money-flow evidence but may also reorder the account queue. DOiT separates these decisions at equal review capacity: account selection determines where graph investigation starts. Across AMLWorld and AMLNet, GNN reordering harms higher-yield upstream orders but helps lower-yield ones. On AMLWorld, this harm coexists with higher transaction average precision (AP) in dimension-matched, per-run decoders, demonstrating separable ranking abilities. At small capacities, positive neighbors can displace a denser upstream prefix and lower yield; replacement costs fall as that prefix is exhausted, allowing the preferred order to reverse. A restricted-access synthetic benchmark informed by real transaction patterns reproduces this separation and validation-based upstream preservation. Capacity-matched combination protects upstream ordering while retaining GNN money-flow analysis, admitting GNN queue influence only where held-out evidence supports it.
