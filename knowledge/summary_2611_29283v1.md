## 📝 摘要

Adding a singing voice to an existing piano recording requires decisions about melody, syllable placement, pronunciation, timing, and vocal register. ARRANGE organizes this work as a sequence of agent skills with readable intermediate artifacts and composer approval checkpoints. An LLM translates instructions in plain English, notes played on a keyboard, and vocal references into local edits; deterministic scripts validate and apply those edits before open audio models render the result. The Score Canvas makes note, syllable, and pronunciation decisions inspectable and editable. In a benchmark of 16 revision requests, three draws per request, and five LLMs, typed edits achieved exact-change correctness of 0.83-0.98, compared with 0.33-0.69 for whole-plan rewriting without ARRANGE instructions. A separate score-editing benchmark and a first-person worked example examine the interaction between representations and corrections. Author listening evaluations further explore voice transfer and register selection, including 185 listening presentations across eight copied voices. These evaluations support the value of constrained editing and expose limits of both model execution and listening measures. The contribution is an interaction architecture for retaining musical decisions through inspectable intermediate representations, rather than a new singing model or evidence of general usability.
