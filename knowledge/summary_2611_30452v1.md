## 📝 摘要

Functional ANOVA models express a prediction as a sum of main effects and interactions, but existing neural functional ANOVA models require the interactions to be specified in advance, which forces a trade-off between missing higher-order structure and the cost of enumerating many interactions. We propose SCAN (Scalable Component Additive Networks), which learns which features each basis function combines, so that components of different orders are selected and estimated jointly without enumerating candidates. The learned component index sets form a small candidate collection on which post-hoc identification is carried out efficiently. A global component-selection parameter shares information across basis functions, and truncating negligible component-selection weights gives a sparse model that is identified after training. We establish universal approximation, a minimax optimal rate up to a logarithmic factor, and component selection consistency, which together imply that the discovered components contain all identified true components with probability tending to one. In synthetic experiments, SCAN learns components whose maximum order is close to the true one, and recovers higher-order components more accurately than the baseline models, with post-hoc identification taking a few seconds for 100 inputs. SCAN also outperforms most neural functional ANOVA models in prediction on the benchmark datasets and trains at a much lower cost than exhaustive second-order models. We also apply SCAN to predict treatment response in breast cancer from 12,502 gene-expression variables.
