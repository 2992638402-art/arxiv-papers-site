## 📝 摘要

Continuous-time multi-agent path finding on roadmaps (MAPF$_R$) offers a principled way to coordinate robot fleets with geometric collision avoidance, yet its deployment on industrial automated guided vehicle (AGV) infrastructures remains challenging, as highly connected and redundant roadmaps amplify both safe-interval reasoning and multi-agent interaction handling. To bridge this gap, we formalize AGV-MAPF$_R$-CTC, a configuration-space variant of MAPF$_R$ with precomputed continuous-time conflicts that enables the reuse of MAPF$_R$ solvers for AGV fleet coordination with minimal changes. We then revisit continuous-time priority-based search (CPBS), identifying the boundary conditions required by its safe interval path planning (SIPP) low-level planner under priority-induced constraints and enabling earlier solution detection. As a central contribution, we analyze the structure of the priority search space, clarifying which agents are affected by each priority refinement and when replanning can be avoided. Building on this analysis, we introduce a novel algorithm, Lazy-CPBS, which materializes high-level nodes lazily: a child's plans are computed only when it is expanded, so siblings that are never expanded cost no low-level search. Experiments on standard MAPF benchmarks and on industrial AGV roadmaps imported from a proprietary digital-twin simulator show that CPBS-based solvers remain effective well beyond the agent counts reachable by optimal MAPF$_R$ solvers, while Lazy-CPBS substantially reduces CPBS overhead and offers the most consistent robustness/runtime trade-off across industrial layouts.
