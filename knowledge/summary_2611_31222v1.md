## 📝 摘要

Radiology reports describe findings in text only, without visually marking them on the scan, so readers cannot directly see which abnormality each reported finding refers to. We introduce CTLink, a text-prompted 3D segmentation model that segments findings described in a chest CT report. CTLink was trained on 4123 noncontrast chest CT scans with 9547 segmented report findings, including ReXGroundingCTv2, a newly annotated dataset of 1134 scans and 1895 findings with every instance of each finding segmented. On the 200 ReXGroundingCT validation scans, CTLink achieved a hit rate of 0.816, an instance F1 score of 0.456, and a mean Dice score of 0.400, compared with 0.533, 0.175, and 0.234 for VoxTell, a publicly available text-prompted segmentation model (P &lt; .001 for all). On the test set of the MICCAI 2026 ReXGrounding challenge, an ensemble of seven CTLink models achieved the highest hit rate (0.782) and instance F1 score (0.386) of all 48 participating teams. On 50 test scans segmented twice by independent radiologists, CTLink matched or exceeded inter-rater agreement. Diffuse non-focal findings remained the main limitation, partly because their extent is outlined inconsistently even between radiologists. The CTLink model weights are available at https://huggingface.co/rajpurkarlab/CTLink, and the dataset at https://huggingface.co/datasets/rajpurkarlab/ReXGroundingCT.
