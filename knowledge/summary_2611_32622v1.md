## 📝 摘要

Collecting diverse real-world manipulation demonstrations is costly, motivating the use of simulation to augment existing demonstrations for robot policy learning. When objects, layouts, or robots change, transferring motions from a source demonstration can produce behavior that is semantically implausible or physically infeasible in the new setting. We introduce RoboRecast, an agentic optimization framework that preserves the interactions required by the source task while jointly optimizing the simulated scene and robot motion to make those interactions executable in the target setting. When an interaction fails, the agent uses simulation feedback to diagnose the cause, revise the relevant scene or motion parameters, and test whether the revision resolves the failure. This flexibility allows RoboRecast to generate synthetic data from both human and robot demonstrations and to adapt them to new layouts, objects, and robot embodiments. When target demonstrations are available, adding RoboRecast data improves policy success on both real-world single-arm and simulated bimanual tasks. Even without demonstrations in the target configuration, RoboRecast adapts source demonstrations to support policy learning with new objects or robot embodiments.
