## 📝 摘要

Model-generated labels offer a scalable route to training lightweight vision models, but what makes a model a good teacher, and can we tell before training a student? We study this in gaze target estimation, where interactive robots need models that are both accurate and efficient. In a controlled comparison of eleven supervision conditions, we vary only the source of gaze-point labels, keeping the student, training protocol and 361k training samples fixed, across three video datasets, two student sizes and three seeds; every student is evaluated against human annotation. Three findings emerge. First, imperfect model-generated labels can replace human annotation: Gaze-LLE, never trained on the target datasets' gaze annotation, trains students that match annotation-trained ones on ViT-Tiny and are significantly more accurate on ViT-Small (average distance error 0.187 vs 0.196), and the ViT-Tiny student runs 32$\times$ faster than its teacher on a laptop CPU. Second, apparent competence can conceal unreliable supervision: given another person's face centre in the prompt, InternVL3 and Qwen3-VL return it on 93.4% and 81.1% of test samples, and a copy-the-coordinate baseline matches InternVL3's labels and students. Without that coordinate, both models fall far behind the specialist. Third, good teachers can be identified before training: validation label error ranks student test error (Spearman's $ρ= 0.91$), and ten annotated clips single out the two specialist teachers in 95% of draws. These findings give practical principles for assessing supervision, exposing shortcuts and selecting teachers for lightweight models.
