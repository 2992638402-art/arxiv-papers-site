## 📝 摘要

Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) adapts a pretrained weight by an orthogonal transform and preserves its singular values and row angles, but existing methods construct an exact or approximate orthogonal transform at every optimization step. We measure how far a transform $A$ is from orthogonal by its orthogonality defect $\|A^\top A-I\|_2$. For OFTv2, which truncates a Cayley-Neumann series, we show that the truncation order does not determine the defect. At its default order, block defects in checkpoints from the reference implementation span five orders of magnitude. We propose Restarted Tangent Orthogonal Finetuning (RTOFT), which uses the first-order update $W(I+S)$ of a weight $W$ with a skew-symmetric $S$ and restores exact orthogonality by a polar decomposition only when the drift $\|S\|_2$ reaches a tolerance $τ$. The defect of $I+S$ is exactly $\|S\|_2^2$. Therefore, reading the drift after every update and restoring before the next forward pass guarantees a defect of at most $τ^2$ in every forward pass, independently of the loss, data, model, and optimizer. A final restoration makes the returned transform exactly orthogonal. On GLUE, RTOFT reduces the training time of block-diagonal, subspace, and butterfly parameterizations from $1.10$-$1.58\times$ to $0.97$-$1.13\times$ that of LoRA, and its block-diagonal version exceeds LoRA by $1.98\pm0.39$ points on the six-task average over five seeds. At $τ=0.10$, the largest drift read during training stays between $0.10$ and $0.12$ across DeBERTaV3, Qwen, and diffusion models, whereas it ranges from $0.17$ to $1.65$ when restoring once per epoch. In all seven settings of our ablation, the tangent update is worse without restoration than with it, and restoring after every step yields no measurable accuracy gain over restoring at the tolerance and costs $4.5$-$27.5\%$ more per step.
