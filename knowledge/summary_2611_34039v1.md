## 📝 摘要

Efficient identification of finite impulse response systems under unknown or heavy-tailed noise remains a challenge in system identification. Classical estimators, including least squares (LS) and robust alternatives like least absolute deviations (LAD), are often sensitive to outliers or statistically inefficient when noise characteristics deviate from their underlying assumptions. To address this, we propose a novel class of adaptive estimators that achieves asymptotic efficiency without requiring prior knowledge of the noise distribution. Our approach leverages kernel density estimation to construct two nonparametric maximum likelihood-type estimators: the estimated maximum likelihood estimator (eMLE) and the estimated profiled MLE (epMLE). These estimators approximate the likelihood using residuals from consistent initial estimates, avoiding parametric assumptions on the noise. We establish theoretical guarantees showing that, under mild regularity conditions, both eMLE and epMLE are consistent, asymptotically normal, and attain the Cramér--Rao lower bound, thereby achieving asymptotic efficiency. Additionally, we develop computationally efficient one-step Newton--Raphson algorithms that implement these estimators without sacrificing asymptotic efficiency. Extensive simulations across various noise distributions confirm that the proposed methods outperform classical LS and LAD estimators in both robustness and accuracy, often matching oracle MLE performance.
