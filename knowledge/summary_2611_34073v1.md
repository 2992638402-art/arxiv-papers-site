## 📝 摘要

Many sampling and inference tasks require a single distribution that balances several targets. We formulate this task as multiobjective optimization of Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergences over probability measures, in the Pareto sense. The particle method MWGraD (Nguyen et al., 2025) uses explicit Wasserstein gradient steps, and its accelerated version A-MWGraD (Nguyen et al., 2026) lacks discrete-time guarantees. We propose the multiobjective Wasserstein proximal gradient (MWPG), which uses implicit Wasserstein gradient steps, and its accelerated version, multiobjective Wasserstein accelerated proximal gradient (MWAPG), with rigorous discrete-time guarantees. Our key idea is a Hilbert-space lifting: each measure is represented as the pushforward of a fixed base measure by a map in a Hilbert space. The problem then becomes a constrained multiobjective composite problem over transport maps, to which accelerated proximal gradient methods apply. For convex and smooth potentials, we show that MWAPG decreases a Pareto merit function at the rate $\mathcal{O}(1/k^2)$; for $m$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth potentials, the rate is $\mathcal{O}\big((1-\sqrt{m/L})^k\big)$. We also give closed-form Gaussian updates and particle implementations based on input-convex neural networks, which require no score estimation. Experiments on synthetic and real-world dataset show that MWAPG needs far fewer iterations than MWPG and matches or outperforms A-MWGraD in Bayesian multi-task learning.
