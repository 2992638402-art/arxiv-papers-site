## 📝 摘要

Task allocation in contested-objective team games requires coordinated decisions under uncertainty, spatial constraints, and changing tactical conditions. In such settings, multiple agents must anticipate both teammate behavior and opponent actions. We study a hybrid predict-then-optimize approach for assigning controllable agents to objectives in real time. A masked multi-head model predicts tactical signals for each agent, including survival chance, local superiority, combat interaction, travel time, and objective contribution. These signals are combined with rule-based terms in a score matrix, and a minimum-cost flow solver computes coordinated global assignments constrained by capacity, occupancy, stability, and defensive strategies. Learning is limited to tactical scoring, while explicit rules and solver structure preserve controllability and interpretability. We evaluate the system using predictive metrics, offline assignment diagnostics, online A/B evaluation, and latency measurements. The system is an R\&amp;D research prototype built on a commercial game and is not deployed in the shipped game. The results indicate that learned tactical estimates can support stable, feasible, and inspectable objective allocation under strict real-time constraints.
