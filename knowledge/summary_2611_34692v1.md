## 📝 摘要

The search dynamics of particle swarm optimization (PSO) can be driven by random or chaotic values. We study if there are performance differences between random and chaos if distributional equivalence of the used sequences is observed. Therefore, we consider an anytime performance assessment which quantifies the fraction of runs reaching a given target value within a specified numerical budget. Furthermore, we connect quantifiers of the chaotic dynamics to performance metrics of the anytime analysis. The results show that once the underlying distributions are controlled, deterministic chaotic sequences yield no general or consistent advantage over random sequences. Performance differences arise most prominently from the underlying distribution, but also from search-space dimensions, benchmark targets, and numerical budgets. We also find potential budget-dependent advantages under specific parametrization of chaotic maps and distribution settings.
