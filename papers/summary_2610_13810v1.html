Tracing and Taming Hallucinated Terms via Subsequence-Association Filtering and Breadth-Aware Selection for LLM Query Expansion

arXiv ID: 2610.13810
分类: cs.IR, cs.CL
发布时间: 2026-10-09

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Amit Kumar Jaiswal, Sagar Uprety, Prayag Tiwari
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Large language models (LLMs) are now a standard source of query expansion terms but they hallucinate. They inject non existent entities and off-topic terms that add ranking noise and can hurt strong retrievers. Existing remedies such as corpus grounding, mutual verification and preference alignment treat hallucinated terms as an undifferentiated nuisance to be filtered by agreement heuristics, without a mechanistic account of why a given term appears or a principled criterion for how many terms to keep. We propose Subsequence-Association Filtered, breadth-aware query expansion (SAFE-QE), a training-free, retriever agnostic method that draws on the subsequence-association account of hallucination. We score each candidate expansion term by its corpus conditioned cross-context reproducibility, terms that reappear under randomised query contexts yet lack support in the pseudo-relevant set are flagged as products of dominant hallucinatory associations and down-weighted. Drawing on the characterisation of language generation as a trade-off between consistency (no hallucination) and breadth (coverage), we cast term selection as constrained coverage maximisation over the pseudo-relevant set, keeping a set that is approximately broad but consistent and stable across resampling. We also introduce the Hallucinated Expansion Term Rate, a query side faithfulness metric that counts injected expansion terms no relevant document supports. We evaluate our approach on benchmarked TREC and BEIR collections. Our SAFE-QE is statistically indistinguishable from generative and corpus-steered expansion on nDCG@10 while injecting fewer unsupported terms 4.3 per query against 27.8 for Query2doc and 57.5 for HyDE and on a strong learned sparse retriever, where every expansion method regresses. Faithfulness is therefore available at no measurable cost in ranking quality.
