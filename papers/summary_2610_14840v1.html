Push the Limit: Learning High-Speed Sprinting and Aggressive Cornering on a Full-Scale Humanoid

arXiv ID: 2610.14840
分类: cs.RO
发布时间: 2026-10-10

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Huaxing Huang, Ruidong Li, Jinchao Han, Ziyang Dong, Wenhao Cui
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

We present a learning-based framework that enables a full-scale bipedal humanoid to sprint at over 7 m/s with motion-prior-guided gait and perform aggressive cornering at high speed with a pronounced trunk lean of up to $25^\circ$. Achieving this combination is challenging: above approximately 4 m/s, periodic foot impacts induce severe, high-frequency yaw-rate oscillations intrinsic to high-speed gaits. This high-frequency temporal structure corrupts the history of proprioceptive feedback that flat-MLP policies rely on, while rendering conventional yaw-rate tracking rewards counterproductive, as they fight against the robot's natural oscillatory dynamics. Moreover, a single style prior shared across skills cannot tell which skill is commanded and inherently compromises the low-speed skill fidelity. To overcome these limitations, we adopt a causal multi-head attention (MHA) module to explicitly model the high-frequency temporal feedback. In place of the oscillatory yaw rate, we design a heading-direction reward that supervises only the low-frequency velocity direction, allowing agile turning at high speed without suppressing natural gait oscillations. We further introduce skill-conditioned multi-adversarial-motion-prior (MAMP) discriminators that condition the style reward on the commanded skill. Ablation experiments confirm that each mechanism is essential: replacing structured temporal encoding with a flat-history MLP caps the top speed; reverting to yaw-rate tracking prevents high-speed turning; and a single shared discriminator makes in-place turning collapse into a quasi-static stance. Our framework bridges the gap between raw sprinting speed and reference-motion fidelity, demonstrating stable high-speed sprinting and aggressive cornering in both simulation and real-robot deployment.
