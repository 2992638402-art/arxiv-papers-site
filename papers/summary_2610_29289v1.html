Approximate Solomonoff Induction: On the Robustness of Learned Universal Predictors

arXiv ID: 2610.29289
分类: cs.LG, stat.ML
发布时间: 2026-10-21

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Qian Li, Shang-Hua Teng
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Why large language models trained via log-loss minimization over massive and diverse datasets exhibit broad capabilities and generalizable skills is not yet fully understood. Grau-Moya et al. (ICML 2024) offer a conceptual explanation through the lens of meta-learning: if sequence-generating environments $ν$ are drawn from a prior $w_ν$, minimizing population log-loss recovers the corresponding Bayesian mixture predictor $ξ=\sum_νw_νν$. In the idealized limit in which $w$ is a universal prior, the optimal predictor corresponds to Solomonoff universal induction. Practical machine learning algorithms, however, generally produce only \emph{approximately} optimal predictors. This raises a natural question: \emph{Does the prediction performance remain robust under approximation}? In this paper, we first provide a tight bound on the worst-case effect of approximation on prediction error. We show any predictor $ρ$ satisfying $D_{KL}(ξ|ρ)\leq η$ retains bounded cumulative-expected-squared prediction error. However, while the Bayesian mixture predictor $ξ$ has error $O(\log(1/w_μ))$ in an environment $μ$, we establish a tight $Θ(η/w_μ)$ bound on the excess error of $ρ$ relative to $ξ$. Thus, approximation can substantially degrade performance in environments assigned small prior probability. In sharp contrast, when environments are averaged according to prior $w$, we prove that the excess prediction error is only $O(η)$. Furthermore, we establish a distribution-shift theorem for dominated shifts from a training prior $w$ to a test prior $w'$, interpolating our average-case and worst-case guarantees. Finally, we extend our robustness analysis to a finite-sampling setting relevant to learning universal predictors with neural networks and Transformers, where $D_{KL}(ξ|ρ)$ could be unbounded.
