Covariance-Informed Ensemble Score Filtering: Embedding Localized Covariance Geometry into Score-Based Transport

arXiv ID: 2610.32148
分类: math.NA, physics.ao-ph, stat.ML
发布时间: 2026-10-23

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Siming Liang, Peter Jan van Leeuwen, Hristo G. Chipilski, Zezhong Zhang, Feng Bao, Guannan Zhang
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Background-error covariance models are central to atmospheric data assimilation because they spread partial observational information across space, variables, and dynamically coupled scales. Ensemble and variational methods exploit this covariance geometry efficiently, but their standard analysis formulations are largely built around Gaussian or locally quadratic approximations. Score-based ensemble filtering provides a framework for nonlinear distributional transport, but existing Ensemble Score Filter (EnSF) formulations lack an intrinsic covariance-mediated pathway for propagating likelihood information from observed to unobserved state components. We introduce a covariance-informed ensemble score filter (CI-EnSF) that embeds localized forecast-error covariance geometry directly into the forward diffusion and reverse transport. The proposed forward diffusion is covariance-shaped in standardized state space and becomes isotropic under the corresponding control-variable transformation. This covariance structure continuously propagates observational information from observed to unobserved state components throughout the reverse transport analysis trajectory. Experiments with Lorenz-96 and surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) dynamics show a clear regime dependence. For linear observations, a well-tuned LETKF is more accurate, while CI-EnSF maintains stable cycling and effective covariance-mediated information propagation. Under strongly nonlinear observations and under climatological initialization, CI-EnSF achieves lower analysis error and greater cycling robustness than Gaussian ensemble benchmarks such as the LETKF, with improvements extending across spatial scales in the nonlinear SQG experiment. These results suggest that the principal benefit of CI-EnSF arises from combining nonlinear distributional transport with the mature covariance geometry used in conventional data assimilation.
