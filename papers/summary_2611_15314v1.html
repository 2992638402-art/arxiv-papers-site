AcrossPlace: Do Vision--Language Models Recognize Places Beyond Their Source Views? A Comprehensive Benchmark

arXiv ID: 2611.15314
分类: cs.CV
发布时间: 2026-11-11

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Zhenyu Li, Tianyi Shang, Shuhan Dong, Ruotong Xu, Qingyu Zhu
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Language-guided place recognition requires recovering geographic identity across visual observations beyond the image from which a description originates. However, aggregate retrieval scores often entangle observation transfer, gallery competition, and linguistic evidence, obscuring the underlying recognition capability. We introduce AcrossPlace, a provenance-aware benchmark establishing a systematic evaluation standard for bidirectional language--vision place recognition. Built upon GSV-Cities, AcrossPlace comprises 56,726 places across 23 cities, 156,800 automatically generated, rule-filtered descriptions, and 113,404 target images. Each description retains its source-image provenance, while retrieval operates on disjoint target observations. Text-to-image (T2I) and image-to-text (I2T) retrieval share a unified place-identity relevance criterion supporting multiple valid matches. Two geographic protocols evaluate unseen places within represented cities (P0) and generalization to held-out cities (P1). Paired observation comparisons, gallery-controlled evaluations, fixed-query scaling, and blinded annotation auditing systematically characterize retrieval performance. Across three training seeds, place-supervised SigLIP2 T2I Recall@1 decreases from 19.69% on source images to 12.31% on independent targets under P0. For fixed P1 queries, expanding the gallery from 500 to 8,425 places reduces Recall@1 from 14.67% to 2.87%. A 240-description audit further reveals discrepancies between automated acceptance and factual evidence. These findings establish AcrossPlace as a reproducible evaluation standard for assessing transferable place identity across visual observations and geographic environments.
