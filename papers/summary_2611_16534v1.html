Context-Predictive Low-Rank Adaptation for Face Presentation Attack Detection

arXiv ID: 2611.16534
分类: cs.CV, eess.IV, eess.SP, stat.ML
发布时间: 2026-11-12

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Aminu Yusuf, Shakeel Muhammad Ibrahim, Sulaimon Oyeniyi Adebayo, Muhammad Sohail Ibrahim, Muhammad Shahid Jabbar, Shujaat Khan
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Face presentation attack detection requires representations that distinguish physical spoofing cues across changes in acquisition devices, subjects, and attack media. Large visual encoders offer transferable representations, but conventional binary fine-tuning supervises their final decisions without explicitly constraining how local facial evidence is represented under partial observation. We present CP-JEPA, a context-predictive adaptation framework that combines low-rank tuning of a pretrained CLIP image encoder with a joint-embedding predictive objective. A student predicts complete-image teacher features at masked spatial locations, while unmasked classification, masked classification, and prediction consistency retain the connection to the live/spoof task. An exponential-moving-average teacher supplies evolving latent targets. The auxiliary components are used only during training; inference requires only the LoRA-adapted CLIP visual encoder and binary classifier. {We evaluate CP-JEPA under single-source and multi-source OCIM transfer, additional CelebA-Spoof training, transfer to CelebA-Spoof, two-source training, and unseen-attack evaluation on SiW-Mv2.} On the standard OCIM benchmark, CP-JEPA achieves 3.74% mean HTER and 98.73% mean AUC. {Single-source evaluation achieves 11.36% mean HTER across twelve source--target combinations.} With CelebA-Spoof added to the three OCIM training sources, CP-JEPA reduces mean HTER from 3.45% to 2.35% and increases mean AUC from 98.68% to 99.32%. On SiW-Mv2, it achieves 3.36% mean HTER across the held-out attack categories. Controlled loss ablations examine the contributions of latent prediction, masked classification, and prediction consistency. The results show that context-predictive adaptation can improve cross-dataset PAD performance, although the gains vary with the training sources and evaluation setting.
