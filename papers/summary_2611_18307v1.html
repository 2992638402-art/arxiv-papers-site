AI for Parameter Selection in Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization

arXiv ID: 2611.18307
分类: physics.chem-ph, cs.AI
发布时间: 2026-11-13

  • PDF: 下载

📝 摘要

Sample-based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) methods provide a way to compute molecular ground-state energies, but their accuracy and runtime depend sensitively on configuration choices such as the subspace dimension, propagation timestep, and the allocation of a fixed sampling budget across circuits, for which no formal selection method exists. In this work, we explore whether oracle models can predict the quantum runtime and approximation error from the Hamiltonian and the candidate configuration. Using synthetic Hamiltonians with controllable spectral properties, we show that machine-learned models can map a Hamiltonian directly to the best-ranked configuration within a predefined grid for the SKQD algorithm, validated against classical Krylov propagation, achieving strong rank correlations between true and predicted error and runtime, Spearman $ρ= 0.969$ and $ρ= 0.905$, respectively. Extending the approach to 50 molecular Hamiltonians (20-50 qubits), with reference ground-state energies from classical calculations, we train regressors that predict the simulation error and runtime for a given molecule--configuration pair ahead of execution, and classifiers to distinguish, from 600 SqDRIFT quantum-hardware runs, whether a configuration achieves a ground-state energy within chemical accuracy ($1.6\times10^{-3}$ Ha $\approx 1$ kcal/mol). For the molecular Hamiltonians, the models additionally provide an a priori feasibility and resource estimate, predicting whether a molecule can reach chemical accuracy on current hardware and at what runtime, thus correctly classifying held-out molecules at the molecule level, achieving balanced accuracy 61.8% in-distribution and 100% for five larger held-out molecules.
