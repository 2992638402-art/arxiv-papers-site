Clinician Assessment of Laryngeal Disease with Auditory AI before Laryngoscopy: A Paired Reader Study

arXiv ID: 2611.20050
分类: eess.AS, cs.AI
发布时间: 2026-11-15

📄 论文信息

  • PDF: 下载

📝 摘要

Background: Before laryngoscopy, clinicians assess the likely cause of hoarseness and whether to suspect a precancerous or malignant lesion. Whether voice-based artificial intelligence (AI) supports pre-laryngoscopic categorization is unknown. Methods: We developed an auditory large language model that generates a patient-level assessment card (provisional category, voice-quality ratings, and rationale) and a prespecified malignancy alert from voice recordings, patient information, and acoustic measures obtained before laryngoscopy at one center. We evaluated both in a held-out test set and a later cohort. In a sequential reader study, 32 otolaryngology clinicians categorized held-out cases without AI and, 1 to 2 weeks later, reviewed their initial categories with the card (without the alert). Results: Across 800 paired assessments, reader accuracy was 49.8% before and 56.8% after review (difference, 7.0 percentage points; 95% CI, 1.7 to 13.5; P < 0.001); 99 categories changed from incorrect to correct and 43 from correct to incorrect (ratio, 2.30; 95% CI, 1.22 to 5.31). Accuracy rose 14.7 points when the model was correct and fell 8.7 when incorrect. Provisional-category accuracy was 72% in held-out testing and 67% in temporal validation. The malignancy alert flagged 85.7% and 86.8% of precancerous or malignant lesions. Sensitivity for precancerous or malignant lesions increased from 53.9% to 75.0% after AI-assisted review (difference, 21.1 percentage points; 95% CI, 4.3 to 42.0). Conclusions: In this single-center study, clinicians' pre-laryngoscopic categorization accuracy was higher after reviewing the AI assessment card, although the sequential design cannot fully separate AI assistance from reassessment; gains depended on model correctness without clearly selective adoption. Randomized and external evaluations, including of the malignancy alert, are needed.
