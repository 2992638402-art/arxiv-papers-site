The Silent Cost of Conversation History in LLM Reasoning

arXiv ID: 2611.20071
分类: cs.CL, cs.AI
发布时间: 2026-11-15

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Ali Şenol
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Large language models (LLMs) in multi-turn information systems accumulate their own earlier replies and a growing task state. We asked whether this conversation history lowers accuracy, and whether process-level quality metrics warn before it does. Each analysis was fixed before its outcomes were computed. In Study 1, on 48 sharded math tasks, retaining its replies lowered final success by 0.21 for GPT-4o-mini and 0.29 for Mistral-7B (Holm-adjusted p = 0.026 and 0.004); Llama-4 Scout showed the same direction, not significant. Study 2 introduces CDR-Stream, 100-turn state-tracking dialogues with an exact answer per turn and per-model difficulty calibration. With the full history, accuracy fell from about 0.6 to 0.1 within twenty turns at matched difficulty, whereas a solver-verified state summary kept it 0.52-0.59 higher (Holm-adjusted p less than 0.001). Consistency and logical coherence fell far less than accuracy, and stability rose. None of these metrics, a paired readiness measure or calibrated single-stream drift detectors gave robust, model-general early warning, although in Study 2 the repeat-based metrics were complete only for GPT-4o-mini. In the monitors we tested, a single 100-turn stream had power only for very large drops. Systems built on LLMs should keep task state outside the transcript and monitor across sessions.
