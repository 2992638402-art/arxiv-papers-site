When Valid Decisions Are Not Enough: Evaluating Structural Constraints in Knowledge-Guided Generative Systems

arXiv ID: 2611.20111
分类: cs.AI, cs.CY
发布时间: 2026-11-15

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Josef Pavlíček, Petra Pavlíčková, Martin Molhanec
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Knowledge-guided generative systems use explicit domain constraints to improve decision consistency and alignment with human-created solutions. However, locally valid decisions do not necessarily preserve contextual relationships in human decision-making. This paper examines when structural constraints improve or degrade agreement with human reference solutions. We introduce HARMONIX, an experimental framework using automated musical harmonization as a controlled testbed for knowledge-guided decision-making. Four configurations share the same Viterbi-based optimization mechanism: a baseline, repetition constraints, functional constraints, and their combination. Experiments cover 19,745 records and 143,634 motif pairs across training and validation partitions. Combined constraints improve reference root-interval agreement by approximately five percentage points in stable structural contexts but reduce it by nearly nine percentage points in contexts involving transposition tracking. These opposing effects are consistent across both partitions, while overall exact chord agreement changes only marginally. Contexts are identified retrospectively using reference annotations; no adaptive constraint-selection mechanism is evaluated. The findings demonstrate that interpretable constraints can introduce systematic context-specific failures concealed by aggregate accuracy metrics. They highlight the need to evaluate relationships between decisions and the contextual applicability of constraints in knowledge-guided generative systems.
