Do We Still Need Fine-Tuned Classifiers? Swapping Typed Decision Models into 24 ACL-Venue Papers

arXiv ID: 2611.20191
分类: cs.CL
发布时间: 2026-11-15

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Marek Šuppa, Jaroslav Kopčan
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Typed Decision Models such as TypeSafe's Jev, also called System One or System 1 models, answer typed questions about an input with a probability for each option, and they need no training. Many NLP papers still fine-tune a task-specific classifier, and prompted large language models (LLMs) trail such classifiers on most text classification benchmarks. We ask how many of these fine-tuned classifiers a Typed Decision Model can replace under each paper's own evaluation, and what is needed where it cannot. We swap the predictors of 24 ACL-venue papers for Jev, keep each paper's splits, label definitions and metric, and check the results with Cloudflare's Clef, Microsoft-Decision-1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna Decisions. The resulting 46 English test sets, with their questions and published scores, form the TDM-Gauntlet benchmark. With a zero-shot prompt, Jev directly replaced the paper's predictor on 12 of the 46 test sets, mostly single judgments with plain labels, but rarely where the paper's method was trained on its labels. Where Jev scored lower, we most often attributed the shortfall to an annotation convention that the label name does not state. With 500 training labels plus validation and no user-managed GPU, calibration and GEPA-style prompt optimization closed a median 72% of the gap to the paper's method, about twice what off-the-shelf GEPA closed, for a median $1.66 per search. On the same labels, fine-tuned encoders such as RoBERTa and DeBERTa closed 59%, and LoRA fine-tuning of the open LLM Qwen3.8-27B closed 97%. Fine-tuning remains the most accurate option, but a zero-shot prompt is often enough for plain labels, and prompt optimization closes most of the remaining gap.
