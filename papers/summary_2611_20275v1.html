The Manifold-Index Model: provably and efficiently avoiding the statistical and computational curse of dimensionality

arXiv ID: 2611.20275
分类: stat.ML, cs.LG, math.ST
发布时间: 2026-11-15

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Yantao Wu, Mauro Maggioni
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

We study vector-valued regression with predictors filling a tube around an unknown $m$-dimensional manifold $\mathcal{M}$ in $\mathbb{R}^D$. In the Manifold-Index Model, the mean response is $F=f\circΠ_{\mathcal{M}}$, where $Π_{\mathcal{M}}$ is the nearest-point projection onto $\mathcal{M}$ and $f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^p$ is a possibly non-injective link function. Multiscale ANisotropic lOcal Regression (MANOR) estimates tangent planes by multiscale PCA of predictors with similar responses, then refines them by pooling outer products of local slopes. The neighborhoods narrow tangentially while retaining complete normal fibers. We assume a Lipschitz link differential and bounded response noise. For $p\ge m$, under our geometric and sampling conditions, $n$ independent observations give mean tangent-space error of order $n^{-2/(m+4)}$ and integrated squared prediction error at the $m$-dimensional nonparametric rate $n^{-4/(m+4)}$, up to logarithmic factors. In a scaling regime with fixed intrinsic dimension, model and scale parameters, and ratio of manifold volume to the $m$-th power of its reach, the required sample size grows polynomially in $D+p$, and computation is polynomial in $n$, $D$ and $p$. Numerical experiments on curves with non-monotone links and surfaces with vector-valued responses compare observed convergence rates with the theoretical bounds; a Langevin dynamics example tests prediction of hitting probabilities under an approximate manifold-index model.
