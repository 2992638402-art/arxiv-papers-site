Instructions Before Actions: Prospective and Retrospective Language Supervision for Driving Vision-Language-Action Data

arXiv ID: 2611.20631
分类: cs.CV, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.RO
发布时间: 2026-11-16

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Angel Martinez-Sanchez, Ross Greer
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Driving vision-language-action (VLA) models can accept navigation instructions, yet predicting a driver's trajectory does not establish that a model follows the instruction. We study how driving-language supervision is collected and evaluated, distinguishing prospective instructions issued before an action from retrospective labels written after observing it. We develop ADVLAT-Engine to synchronize spoken instructions, road-scene video, and vehicle trajectories, and compare three navigation apps on 48 replayed routes. On 87 maneuvers announced by both Google Maps and a passenger, the passenger uses more scene-dependent language. We evaluate four open driving VLAs before and after fine-tuning on prospective navigation-app drives. Fine-tuning reduces passenger-drive trajectory error by 23-59%, but instruction effects remain inconsistent. At real intersections, counterfactual instructions reveal stronger responsiveness: fine-tuned Alpamayo2 Super gains 31 and 18 accuracy points at 15 and 5m, while Qwen-Drive follows alternative maneuvers more reliably with a discrete command than with a sentence. We additionally collected retrospective annotations from four people for the driving recordings and describe controlled comparisons of annotation content, timing, and downstream learning; those analyses are pending. Our findings separate maneuver prediction from instruction-following and establish a framework for comparing prospective and retrospective supervision.
