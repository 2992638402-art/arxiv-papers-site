Premise Recovery in Software Design: What Does This Decision Rest On?

arXiv ID: 2611.21367
分类: cs.SE, cs.AI, cs.MA
发布时间: 2026-11-16

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Priyabanta Sandulua, Islam Mesabaha, Sebastian J. Vollmer
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Design decisions shape software architecture, and writing them down preserves the reasons behind it. Architecture decision records (ADRs) capture them, each with its context, choice, and consequences. A decision can depend on earlier ones: reversing one of them would force its review. We call these its premises. Yet ADRs do not always name their premises. When a decision is reversed, engineers supersede its ADR and search by hand for what it affects. That search can miss ADRs resting on an unnamed premise. We frame this as premise recovery: given an ADR with identifiers masked, find its premises among earlier records. We collect 1,060 ADRs from 36 repositories and label 1,529 candidate pairs for 60 target ADRs. Two LLM annotators agree at \k{appa} = 0.859. An independent human annotator, blind to their labels, agrees with the stated/implicit tag on 30 of 32 premises (\k{appa} = 0.875). We compare 7 open-weight models with 2 baselines that use no LLM. Qwen3.8-27B reaches F1 0.756, against 0.466 for word overlap; 2 models added post hoc score similarly. On the 11 premises whose titles barely overlap the target, it finds 0.758, while word overlap finds none. Reasoning raises its recall of implicit premises but not its F1. Word overlap with as many predictions gains 69% as much. We find only 2 to 3 stale dependents, though we searched for unlinked ones in only 30 of 66 supersessions. Open models could thus suggest unwritten premises when a decision is reversed, with precision between 0.68 and 0.78.
