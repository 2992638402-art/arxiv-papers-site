The cost of enforcing long-run coverage: a phase transition in online conformal prediction

arXiv ID: 2611.21542
分类: math.ST, cs.LG, stat.ML
发布时间: 2026-11-16

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Yu Gui, Rina Foygel Barber, Cong Ma
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Consider an online prediction setting, where at each time, we are given a new feature vector and asked to provide a prediction interval or prediction set that will likely contain the corresponding response. In this setting, if the data points are i.i.d. (or more generally, exchangeable), conformal prediction provides a mechanism for building valid prediction sets around the output of any trained model, offering coverage probability $\geq 1-α$ at each time $t$. In practice, however, issues such as distribution drift, changepoints, or other challenges may cause violations of the i.i.d. assumption. In this adversarial setting, online conformal prediction methods can still offer guarantees on long-run empirical coverage. However, there is a trade-off between these two types of goals: enforcing tight constraints on long-run empirical coverage to protect against the adversarial setting can lead to unstable oscillations in the instantaneous training-conditional coverage in the i.i.d. setting. In this work, we seek best-of-both-worlds algorithms that aim to minimize cumulative error in instantaneous training-conditional coverage when in the i.i.d. setting, while also offering long-run empirical coverage guarantees in the adversarial setting. Specifically, consider a condition that requires long-run empirical coverage to deviate from the target level $1-α$ by at most $\mathcal{O}(T^{-β})$, for some $β\in[0,1]$. We prove that this family of conditions can be described with a phase transition. For $β\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]$, it is possible to construct algorithms satisfying such a constraint in the adversarial setting, while offering the same instantaneous coverage performance as split conformal prediction if the data is in fact i.i.d. On the other hand, for $β\in(\frac{1}{2},1]$, this type of condition comes at a cost, and does not achieve best-of-both-worlds behavior.
