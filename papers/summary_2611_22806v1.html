Virtual Double Oracle: Faster Convergence by Leveraging Strategies That Were Watching All Along

arXiv ID: 2611.22806
分类: cs.LG, cs.GT, cs.MA
发布时间: 2026-11-17

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Ruilan Wang, Francisco Aristi Reina, Katerina Papadaki
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Double Oracle (DO) and PSRO approximate Nash equilibria in two-player zero-sum games by iteratively expanding a strategy population with best responses supplied by an oracle. Online Double Oracle (ODO) interleaves multiplicative weights updates (MWU) with population discovery and obtains an $O(\sqrt{k\ln k/T})$ rate, where $T$ is the number of learning rounds and $k$ is the larger of the two players' final population sizes. We identify the reinitialisation step as an underexplored design choice: at each expansion, MWU-based methods reset newly discovered strategies to a uniform prior. We introduce Virtual Double Oracle (VDO), which exploits a bilinear identity (a strategy's full hindsight loss history collapses to its utility against the time-averaged opponent) to initialise each new strategy with the MWU weight it would have had from the start. We prove that VDO achieves $O(\sqrt{k/T})$ exploitability over the discovered population as it grows, provided the cumulative cost of adding strategies is at most linear in $k$, a condition supported by our empirical measurements. Discounted VDO (dVDO) moderates historical preferences to reduce over-concentration at reinitialisation. Experiments across matrix games, poker, and Goofspiel show that, with a suitable discount, dVDO can substantially reduce finite-horizon exploitability relative to both VDO and uniform-reset ODO, and can improve the discovered strategy populations.
