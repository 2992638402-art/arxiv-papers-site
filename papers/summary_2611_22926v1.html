When Do Typed Decision Models Work in Transportation? Evidence from Travel Choice and Traffic Signal Control

arXiv ID: 2611.22926
分类: cs.CL, cs.AI
发布时间: 2026-11-17

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Hongyang Zhang, Boyuan Deng, Siyuan Feng, Kai Chen, Junbo Zhang
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Typed decision models combine natural-language understanding with a structured interface that returns a selected action and an option-level probability distribution without generating lengthy textual responses. Their low inference cost and response latency make them attractive for transportation problems with predefined alternatives. This paper investigates their effectiveness, robustness, and efficiency in two representative transportation tasks: individual travel-mode choice prediction and sequential traffic signal control. We systematically evaluate TypeSafe Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions against conventional transportation methods, reinforcement learning algorithms, and four chat-based large language models. Experiments on the Swissmetro dataset show that typed decision models achieve lower prediction accuracy than traditional discrete choice models and the strongest chat-based baseline, while their performance varies substantially with input representation and question framing. In closed-loop traffic signal control experiments, typed decision models demonstrate competitive performance against established controllers in several scenarios, but exhibit performance degradation under more challenging network conditions. Furthermore, the typed models achieve lower inference latency and computational cost than the evaluated chat-based large language models, highlighting their potential for efficient, zero-shot transportation decision-making. Overall, these findings demonstrate both the potential and limitations of typed decision models, emphasizing the importance of input design, task characteristics, and robustness evaluation for their effective application in transportation systems.
