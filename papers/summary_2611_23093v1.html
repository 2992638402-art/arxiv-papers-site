What's in a Tree ? Hierarchical $n$-gram models as controlled approximators of language

arXiv ID: 2611.23093
分类: cs.CL, cond-mat.dis-nn, cs.AI, cs.LG
发布时间: 2026-11-17

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Aritra Das, Francesco Cagnetta, Darshil Doshi, Victor V. Albert, Maissam Barkeshli, Andrey Gromov
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Hierarchical models have been proposed as synthetic settings for studying language. However, their relevance to realistic language corpora remains underexplored. In this work, we introduce tree-grams, autoregressive generative $n$-gram models that build the conditional probability tensor $p(x_n \mid x_{1:n-1})$ through a hierarchical composition of order-2 and order-3 tensors. $n$ controls the maximal context window, whereas a rank hyperparameter $d$ controls model capacity, yielding an interpretable and systematically scalable family of approximators of language. We show how tree-grams outperform existing $n$-gram baselines, while remaining competitive with deep transformer-based language models. We demonstrate the utility of such controlled approximations by using tree-grams as generative models. In particular, we show that hyperparameter transfer under $μ$P can be made more robust when narrower models are tuned on simpler data, generated by tree-grams with moderate $n$ and $d$. In addition, we show that entropy and effective range of correlations control performance gap after fine-tuning. This is because tree-grams allow us to explicitly control horizon and capacity. When allowed to route across candidate topologies, we observe emergent sparsity in tree-grams. Such concentrated routing permits pruning upto 95% of topologies without significant change in performance.
