Event-Based Dynamic Lip Region Estimation During Speech

arXiv ID: 2611.23111
分类: cs.CV, cs.LG
发布时间: 2026-11-17

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Hamze Hammami, Omar Eldardeer, Francesco Rea
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Event cameras offer high temporal resolution and low power consumption, making them promising for speech-related applications. These include automatic lip reading, audiovisual speech analysis, and human-robot interaction, particularly in challenging environments characterized by low illumination or high dynamic range. Event-based lip reading, speaker recognition, and voice activity detection, however, mostly operate on a cropped region of interest (ROI), by hand or by the dataset, and no event-based method supplies that ROI as a spatial region; existing event-based face analysis gives the mouth as landmarks, which presents a mismatch between application and localization. The present work predicts a dense heatmap over the lip region from the full event "frame". Events are encoded as a polarity-separated voxel grid and processed at every location by a two-pathway continuous-time recurrent network (CT-RNN) with learned, log-space bounded time constants acting on the real elapsed time of each bin: a stateless fast pathway and a stateful slow pathway, utilised for refining and anchoring the prediction. The model reaches 69.5% on lips on four unseen speakers and switches target only 2.1 times per second, against 8.9 and 11.0 for convolutional baselines with more parameters. Adding a spatial encoder improves precision but fails on a new face scale. On a real event sensor, trained only on simulated events, the proposed model reaches 58.0% on lips against 43.2% for the CNN, with fewer than half its target switches.
