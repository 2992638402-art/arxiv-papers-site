Retrieval-Augmented Correction for Generalist Medical Image Segmentation

arXiv ID: 2611.23189
分类: cs.CV, cs.AI
发布时间: 2026-11-17

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Vincenzo Marcianò, Xiaoming Zhang, Michela Antonelli, Sebastien Ourselin, Maria A Zuluaga
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Automated segmentation is routinely used as the first step in quantitative image-based clinical workflows, where predicted masks are subsequently reviewed and corrected by human operators. Automated correction of the residual structural errors is currently addressed one anatomy at a time, so the number of trained correctors scales with the number of segmentation targets. We present GRACE, a retrieval-augmented refiner that edits an input mask under context drawn from a shared knowledge base of verified prior cases. The refiner is trained once on a multi-organ corpus spanning compact organs, cavities, branching vessels, and tumour lesions. In the held-out dataset-transfer evaluation, three held-out datasets query a fixed knowledge base built from training data only. A separate leave-one-anatomy-out experiment tests parameter-free index extension: verified cases of an unseen solid organ enter the knowledge base, and the refiner stays frozen. A light shape-aware projection head on a frozen multimodal encoder maps embedding similarity to the shape agreement of the masks, so the knowledge base returns cases that the refiner can use as a shape prior. We evaluate GRACE on 18 public datasets spanning CT, MRI and ultrasound, using nine refinement configurations and seven upstream segmentors that span weak, mid, and strong operating regimes. We report each change with the DSC of its input mask. On the weak tier, the input DSC is 0.386 and GRACE adds up to +0.35 DSC. On a strong segmentor, the input DSC is 0.932 and the mean change is small. Transfer to an imaging modality that is absent from training and from the index is partial and depends on the anatomy, and remains an open research direction. Our results suggest retrieval-conditioned refinement is a practical alternative to anatomy-specific correction models. Code: https://github.com/robustml-eurecom/grace
