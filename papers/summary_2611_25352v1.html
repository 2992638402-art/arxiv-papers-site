Compiling Constraints into Neuromorphic Ising Machines

arXiv ID: 2611.25352
分类: cs.ET, cond-mat.dis-nn, cs.AR, cs.NE, quant-ph
发布时间: 2026-11-19

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Faiek Ahsan, Shantanu Chakrabartty
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Most practical combinatorial optimization problems involve constraints, and an Ising machine that flips one spin at a time can respect them only through energy penalties that raise barriers between feasible solutions. Here we show that a higher-order neuromorphic Ising machine can enforce constraints through programmable transitions rather than penalties. In an autoencoder-like architecture, each inner-layer neuron represents a move, a family of multi-spin transitions that maps every feasible state to another feasible state, receives the energy change of its move through connections that outer-layer neurons multiplicatively switch on or off, and updates the state through the same connections. A compiler turns constraints into this wiring with a number of auxiliary clauses that grows only polynomially for parity, cardinality, one-hot, partition and permutation constraints. Restricting the search to feasible states reduces constraint handling to move design, and we show that the fastest-annealing moves are not those that mix fastest but those that lower the deepest energy barriers, and that moves chosen from the objective lower them further than random ones. On third-order graph matching, the machine needs orders of magnitude fewer iterations than penalty-based neuromorphic machines. Configured as a sampler on a field-programmable gate array, it decodes the surface code with maximum-likelihood accuracy up to code distance 15 within 52 $μ$s per shot and, under noisy syndrome measurements, decodes in real time for millisecond measurement rounds up to distance 9 with fewer logical errors than belief-propagation decoders.
