Evolving Decoding Algorithms for Quantum Error Correction

arXiv ID: 2611.25880
分类: quant-ph, cs.AI, cs.MA, cs.NE
发布时间: 2026-11-19

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Eduardo Dueñez, Edgar Said Hernandez Sanchez, Edgar A. Duéñez-Guzmán, Suzanne Sadedin
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Quantum computation entails robustness engineering: a noisy quantum state is measured continuously, and a classical algorithm (the decoder) must infer corrections that reliably remove the noise, fast enough to keep up. We write several published belief-propagation (BP) decoders for quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes in a Domain-Specific Language (DSL), and search over it with grammatical evolution. Written in the language, the published decoders show their parameters in plain view, allowing us to re-parameterise BP+OSD (ordered statistics decoding) and BP+LSD (localized statistics decoding) decoders with damping, before any search, making each 17.1--41.0$\times$ faster at a higher error rate, and BP+OSD-0 about 103 faster than the ldpc package's version tuned on the [[72,12,6]] bivariate-bicycle code. Evolution over the language finds decoders that improve the time-error front. One makes 23% fewer logical errors than BP+OSD-0 with damping at 0.64$\times$ its time, and decoders evolved from random code are more accurate than ldpc's tuned BP+OSD-0 at 1/31 of its time or less. Frozen at one operating point, the two fastest evolved decoders generalize well across noise levels, code sizes and round counts, staying at the time-error front. Ultimately, we demonstrate that capturing expert knowledge within a DSL creates a powerful intermediate layer---one that effectively bridges human intuition and machine-driven optimization to unlock highly efficient quantum error correction.
