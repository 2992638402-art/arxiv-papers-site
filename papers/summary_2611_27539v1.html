Joint Optimization of Fleet Size, Localization Infrastructure, and Trajectories for Multi-AUV Persistent Monitoring

  • PDF: 下载

📝 摘要

Persistent monitoring of large-scale subsea infrastructure, such as offshore wind farm foundations, export cables, and inspection corridors, remains a fundamental challenge for autonomous underwater systems. The problem is not solely spatial coverage, but rather temporal coverage: ensuring that all relevant regions of interest are revisited within bounded time intervals under limited sensing, navigation uncertainty, and constrained vehicle resources. We compare three classes of trajectory strategies: (1) deterministic lane-based coverage (lawnmower patterns), (2) stochastic exploration (biased random walk with boundary constraints), and (3) coordinated partition-based strategies (Voronoi tessellation). Rather than seeking a universally superior method, we evaluate how varying levels of localization accuracy impact temporal coverage across two distinct operational profiles: persistent monitoring of the entire operational workspace versus targeted inspection of critical infrastructure. We present a simulation-based, quantitatively grounded study of temporal coverage in multi-AUV persistent monitoring, disentangling three interacting design dimensions: (i) trajectory generation strategy, (ii) fleet size, and (iii) localization accuracy provided by fixed acoustic beacon infrastructure. We introduce a cost-aware evaluation framework combining fleet size, number of beacons, and mission energy expenditure into a unified cost function. Results demonstrate that beyond a certain accuracy threshold, improvements in localization yield diminishing returns compared to coordinated trajectory design, an effect often obscured in integrated navigation-planning approaches. Coordinated-partition trajectories enable a reduced fleet size to achieve superior temporal coverage at a 39-46% lower overall life-cycle cost than rigid deterministic paths unoptimized for the localization infrastructure.
