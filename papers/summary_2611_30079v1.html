Designing Incoherent Multispectral Diffractive Optical Networks for Satellite Image Classification

arXiv ID: 2611.30079
分类: physics.optics, cs.NE, physics.app-ph
发布时间: 2026-11-23

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Xilin Yang, Md Sadman Sakib Rahman, Ryan Lee, Aydogan Ozcan
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Optical neural networks and diffractive optical computing provide a promising route for performing task-specific inference with high parallelism and lightweight electronic post-processing. These advantages are especially attractive for multispectral satellite imaging, where conventional workflows rely on transmitting high-dimensional image cubes to the ground for downstream electronic analysis. Here, we introduce a computationally efficient surrogate model-guided framework for designing incoherent multispectral diffractive optical networks that directly output class probabilities onboard, eliminating the need to capture and transfer the full multispectral data cube for ground-side inference. We first train a physically compatible surrogate model, represented by a non-negative fully connected model that accommodates a generalized detector readout, yielding a task-specific non-negative multispectral intensity transformation that can be transferred to an optical processor. We then synthesize its diffractive counterpart through data-free point-spread-function-based optimization under spatially incoherent illumination. Using this approach, we numerically demonstrate a six-spectral-channel diffractive classifier for tri-class satellite-scene recognition, achieving a blind-testing accuracy of 79.8% for artificial surface, water body, and vegetated land images, closely approaching the 81.8% accuracy of its surrogate. We further demonstrate a more compact three-spectral-channel design that numerically achieves 72.7% blind-testing accuracy, compared with 75.1% for its corresponding surrogate model. This surrogate model-guided strategy provides a practical pathway for designing compact, task-specific incoherent multispectral optical processors for resource-constrained remote-sensing inference.
