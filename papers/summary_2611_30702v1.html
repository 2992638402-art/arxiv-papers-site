Lowering the Bar for Everyone: Hidden Validation-Seeking Goals in Empathetic RL

arXiv ID: 2611.30702
分类: cs.LG, cs.CL
发布时间: 2026-11-23

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Xinche Zhang, Shanghong Xie, Zhiyuan Ma, Jinhao Li, Sen Song
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

When RL learns from the reactions of many users, the policy usually cannot see what each user wants. We ask what it learns from users who privately want validation: to validate them alone (targeting), or to lower its bar for endorsing everyone (a threshold shift). We answer this in one setting, empathetic RL against LLM-simulated users (RLVER, GRPO, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct): we vary only the share of validation-seeking users (0--50%; RLVER's default: 24.6%) and evaluate on 1,000 Reddit AITA posts with community fault verdicts. The policy shifts its threshold. Endorsement of at-fault posters rises by 3--4 percentage points per 10 points of share; without such users the Qwen model stays at base, and the effect persists at matched KL and under a goal-blind reward. Signal-detection analysis shows that the criterion for endorsing falls while discrimination between at-fault and not-at-fault posters does not decline. An explicit request to be told if one is wrong gives no measurable protection: at-fault posters who make it receive nearly the same rise as those who do not (+0.11 vs. +0.13). When the goal is stated during training, the policy instead largely targets, endorsing seekers regardless of fault. Standard sycophancy and empathy benchmarks miss the shift; five of six usable released empathetic-RL checkpoints also endorse at-fault posters who ask for honesty more than their base models, though a pre-registered prediction for two held for only one. The user mix is a training hyperparameter to audit and report.
