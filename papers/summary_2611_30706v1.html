Bayesian sequential decision-making for adaptive early warning of bridge failure risk

arXiv ID: 2611.30706
分类: stat.ME, cs.LG
发布时间: 2026-11-23

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Teng Li, James L. Beck, Yong Huang, Hui Li, Hongju Han
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

As a critical functionality of structural health monitoring (SHM) systems, early warning of increased bridge failure risk is essential for structural safety. However, traditional methods treat early warning merely as passive detection against a fixed safety limit and neglect evolving uncertainties and the economic consequences of decisions, leading to delayed alarms that increase the failure risk and hinder reliable and rational decision-making. This study formulates the early warning of increased structural failure risk as a sequential decision-making problem and develops a Bayesian framework for its solution. The presented framework unifies a probabilistic state transition model, an observation prediction model, cost models for early warning decisions, and a threshold-based decision strategy into a closed loop encompassing update, prediction, optimization, and decision execution. The structural state is updated iteratively via Bayes' theorem upon the arrival of new monitoring data, ensuring each decision is conditioned on the latest information. Through pre-posterior analysis over predicted future observations, the warning threshold is optimized by minimizing the expected total cost, which explicitly balances structural failure risks against warning response costs. In this way, the warning paradigm is shifted from passive detection to proactive decision-making, with the warning threshold adaptively updated as uncertainties and costs evolve. The framework was verified via two bridge case studies covering typical scenarios that lead to increased structural failure risk, including steel truss member fracture and stay cable rupture. Results demonstrate that warning decisions can be triggered in a timely and effective manner before severe consequences occur.
