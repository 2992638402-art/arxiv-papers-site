WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency

arXiv ID: 2611.30989
分类: cs.RO
发布时间: 2026-11-23

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Xinyang Zhou, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce \textbf{WorldLeague}, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of \textbf{60 simulated robot tasks} by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in \textbf{WorldSeek}, rearrange objects in \textbf{WorldShape}, and adapt to changes in \textbf{WorldShift}. Tying these goals to a single control interface limits which AI systems can be compared. In WorldLeague, vision-language models (VLMs) choose targets and motions, while vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs) use their native controls. For each of six capability dimensions, we measure how much progress a system makes toward its goals (Interactive Score) and how often it achieves them (physical success rate). Together, these measures form the system's \emph{Physical Agency Profile}. Among 13 representative AI systems, Claude Fable 5.2 achieves 13 of the 60 goals and earns the highest mean Interactive Score (28.58/100). Fable leads in four dimensions, while GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tie for first on rule-following tasks. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion.
