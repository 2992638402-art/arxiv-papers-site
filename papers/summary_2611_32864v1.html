Fresh Keys, Stale Secrets: Numeric Leakage across Linked LLM Prompts

arXiv ID: 2611.32864
分类: cs.CR, cs.CL
发布时间: 2026-11-24

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Jian Li, Qinglin Zhao, Shuoyao Wang, Jincheng Cai, Zhifeng Hao, Naiqi Wu
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Small businesses repeatedly ask cloud language models about the same confidential prices, unit costs and margins. Numeric prompt disguises for such numbers are evaluated one prompt at a time, but a provider sees a firm's history, in which each fresh secret gives a new, independent view of the same numbers. Since real costs are not public, we measure on synthetic firms with known values how much an honest-but-curious provider recovers when it knows the disguise algorithm, holds sector-level price and margin statistics, and links a firm's queries. In one prompt, per-field scaling, a fixed-axis instantiation of Zhang et al.'s order-preserving substitution and Pr$\varepsilon\varepsilon$mpt's released sanitizer keep the attacker's margin-hit advantage over a prior-only guess between -0.6 and +4.4 percentage points. After 16 linked queries with fresh secrets, every disguise we test (Pr$\varepsilon\varepsilon$mpt at $\varepsilon\geq 0.5$) has a target recovered significantly more often: under the order-preserving substitution, margin recovery rises from 25.0% to 59.4%. The same attacker recovers 29.4 percentage points more margins with fresh maps than with one reused map. Theory explains the contrast: one reused secret bounds all queries by a single full-precision view of the fields shown, whereas fresh scaling keys are identifiable. Except for Pr$\varepsilon\varepsilon$mpt at $\varepsilon=0.5$, the gains persist when the attacker's prior comes from another population model and when values drift by up to 5% between queries; on our firms, whose prose cues are weak, reading the prose with a language model adds at most 2.5 points. Code and data: https://github.com/Jamli58/fresh-keys-stale-secrets
