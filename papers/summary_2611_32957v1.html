"I Do Intend to Deliver": Will LLMs Run a Marketplace Advance-Fee Scam?

arXiv ID: 2611.32957
分类: cs.SI, cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CR
发布时间: 2026-11-24

📄 论文信息

  • 作者: Megan Squire
  • PDF: 下载
  • arXiv 链接: 查看

📝 摘要

Online fraud is traditionally limited by the cost of the human labor needed to talk to potential victims. We ask whether hosted large language models will now supply that labor. We prompted ten off-the-shelf models to play the seller in the "free item, just pay delivery" advance-fee scam common on buy-and-sell sites, using a plain system prompt with no jailbreak. Each model played 30 conversations against each of four buyer personas, from credulous to guarded, with the same model playing the buyer (1,200 conversations). We label each conversation by what happened to the buyer's money, and validate the labels against two human coders. 737 conversations (61%) ended with the buyer paying in advance. Eight of the ten models played in almost every conversation, and running both sides cost about 2.5 cents per payment. Only the guarded buyer, who wanted a findable identity, resisted, and the most willing sellers got past it by inventing names and phone numbers. Sellers that recognized the scam still took the money in 133 conversations, usually reasoning about their own honesty rather than the buyer's risk. Several models protected the buyer without refusing, by conceding payment on delivery, so refusal rates alone misstate safety. We release the benchmark, prompts, code and labels.
